To address both, we propose HyperSkill , a Training-Free Omnimodal GRPO framework that keeps the backbone frozen and replaces gradient updates with auditable edits to a Hypergraph-Indexed Skill-Library , where skills are advantage-bearing nodes and compositions are hyperedges. It has three components: training-time distillation admits counterfactually checked skills and learns a protocol skill from failures; gated hypergraph retrieval injects advantage-ranked skills where a per-dataset gate admits them; and an execution protocol makes the frozen backbone commit to an answer. On 11 text, vision-language, and omnimodal benchmarks, HyperSkill is best or tied on every dataset and beats the best baseline by 13.0 average F1 points while keeping the backbone frozen. Reinforcement learning is now the main way to improve the reasoning of large language models (LLMs), with group-relative policy optimisation (GRPO) [ 39 , 52 , 55 ] used to train systems in math, search, and tool use [ 14 , 49 , 18 , 10 ] . However, it is still hard to keep improving an agent as it moves to omnimodal tasks such as web search, long-document reasoning, and audio-video question answering [ 45 , 48 , 58 , 23 ] , without a per-domain cost of updating its backbone. Existing methods follow two main lines. The mainstream parameter-space recipe applies gradient updates to the backbone, yielding capable but costly systems [ 14 , 49 , 18 , 10 ] . A growing training-free line keeps the backbone frozen and grows an external textual experience store across rollouts, retrieved in-context at inference [ 2 , 1 , 3 , 50 , 41 , 44 ] . Both lines have limits. On the parameter side (Figure 1 ), gradient updates remain expensive at frontier scale [ 14 ] , every new task risks catastrophic forgetting [ 54 ] , and improvement is buried in opaque weight deltas with no auditable trace of which patterns the agent has internalised. On the training-free side, three failure modes recur (Figure 2 ): (i) comparison is at the trajectory level [ 2 , 1 ] , so every divergent step is fused into one lesson; (ii) candidates are admitted by co-occurrence with successful rollouts [ 2 , 3 , 50 ] , so the library keeps patterns that track reward but do not fix the failure; (iii) skills are stored as dictionaries or uniform-quality lists, which cannot express that several skills must act jointly or give a per-skill quality signal. Hypergraph retrieval [ 26 , 11 , 53 ] links several entities in one hyperedge, but it targets static factual knowledge bases rather than self-evolving skill libraries. Figure 2: Three failure modes (i)–(iii) of training-free libraries (trajectory-level, correlation-based, flat skills) vs. HyperSkill’s step distillation, counterfactual verification and hypergraph evolution. To close all three gaps, we propose HyperSkill, a Training-Free Omnimodal GRPO framework whose policy is an external Hypergraph-Indexed Skill-Library : atomic skills are advantage-bearing nodes, joint compositions are hyperedges, and the library is the optimisation target. C1 (training-time distillation) handles (i) and (ii): it admits a step-level candidate only after a counterfactual test (“would the failed trajectory have succeeded had the agent known this skill?”) and learns a protocol skill from failures. C2 (gated hypergraph retrieval) handles (iii): an advantage-weighted retriever [ 20 , 13 , 15 ] ranks hyperedge-linked skills [ 26 , 11 , 25 ] behind a gate with zero-skill fallback. C3 (execution protocol) makes the frozen backbone call its tools correctly and then commit to a final answer. We validate this design across 11 text, vision-language, and omnimodal benchmarks [ 45 , 4 , 27 , 16 ] : HyperSkill is best or tied on all 11 without updating a parameter, and lifts the average F1 from 29.0 29.0 for the strongest baseline, XSkill [ 17 ] , to 42.0 42.0 ( + 13.0 +13.0 , 44.9 % 44.9\% relative). Each component matters: removing C1, C2 or C3 alone lowers the average F1 by 2.5 2.5 , 4.2 4.2 and 10.4 10.4 points, respectively. 2 Related Work Omnimodal LLMs and reinforcement learning. Modern backbones fuse text, image, video, and audio into a single policy [ 35 , 24 , 36 , 31 , 48 , 58 , 23 ] , and group-relative policy optimisation [ 39 , 52 , 55 ] trains reasoners [ 14 , 32 ] and search and tool agents [ 18 , 10 , 25 ] , while reflection-style agents [ 51 , 40 , 28 ] act through prompting on web and OS benchmarks [ 57 , 45 , 47 ] . The RL-trained ones still pay for backbone gradient updates and inherit the forgetting and opacity problems motivating this work. Training-Free Omnimodal GRPO keeps the backbone frozen and redirects a normalised reward advantage from the gradient onto an external Hypergraph-Indexed Skill-Library that acts as the policy, so each improvement is a readable library edit rather than an opaque weight delta. Training-free experience self-evolution. TrainingFreeGRPO [ 2 ] replaces the gradient with an LLM-curated experience pool, skill-centric agents [ 1 , 3 , 50 , 41 , 44 ] grow flat dictionaries or frontier sets, and memory [ 33 , 6 , 34 ] with graph and hypergraph RAG [ 9 , 15 , 26 , 11 ] supplies the retrievable side. Most of them compare whole trajectories and keep flat skills with no per-item quality for ranking; HyperSkill instead distils individual steps and ranks skills by advantage in a hypergraph. A frozen backbone often breaks the tool or answer format, not the task. C1 therefore sorts every training rollout that ends without an answer into four failure types: malformed tool call, call beyond the budget, call without tools, and no <answer> tag. Its last line fixes the answer form: the integer without \boxed{} on math, else a short answer or, per dataset, a form derived from the training gold answers (option letter, phrase or sentence, plus an abstention line). ∎ 3.3 C3: Execution Protocol Tool Calls and Final Answer. C3 decides how the frozen backbone executes a rollout; it never edits ℋ \mathcal{H} , and its options are set per dataset (App. E.7 ). A concrete tool prompt names the real tool tag (e.g. <text_search> ) instead of a TOOL_TAG placeholder, and a lenient parser also executes copied placeholder forms such as <TOOL_TAG>text_search>q . If a rollout ends without a closed <answer> tag (e.g. once the tool budget is spent), a final-answer turn asks once more. An answer suffix requests the final integer without \boxed{} on math and the option letter on multiple choice. Evidence and Voting. Question pre-search sends the question verbatim to the text_search tool once, outside the tool budget; locate pages adds the text of the pages the question names and of the top BM25 [ 38 ] pages of the question’s own PDF, with no API call; on Omni, time grounding lists the keyframe times and, on multiple choice, an option check asks the model to weigh every option against frames and audio. Self-consistency [ 43 ] adds 5 5 samples at temperature 0.7 0.7 and keeps their most frequent answer, or the greedy one on a tie or when no sample answers. C3 also sets per-dataset token budgets, and on five datasets its options also apply to the training rollouts that build the library, so the library is learned under the same protocol that serves its test questions (App. E.7 ). We evaluate on HyperSkill-Bench , a collection of 11 11 public datasets in four regimes (Appendix D.1 ): Text reasoning (AIME2024/2025, WebWalkerQA), VL-Web (FVQA, InfoSeek, SimpleVQA, LiveVQA), VL-Doc (DocBench, MMLongBench-Doc) and Omni (OmniVideoBench, WorldSense). The metric is token-level F 1 F_{1} [ 37 ] with answer-shape awareness and a CJK-aware tokeniser that splits Chinese into characters; every cell of Table 2 and Figure 4 is the full-set mean over the dataset’s test questions ( 30 30 for AIME2024/2025, 64 64 otherwise; 636 636 in total), from one evaluation run per cell, scored by the same rule for every column (Appendix D.2 ). Baselines and Ablations. We compare against bare Zero-shot [ 54 ] (same tools, no library or C3) and the 6 6 training-free skill methods of Table 1 : TrainingFreeGRPO [ 2 ] (experience pool), EvoSkill [ 1 ] (frontier set), SkillCraft [ 3 ] and AutoSkill [ 50 ] (code, tip cards), Memento-Skills [ 56 ] and XSkill [ 17 ] (memory). Each baseline builds its library on the same 128 128 training questions by re-implementing its published procedure (Appendix E ), likewise without C3. Three ablations reuse the trained library (Appendix E.5 ): w/o C1 replaces all distilled content by a generic protocol line, w/o C2 also drops that line, and w/o C3 , applied on its own, only resets the C3 execution options to their defaults. Implementation Details. Every method shares the frozen gemini-2.5-flash-lite [ 7 ] backbone with native vision/audio interfaces and the gemini-embedding-2-preview [ 19 ] encoder f f , both via OpenRouter. Each HyperSkill library is trained for 1 1 epoch on 128 128 training questions per dataset (deduplicated, seed 42 42 ) with batch size 4 4 , n = 2 n\!=\!2 rollouts per question, seed 1 1 and the defaults of § 3 . Three library-run settings (R1–R3) differ in rollout temperature, early stopping, hint synthesis, probe sampling and training-time floor. Each dataset’s run, floor τ \tau and C3 options are selected on held-out training questions, and each library is then retrained on all 128 128 questions (Appendix E.7 ). Tool Stack and Decoding. Every method uses the same regime tools: a text_search tool for WebWalkerQA and VL-Web, served by gemini-3.8-flash ; images passed to the backbone; a BM25 index over the question’s own PDF for VL-Doc; and uniformly sampled keyframes for Omni, 8 8 on OmniVideoBench and 32 32 plus the audio track on WorldSense. Each question gets at most 3 3 (Text), 5 5 (VL) or 8 8 (Omni) rounds and 2 2 tool calls. Decoding is greedy at temperature 0 0 with seed 1 1 . The baselines use 1024 1024 new tokens ( 2048 2048 on AIME2025, 4096 4096 on WorldSense) and retrieve without a similarity floor; HyperSkill’s token budgets and votes belong to C3. Appendix F Future Work HyperSkill improves a frozen backbone by editing an auditable skill library instead of its weights, which leads to four future directions. First, the library can be paired with lightweight parameter updates, e.g. a low-rank adapter that complements the in-context skills. Second, because skills are plain text with an advantage, the libraries of related datasets, such as the four VL-Web datasets that share the text_search tool, can be merged and re-weighted by the EMA of Eq. (4) from new rollouts. Third, C3 refers to tools only through their tags, so extending it to code execution or GUI actions mainly requires new tool tags, budgets and evidence texts, while C1 and C2 stay unchanged. Finally, batched or importance-sampled counterfactual probes can lower the admission cost of C1. NeurIPS Paper Checklist 1.
