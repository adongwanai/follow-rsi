Agents must then infer success from observable evidence, and incomplete verification can misguide subsequent updates.
We introduce Verify by Doing (VbD), a method that co-evolves Actor and Evaluator harnesses within a single task while keeping model weights fixed.
The Evaluator actively inspects artifacts and environment state with tools; its findings ground an internal signal for output selection and guide updates to both execution and verification procedures.
Proposed Evaluators undergo comparative review on common candidates, while Actor updates require support from their own fresh outputs.
Across AutomationBench, τ 3 \tau^{3} Banking, and selected GDPval tasks, VbD achieves the highest overall scores among the compared methods operating without external feedback, with gains of 3.3, 4.1, and 2.3 percentage points over the strongest baselines on the main backbone.
Ablations support the contributions of active verification and Evaluator evolution, and selection from fixed candidate pools shows that improved verification can recover successful outputs without generating new ones. 1 Introduction Agent self-improvement depends on feedback that identifies successful attempts and guides updates to the procedures that produce them.
At test time, agents can update their workflows or harnesses while keeping model weights fixed ( Agrawal et al., 2026 ; Lee et al., 2026 ) , or use additional inference to generate and check more candidates ( Snell et al., 2025 ; Brown et al., 2024 ) .
Both strategies depend on reliable feedback about whether the task has been completed.
Beyond standardized benchmarks, reference answers and task completion signals may be unavailable, leaving the task request and observations from environment interaction as the main basis for verification. This setting creates two coupled challenges: inferring task success from incomplete observations, and improving the agent when the resulting feedback may itself be incomplete. The budget example in Figure 1 illustrates why both matter: finding expected labels does not establish task completion, while generating more outputs under the same checks can preserve the same blind spots. This motivates active verification and adaptation of the verification procedure. Existing methods address parts of these challenges. Tool-based critique can acquire additional observations ( Gou et al., 2024 ) , and retrospective diagnosis and preference history can guide updates to actor procedures ( Pan et al., 2026 ; Lee et al., 2026 ; Nie et al., 2026 ) , but they do not update the verification procedure itself. Figure 1 : Active verification of a budget workbook. The task requires correct payer assignments, consistent expense totals, and after-tax revenue. Initial checks inspect labels and headers; an updated Evaluator skill retrieves expense and revenue cells while the workbook remains selected. Offline recomputation checks expense totals and after-tax revenue from the retrieved cells. Incomplete verification can leave blind spots unresolved and misguide later adaptation ( Wang et al., 2026a ) . Red Queen Gödel Machine jointly evolves agents and evaluators, using held-out ground truth to guide Evaluator replacement ( Iacob et al., 2026 ) . We study within-task adaptation where execution and verification evolve without external feedback from benchmark scores or labeled references. We introduce Verify by Doing ( VbD ), which couples task execution with active verification.
The Actor executes the task through an editable harness of instructions, skills, and working checks, while the Evaluator uses its own harness to inspect artifacts and environment state, recording satisfied, violated, and unresolved requirements.
These findings ground preferences between task results and guide updates to both harnesses. Updating the verification procedure changes how this internal signal is produced, while requirements remain fixed. After initialization, VbD tests retained and proposed Actor harnesses in the task environment (Figure 2 ).
Each trial produces a candidate comprising an execution record, resulting state, and response or deliverable.
Proposed Evaluators are reviewed against the retained version on the same candidates, comparing the evidential support and applicable criteria in their reports.
The retained Evaluator’s findings guide output selection, while Actor adoption separately requires support for the proposed harness’s own fresh output.
An Editor uses the findings, comparison decisions, and task history to propose further updates to either or both harnesses. VbD makes three contributions: • Active verification: verify by doing. Tool-based inspections gather evidence of task completion from artifacts and environment state. • Evidence-driven harness co-evolution. Shared findings guide Actor and Evaluator harness updates within a single task. • Comparative review and separate retention. Trials and report comparisons support separate decisions for Evaluator adoption, candidate selection, and Actor retention. Across AutomationBench, τ 3 \tau^{3} Banking, and selected GDPval tasks ( Patwardhan et al., 2025 ) , VbD achieves the highest overall scores among the compared methods without external feedback on two backbones.
Ablations support the contributions of active verification and Evaluator evolution, with positive mean gains on all three task sets.
Holding BoN candidates fixed, the retained Evaluator improves pass rate and partial credit over the initial Evaluator, demonstrating better selection from the same candidate outputs.
Case studies connect verification findings to execution improvements and selection of earlier successful candidates, while revealing remaining errors in evidence interpretation. The contact-sheet example connects one finding to changes in both roles. 2 Related Work Improving agent procedures. Agent improvement methods differ in what they update and how feedback guides those updates. DSPy optimizes modular language-model pipelines, while AFlow searches code-represented workflows ( Khattab et al., 2024 ; Zhang et al., 2025 ) . Voyager accumulates executable skills through environment feedback ( Wang et al., 2023 ) . Self-Refine and Reflexion improve outputs or later behavior ( Madaan et al., 2023 ; Shinn et al., 2023 ) . GEPA evolves prompts, while DGM, HGM, and Hyperagents edit agent programs or improvement procedures ( Agrawal et al., 2026 ; Zhang et al., 2026b ; Wang et al., 2026b ; Zhang et al., 2026c ) . Self-Harness accepts edits using regression tests ( Zhang et al., 2026a ) ; ModularRSI combines program checks, overfitting review, and execution checks ( Wu et al., 2026 ) .
Several methods adapt harnesses without labeled validation signals. RHO diagnoses past rollouts and selects harnesses for future tasks using a fixed self-preference ranking procedure ( Pan et al., 2026 ) . RHI revises a task-specific textual harness from pairwise preference history under a fixed evaluation prompt ( Lee et al., 2026 ) ; TTHE edits executable harnesses from unlabeled traces ( Nie et al., 2026 ) . In contrast, VbD uses verification findings to update both Actor and Evaluator harnesses. Obtaining evidence for judgment. Judging task completion can require observations beyond the Actor’s trace. ReAct interleaves reasoning with actions that gather external information ( Yao et al., 2023 ) . CRITIC uses tools to critique outputs, Self-RAG learns retrieval and critique decisions, and LATS combines environment feedback with search ( Gou et al., 2024 ; Asai et al., 2023 ; Zhou et al., 2024 ) . Agent-as-a-Judge inspects artifacts against requirements, while VAGEN actively probes graphical interface state ( Zhuge et al., 2025 ; Cui et al., 2026 ) . The Verification Horizon studies informative verification as generation changes; AIChilles searches for workloads exposing regressions ( Wang et al., 2026a ; Zhou et al., 2026 ) . Studies of intrinsic self-correction report failures without external feedback ( Huang et al., 2024 ) , while ProCo improves correction through structured condition verification ( Wu et al., 2024b ) . VbD carries inspection findings into later rounds as proposed changes to both execution and verification. Co-evolving execution and evaluation. Process supervision trains reward models with human feedback on intermediate reasoning steps ( Lightman et al., 2024 ) . Other training approaches couple generation with learned rewards or feedback on judgments ( Yuan et al., 2024 ; Wu et al., 2024a ) ; related work studies rubric learning and prover–verifier interaction ( Ding et al., 2026 ; Kirchner et al., 2024 ) . At the program level, RQGM co-evolves agents and evaluators through controlled utility evolution: evaluation remains fixed within each search stage and changes at qualified boundaries ( Iacob et al., 2026 ) . Its shared workspaces also permit edits affecting both roles. Double Ratchet selects evolving metrics with an anchored reference set ( Zhang et al., 2026d ) . VbD uses active verification to couple execution and verification updates within a task, without external feedback. Comparative review evaluates evolving feedback procedures before they guide output selection and Actor retention decisions separately. 3 Problem Setting We study agent improvement within a task x x , given its request, materials, and permitted tools. Each Actor candidate links an execution record, resulting state, and response or deliverable to its generating harness. Benchmark outcomes, official rubrics, and expert outputs are reserved for offline scoring. Actor harness H H and Evaluator harness Φ \Phi contain instructions, working rubrics, and skills. Rubrics express checks against public requirements: Actor rubrics guide execution and self-checks; Evaluator rubrics guide assessment. Skills specify execution or verification procedures. H H also includes persistent check plans, distinct from temporary trial questions. Model weights, task requirements, permissions, editor, selector, and reviewer instructions, eligibility, and acceptance rules remain fixed. The retained state includes an incumbent candidate z z , candidate set 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , and history 𝒟 \mathcal{D} . The incumbent need not come from the current Actor. Harnesses and history reset between tasks; the final output is frozen before scoring. 4 Verify by Doing VbD addresses the two challenges in Section 1 by making verification both active and adaptable. Inspection supplies evidence for judging current outputs; the same findings guide changes to how the Actor executes and how the Evaluator checks completion. Because both procedures evolve, VbD reviews verification changes before selection and evaluates Actor changes through fresh trials. Initialization inspects two attempts from the initial Actor and selects an incumbent without adopting updates. The editor prepares proposals if another round remains. Following harness optimization ( Pan et al., 2026 ; Lee et al., 2026 ) , Step 1 runs the current Actor H H and proposed Actor H ~ \widetilde{H} to produce fresh candidates a a and b b . Both receive the same temporary checks; only the proposal uses persistent edits. This exposes both Actors to the current verification concerns while testing the proposed execution procedure. Temporary checks expire after the round unless reconsidered. Without a proposal, both attempts use H H . The unranked external feedback reference ( † \dagger ) runs HGM search under fixed benchmark scoring. 5.3 RQ1: Task Performance at Test Time Does co-evolving execution and verification improve task performance? VbD achieves the highest overall scores among the compared methods without external feedback on all three task sets (Tables 1 – 3 ). The gains span workflow execution, interactions requiring policy compliance, and professional deliverables, supporting the value of improving execution and verification together. The gains extend to professional deliverables: VbD leads all nine GDPval sectors, with 82.4% overall rubric quality versus RQGM’s 80.1%. On AutomationBench, selected outputs also satisfy more individual requirements, as shown by higher partial credit (Appendix B.7 ). The evidence differs across tasks, but verification serves a common purpose: inspection findings connect the Actor’s actions to observable task requirements. This grounds adaptation in evidence about completion while keeping the task requirements fixed, allowing the same findings to guide both execution and verification. The variant without active verification assesses existing traces without new inspections while retaining harness evolution. The variant without Evaluator evolution inspects candidates with fixed Evaluator instructions, rubrics, and skills while the Actor evolves. Tasks, initial harnesses, base model, three-round schedule, and strict Actor acceptance rule are shared. Both removals lower the mean score on every task set while Actor evolution remains active (Tables 1 – 3 ). On AutomationBench, removing active verification lowers the mean by 2.7 percentage points, and freezing the Evaluator lowers it by 1.6. Banking and GDPval follow the same pattern. The ablations distinguish acquiring evidence from retaining verification lessons. A frozen Evaluator still gathers observations, but its instructions, rubrics, and skills remain unchanged. Its lower scores support carrying lessons forward: missed requirements motivate persistent checks, and successful inspections supply reusable procedures. Active verification expands the evidence for current judgments, while Evaluator evolution adapts future checks under fixed task requirements. Actor evolution cannot fully compensate. Answer to RQ3. Active verification gathers evidence for current judgments, while Evaluator evolution refines the checks that produce the internal signal. The ablations support both components in improving task performance. 5.6 RQ4: Co-evolution in Practice How does a verification finding improve both execution and feedback? The cases trace how findings become reusable actions and checks through two paths: exposing a missed requirement and discovering an operation the Actor can reuse. In Sales063, initial attempts omit customer-record updates and violate a reply restriction (Figure 7 ). Verification accepts an untested claim that the service is unavailable and overlooks the restriction. VbD adds an Actor skill for reply scope and a matching Evaluator criterion; the next attempt completes the updates and satisfies the restriction. HGM and RQGM complete the main actions but retain replies naming excluded attendees. The case shows how a missed requirement becomes guidance for both execution and assessment. In Marketing015, the Evaluator retrieves a contact sheet through an operation the Actors missed. The successful retrieval becomes an Actor instruction and an Evaluator availability check (Figure 2 ); distribution then succeeds. The initial Evaluator also recognizes success. Verification supplies more than a verdict here: it reveals an action the Actor can reuse and a check for future claims that a resource is unavailable. Other cases show why output selection and Actor adoption are separate decisions. Support091 recovers an earlier passing output despite failed fresh attempts. Sales047 selects a passing new output but withholds the Actor update because the final comparison lacks strict support (Appendix C.8 ). Verification can still misinterpret evidence: Finance024 treats a file timestamp as a business date and selects a failing output (Appendix C.9 ). Actor behavior complements these cases: attempts containing state verification rise from 20.4% in Round 1 to 25.3% in Round 3 (Appendix D.1 ). The cases trace how findings become Actor checks; the aggregate trend is consistent with this transfer from verification to execution. Answer to RQ4. A verification finding can become both a better way to act and a better way to check success. The cases show how shared evidence turns a local discovery into reusable improvements to execution and verification. 6 Conclusion VbD co-evolves Actor and Evaluator harnesses through active verification, comparative review, and separate retention. Across three task sets and two backbones, it achieves the highest overall scores among the compared methods without external feedback. Ablations and fixed-candidate selection support improving execution alongside the verification procedure that supplies the internal signal. Together, these findings explain the value of evolving verification alongside task execution. Active verification supplies evidence, co-evolution turns findings into execution guidance and reusable checks, and separate retention distinguishes successful outputs from supported updates. Task requirements and model weights stay fixed; adaptation changes how the agent executes, checks completion, and carries verification findings into subsequent attempts. More broadly, VbD suggests that test-time agent improvement can progress beyond choosing among existing behaviors by evolving the procedures that produce and evaluate feedback. Limitations VbD relies on observable evidence and model-based judgments. The Actor, Evaluator, and reviewer may share errors in interpreting evidence, so active verification and comparative review cannot eliminate incorrect selections or updates ( Wang et al., 2026a ) . Repeated trials require states or artifacts that can be isolated and inspected. As with replay-based adaptation ( Pan et al., 2026 ) , irreversible actions and environments that cannot be reset limit direct application. Delayed effects or inaccessible state may also escape verification. Our experiments evaluate three-round adaptation within individual tasks. Transfer across tasks and sustained improvement over longer evolution remain untested. Additional execution, verification, and harness revision incur inference and tool-use costs; deployment latency also depends on environment response times. Ethical Considerations VbD studies tool-using agents on benchmark tasks. Errors in execution or verification can lead to unintended messages, incorrect record updates, or misleading deliverables. Harness adaptation retains fixed task requirements and tool permissions. Deployment in real environments should enforce these permissions, protect sensitive information in execution logs and artifacts, and require human oversight for consequential actions. Access to personal or business data should be limited to the authorized task, and use or redistribution of benchmark materials and derived artifacts should respect the applicable licenses and access conditions. Findings identify the candidate, proposition, observations, and assessment (Equation 1 ). The selector interprets these findings against the task requirements, including observed defects and unresolved requirements. A supported finding may establish a defect, such as excessive page count; its consequence for the task determines the preference, rather than the number of supported findings. Evaluator adoption requires a valid trial and agreement in both review orders. The retained Evaluator supports output selection; Actor adoption additionally requires strict support for the proposed Actor’s own output (Equation 5 ). Fallback selections leave the Actor unchanged. The editor uses findings and trial history, including rejected proposals, to update either or both harnesses. Each proposal connects an observed gap to an expected change in execution or verification. Task requirements, tool permissions, model weights, controller instructions, and acceptance rules remain fixed. VbD returns the final selected output, then resets its harnesses and history. B.1 Execution Schedule All iterative methods use three rounds with two Actor executions per round, totaling six attempts per task per run. For VbD, Round 1 runs the initial Actor twice to initialize the candidate pool and select an incumbent; no harness update is adopted in this round. Rounds 2–3 test current and proposed Actors using proposals prepared in the preceding round. SP uses the same schedule and budgets with fixed harnesses. To keep execution and assessment aligned, each candidate records its generating harness and resulting state or deliverable. Evaluators inspect a common pool, and selection uses the retained Evaluator’s findings, with further inspection as needed. An earlier candidate remains available through its saved output and state. Comparison stages and candidate sets. Evaluator qualification uses the common review pool ℬ \mathcal{B} ; output selection uses the eligible pool 𝒞 ⊆ 𝒢 \mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{G} , which may include earlier attempts. Candidate eligibility is fixed throughout evolution and excludes offline task-success labels. Reused evidence retains its candidate identity and observation scope. The main experiments and ablations apply the retention rules in Equations 3 and 5 , with the comparison procedure described in Appendix A . After the retention decisions, the editor proposes changes to instructions, rubrics, skills, or persistent check plans and specifies how to test them. Both fresh Actors receive the same temporary checks; only the proposed Actor receives persistent edits. Temporary checks expire after the round unless reconsidered. History records component sources, executed checks, and retention decisions. Changes to persistent Actor check plans count as Actor proposals even when the other harness text is unchanged. Budget boundaries. Operations are bounded by their assigned response limits and remaining task allowance. When no response remains, execution terminates with an error and produces no completed prediction. We assess Actor update decisions over the three-round evolution schedule for each method, using offline partial-credit scores for the recorded proposal–comparison pairs (Table 11 ). A proposal is positive if its output scores higher than its recorded comparison output, and negative if it scores lower. TP counts accepted improvements, FP counts accepted regressions, and TN counts rejected regressions, using valid, non-tied offline partial-credit pairs. The gate makes its decisions from public evidence; offline scores label the outcomes for this analysis. For each method, acceptance rate is N a / N p N_{a}/N_{p} , where N p N_{p} and N a N_{a} count all proposed and accepted updates. TNR is TN / ( TN + FP ) \mathrm{TN}/(\mathrm{TN}+\mathrm{FP}) , and precision is TP / ( TP + FP ) \mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FP}) ; both use valid, non-tied score pairs. All three metrics are percentages. These measures describe different aspects of retention. Acceptance rate gives its frequency, TNR measures rejection of observed regressions, and precision measures the fraction of improvements among accepted updates. VbD’s higher TNR accompanies similar precision and acceptance rates to RQGM. The observed distinction is therefore most pronounced in filtering regressions.
