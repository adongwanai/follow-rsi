Scientific discovery requires developing worthwhile hypotheses into practical investigations, yet learning from this evolving research process remains challenging. We introduce an operational definition of valuable and testable research hypotheses : evidence-grounded claims about consequential uncertainties, coupled with feasible investigations, explicit decision criteria, and alternative explanations. Our DiscoveryRSI framework develops hypothesis trees through multi-agent evolutionary search, compiles selected investigations into executable Harbor tasks, and recursively learns through three mechanisms: hypothesis credit optimization attributes scientific assessments to documented research contributions; harness replay identifies productive strategies and recurring failures to refine research workflows; and rediscovery applies accepted improvements to new investigations, generating further experience for subsequent learning. We also introduce DFM-Bench, which provides comprehensive assessment of hypothesis quality and cumulative research progress at the levels of hypotheses, revisions, development paths and research runs. Across four life-science domains, automated evaluation shows that hypothesis quality rises from 4.86 for the base model to 5.39 after two rounds of self-improvement, with the large gains in falsifiability and data resolvability. We further validate hypothesis quality through expert review, comparing our DiscoveryRSI-27B with other research agents. Preliminary human assessments show that DiscoveryRSI-27B scores higher than GPT-6 Luna on hypothesis importance and whether claims are plausible and evidence-bounded. DiscoveryRSI-27B achieved a 6.3% rate of hypothesis correspondence with research published after the model cutoff, largely outperform GPT-6 with 2.5% and Claude Opus 5 with 2.3%, while delivering greater human-rated improvement along research paths.

Research requires developing worthwhile hypotheses into practical investigations. Developing such hypotheses involves gathering evidence, revising explanations, and deciding which directions merit further effort. This process generates experience about both the hypotheses under investigation and the decisions that guide their development, which allows human scientists to better design their next study. For research agents, using this experience to improve future research requires identifying useful contributions and translating them into changes to the model and research workflow. This motivates our central research question: How can research agents turn hypothesis development and investigation into a recursive learning process that improves subsequent research?

Addressing this question requires connecting hypothesis development and experimental execution with learning that improves the agent over investigations. Co-Scientist ( Gottweis et al., 2026 ) develops hypotheses through multi-agent evolutionary refinement, while AI Scientist-v2 ( Yamada et al., 2025 ) connects hypothesis generation with experimental execution through agentic tree search. ScienceIDE ( Geng et al., 2026 ) constructs learning environments from scientific codebases, and ScienceBuddy ( Xue et al., 2026 ) turns researcher interactions into executable tasks and feedback for model and harness improvement. Building on these approaches, we focus on learning from hypotheses that agents actively develop: preserving how proposals evolve, linking assessments of research quality to the contributions behind them, and using this experience to improve subsequent investigations.

Turning hypothesis development into a recursive learning process presents challenges in both execution and learning. Translating a proposal into an executable investigation requires resolving its data requirements and verification criteria while preserving the research question. Learning from the resulting research experience involves a further challenge: a hypothesis’s final assessment does not specify which contributions should be reinforced. One agent may propose an explanation, another introduce a useful control, and a third revise the investigation plan in response to new evidence. Their contributions can remain obscured if only the final proposal is retained. Addressing these challenges involves making investigation plans concrete while preserving the development histories that connect assessments to the actions behind them.

To address these challenges, we introduce DiscoveryRSI , a general framework that turns hypothesis-driven research into a recursive learning process ( Figure 1 ). It develops research trees of testable hypotheses through multi-agent evolutionary search, preserving the evidence and decisions behind each proposal and revision. Selected computational investigations are compiled into executable Harbor tasks with concrete inputs, environments, required outputs, and verification checks. The retained research histories support three coupled recursive improvement mechanisms. Hypothesis credit optimization links assessments of research quality to documented contributions and assigns bounded credit to the contributing histories, providing supervision for the associated model actions. We use this supervision to train DiscoveryRSI-27B . Harness replay examines these histories to identify productive strategies and recurring failures, informing changes to agent coordination, evidence access, and research allocation. Rediscovery applies accepted model and harness updates to fresh investigations, generating new hypotheses and experience for subsequent learning.

We introduce DFM-Bench to evaluate hypothesis quality and cumulative research progress. It assesses whether proposals are worthwhile and testable, what revisions contribute, and how development paths and research runs accumulate evidence and explore different directions. These assessments use hypotheses and their supporting research records, with the execution readiness of compiled Harbor tasks measured separately.

Our contributions are fourfold:

An operational definition and a general framework for hypothesis-driven research. We define valuable and testable research hypotheses as joint objects comprising a claim, an investigation plan, decision criteria, and alternative explanations ( Definition 1 ). DiscoveryRSI develops these objects through multi-agent evolutionary search, compiles selected investigations into executable Harbor tasks, and preserves the underlying research decisions and development histories as reusable learning experience.

Recursive self-improvement from research experience. We develop hypothesis credit optimization to attribute assessments of research quality to documented contributions and train DiscoveryRSI-27B, harness replay to refine research strategies and workflows, and rediscovery to apply accepted improvements to subsequent investigations.

A benchmark for hypothesis quality and cumulative research progress. DFM-Bench evaluates hypotheses, revisions, development paths, research runs, and research contributions. We further evaluate DiscoveryRSI through expert-supervised review and controlled model comparisons with its base model and other research agents. Results show clear gains over rounds of self-improvements and changes in agent behavior patterns.

Empirical evidence for learning from scientific research. DiscoveryRSI increase mean hypothesis quality from 4.86 to 5.39, with gains in falsifiability, alternative explanations, and data resolvability. Expert-supervised assessment shows greater human-rated improvement along research paths, with an average gain per revision of + 0.07 +0.07 , compared with + 0.03 +0.03 for GPT-5.6 Sol and − 0.01 -0.01 for GPT-6 Luna. DiscoveryRSI-27B also achieves a 6.3% rate of hypothesis correspondence with post-cutoff research, compared with 2.5% for GPT-6 and 2.3% for Claude Opus 5. Together, these findings show that learning from research histories improves the formulation of testable investigations and the development of hypotheses toward concrete research opportunities.

We introduce a joint operational definition of valuable and testable research hypotheses for hypothesis-driven research agents. It makes the scientific value of a claim and the evidential capacity of its investigation explicit within one revisable research object.

The four components make this definition operational. The claim specifies the system or population, scope, observable quantities, and scientific uncertainty. The investigation plan identifies the data or measurements, comparisons, controls, analysis, and required dependencies. The decision criteria state, before interpreting results, which outcomes would support or weaken the claim and which would remain inconclusive. The alternative explanations identify competing accounts, confounding, or measurement limitations, together with the comparisons that can distinguish them and the limits of those comparisons.

Scientific value can arise from a new explanation, a useful replication, a measurement check, or an informative negative result when it changes understanding or research priorities. Testability depends on the declared evidence and constraints: unavailable measurements or experiments remain explicit dependencies until they can be obtained. These requirements assess a proposed research opportunity; they do not establish that the claim is true. DFM-Bench evaluates the resulting objects through its scientific-quality profile ( Section 4 ), while completed execution and independent support are recorded separately.

DiscoveryRSI focuses on how a research question is formed and tied to available evidence. IdeaScientist develops proposals from a specified problem and challenge, including evaluation plans and falsifiable predictions ( Liu et al., 2026 ) . Our emphasis is the joint development of a hypothesis and the investigation that can examine it: observations and data constraints shape the question, its comparisons, alternative explanations, and interpretation.

Descriptive analyses characterize observed patterns, variation, and measurement coverage, helping identify questions worth pursuing and the evidence available to investigate them. Each proposal then ties its claim to a defined system or population, observable quantities, and interpretable outcomes.

Its testability comes from a defined population and outcome window, patient-level held-out comparisons against simpler baselines, explicit interpretation criteria, and alternative explanations that bound the claim. A supported lack of added information would also inform whether more complex models merit further investigation.

Recorded hypothesis versions retain links to their predecessors and supporting evidence. These records make changes in the question, investigation plan, and interpretation traceable, providing the research histories used by the trees and learning mechanisms in the following section.

DiscoveryRSI develops research hypotheses across episodes and retains the work behind each version as linked research records ( Figure 1 ). These records connect scientific assessments to contributing actions for model training, and connect execution failures to changes in the research harness. Selected investigations become executable Harbor tasks. Hypothesis credit optimization, harness replay, and rediscovery then turn this accumulated experience into a recursive learning process. Algorithm 1 summarizes the complete protocol, from within-run hypothesis development to accepted updates and fresh collection.

Each node is a versioned hypothesis with its investigation plan, decision criteria, alternative explanations, and evidence. Parent links record scientific development: a revision has a predecessor, while a combination can have several parents. The ancestry can therefore form a directed acyclic graph, although we use research tree as an intuitive description. It spans episodes and remains distinct from the sequence of agent actions that produced each node.

The hypothesis tree is part of a broader network linking hypotheses to sources and data, drafts and supporting artifacts, scientific assessments, agent contributions, and execution records. Files preserve the research material; registry entries, identifiers, paths, and available content hashes preserve its relationships. A shared artifact can inform several hypotheses without making them scientific descendants of one another. These links let us trace an assessed contribution to the histories that produced it, or trace a recurring failure to the decisions and execution events behind it. Appendix A specifies the record types and link semantics.

Following operator-based search such as Frontis-MA1 ( Yang et al., 2026a ) , evolution develops a hypothesis, repair addresses an identified defect, and crossover combines contributions from multiple parents. Scientific restart opens a new question without assigning parents; earlier work may remain as context. Lead selects directions, parents, and operations. Generation and Evolution investigate evidence and publish proposals or revisions. Blinded Critique compares scientific merit, while ancestor-aware Progress assesses substantive progress, necessary repair, repetition, or insufficient evidence. Their assessments guide subsequent work and are retained in the linked records.

Selected computational plans are compiled into Harbor tasks ( Harbor contributors, ) , with concrete inputs, environments, required outputs, and verification checks. Compilation preserves the hypothesis’s scientific commitments: the population or biological system, proposed comparison, discriminating outcomes, and interpretation criteria. Unavailable data and physical experiments remain explicit dependencies.

Execution checks establish whether a task satisfies its computational contract; scientific assessment determines what the investigation can establish. The compiled package remains linked to its selected hypothesis, inputs, artifacts, and validation records. Hypothesis development can therefore supply learning experience before a complete investigation has been executed.

Hypothesis credit optimization follows three links: scientific assessments determine hypothesis credit; contribution records allocate it to documented research histories; supported actions in those histories become training targets ( Figure 1 c).

Is an individual hypothesis worthwhile and testable? Assessment examines the proposal, investigation plan, and supporting evidence. It produces a scientific-quality profile covering importance, novelty, grounding, falsifiability, data resolvability, feasibility, alternative explanations, and actionability, together with an aggregate score when the required assessments are complete.

What did a revision contribute? Assessment compares a hypothesis with its actual predecessor and examines the evidence and actions supporting the change. It distinguishes substantive progress, necessary repair, repetition, and insufficient evidence. Changes in hypothesis scores are recorded separately from these judgments.

Does a development path accumulate useful evidence? Assessment follows an ordered sequence of actual ancestry relationships, examining evidence acquisition, coherent revision, scientific continuity, and decisions to continue or stop. Agent action histories provide supporting evidence for these judgments, while the development path records how the hypotheses themselves changed.

Does a research run explore and allocate effort productively? Assessment examines the full research graph, selection and stopping decisions, and available opportunity and resource records. It considers the breadth and depth of exploration, synthesis across branches, and allocation of the research budget. Run-level quality is assessed directly rather than obtained by averaging path scores.

What distinct scientific contribution emerges across related proposals? Assessment groups proposals by scientific question and records what was added beyond the starting evidence: a new distinction, an informative negative result, a supported finding, or a reduction in uncertainty. Contribution maturity distinguishes proposed ideas from contributions supported by completed investigations and independent checks.

Existing benchmarks examine complementary aspects of scientific work, including ideation, goal-directed discovery, analytical decisions, and executable tasks ( Mo et al., 2026 ; Majumder et al., 2024 ; Gu et al., 2025 ; Chen et al., 2024 ) . OEB further evaluates evidence use, experimentation, and research-process integrity ( Shi et al., 2026 ) . DFM-Bench makes hypotheses, revisions, development paths, research runs, and contribution families explicit assessment objects. Table 1 compares their evaluation coverage; the accompanying definitions and qualifications appear in Appendix D.6 .

We conduct expert-supervised human assessment to examine the scientific value and design quality of generated hypotheses. The review considers whether a proposed investigation addresses a worthwhile question, fits the available data, distinguishes alternative explanations, and yields interpretable outcomes.

The assessment involved 27 medical doctors, medical students and bioscience PhD students with prior biomedical or clinical research backgrounds, under three senior supervisors. The review cohort contains 292 proposals from six dataset groups spanning the four life-science domains: HCC clinical cohort (52), MIMIC-IV (60), eICU (60), UK Biobank (55), GEO targets (35), and GEO mechanisms (30). Proposals were drawn from a fixed hypothesis-atlas snapshot and presented in randomized order.

Reviewers received English proposal copies containing the scientific question, investigation plan, data requirements, alternative explanations, and supporting citations. Producer and execution provenance were withheld, while scientific methods and references were retained. AI reviewer grades were hidden, and reviewers were instructed to assess each proposal on its own merits without consulting its revision history in the atlas. The interface presented the proposal alongside the assessment form ( Figure 3 ); Appendix E consolidates the review protocol.

The protocol asks reviewers to record an overall research-value judgment first, using a five-point scale from not worth pursuing to top priority, together with a recommendation to prioritize, revise, defer, or reject the proposal. Reviewers then assess six criteria on anchored four-point scales: importance, advance over known work, plausible and evidence-bounded claims, data fit, alternative explanations, and informative results. A separate verdict identifies whether a consequential design flaw is absent, fixable, or fatal. “Can’t judge” is available when evidence or relevant expertise is insufficient. A flaw checklist, a description of the main flaw, and further comments are optional.

We evaluate DiscoveryRSI across four life-science domains: clinical and population health, therapeutic target prioritization, disease mechanisms, and population multi-omics. Inputs include the HCC clinical cohort, MIMIC-IV ( Johnson et al., 2023 ) , eICU ( Pollard et al., 2018 ) , UK Biobank ( Sudlow et al., 2015 ) , and molecular studies from GEO and ArrayExpress ( Edgar et al., 2002 ; Sarkans et al., 2021 ) . Agents inspect data and literature, formulate hypotheses, and develop investigation plans specifying comparisons, measurements, and interpretation criteria ( Figure 4 ).

To test transfer beyond life science, we add a battery setting with four domains: electrical diagnostics, health prediction, degradation mechanisms, and usage history and aging. The evidence pack covers capacity and impedance aging measurements ( Luh and Blank, 2024 ) , impedance-based health prediction ( Zhang et al., 2020 ; Jones et al., 2022 ) , degradation-mode analysis ( Li et al., 2025 ; Kirkaldy et al., 2024 ) , and path-dependent aging experiments ( Raj et al., 2020 ) . Investigations separate measured quantities from fitted degradation labels and specify cell, condition, and protocol splits. The same Lead–Generation–Evolution–Critique–Compiler loop is used, with battery-specific checks for operating conditions, sensing, physical plausibility, and competing explanations.

We perform full-parameter fine-tuning of DiscoveryRSI-27B with a 256K-token context window using hypothesis-credit-weighted supervision. From 15,792 collected research trajectories, attribution, filtering, and tokenization yield 2,397 training trajectories: 634 Lead, 861 Generation, and 902 Evolution histories. The training sequences contain 291.12 million tokens, of which 6.18 million (2.12%) receive direct supervision. We train for one epoch using Adam with a learning rate of 5 × 10 − 6 5\times 10^{-6} , a global batch size of 16, and BF16 precision. Detailed trajectory accounting, credit allocation, and token masking appear in Appendix C .

We examine four questions: whether hypothesis quality improves across research rounds, how research behavior changes, whether human reviewers recognize useful revisions, and whether hypotheses inspire subsequent research. Automated quality evaluation covers 241 research questions across the four domains using GPT-5.6 Sol as the judge. Behavioral analysis examines six campaigns of 20 episodes per evaluated model variant. Human assessment covers 292 proposals reviewed by 27 participants with biomedical or clinical research backgrounds under three senior supervisors. Proposals are presented in randomized order with model identities and automated scores hidden ( Section 5 ). Literature-based evaluation uses a shared reference corpus with model-specific temporal cutoffs.

Mean automated hypothesis quality increases from 4.86 for the base model to 5.18 after the first research round and 5.39 after the second ( Figure 5 ). The second-round model exceeds the base model in all four domains, with gains ranging from 0.28 points in disease mechanisms to 0.94 points in population multi-omics. The additional gain decreases from 0.32 in the first round to 0.21 in the second.

Falsifiability rises from 4.00 to 6.20, consideration of alternative explanations from 4.44 to 5.98, data resolvability from 3.73 to 4.99, and feasibility from 4.79 to 5.87. These changes indicate more explicit tests, better-defined evidence requirements, and clearer interpretation of possible outcomes. Gains are uneven: importance declines from 6.48 to 6.04 and novelty from 4.87 to 4.58. The observed improvement therefore concerns investigation design more strongly than the perceived significance or originality of research questions.

Evolve and repair operations increase from 77.5% of episodes for the base model to 90.0% after the first round and 91.7% after the second. Restarts decrease from 21.7% to 5.8% and 5.0%, respectively ( Figure 6 ). The shift is especially pronounced in episodes 11–20, where restarts fall from approximately 32% for the base model to 3% after the second round.

Hypothesis development paths become more evidence-driven. Development-path scores increase most for evidential and inferential discipline ( + 1.25 +1.25 ), followed by technical and knowledge exploration ( + 0.34 +0.34 ) and scientific continuity and synthesis ( + 0.24 +0.24 ). Changes in cumulative progress, stopping decisions, and budget efficiency remain below 0.1 points, while disciplinary connections decline by 0.28. Together, these results suggest more sustained refinement and stronger evidential discipline, with limited improvement in broader exploration or resource allocation. Operation frequencies describe the research strategy; their value depends on the quality of the resulting revisions.

Discovery agents increasingly support hypothesis generation, experimental planning, and computational investigation. Co-Scientist develops hypotheses through multi-agent refinement ( Gottweis et al., 2026 ) , AI Scientist-v2 connects research ideation with agentic experimental search ( Yamada et al., 2025 ) , and Biomni integrates biomedical tools and data ( Huang et al., 2025 ) . Discovery Foundation Models frame problem formation and evidence-grounded revision as learnable capabilities ( Yang et al., 2026b ) , while ScienceIDE constructs environments for learning from scientific workflows ( Geng et al., 2026 ) . Evaluation spans scientific ideation in AgentIdeaBench ( Mo et al., 2026 ) , data-driven discovery in DiscoveryBench and BLADE ( Majumder et al., 2024 ; Gu et al., 2025 ) , executable investigations in ScienceAgentBench ( Chen et al., 2024 ) , and research-process assessment in OEB ( Shi et al., 2026 ) . DiscoveryRSI connects hypothesis development to subsequent model learning, while DFM-Bench makes hypotheses, revisions, development paths, research runs, and contribution families explicit evaluation objects.

Reward-guided training provides a foundation for converting generated experience into model updates. RAFT selects highly rated samples for fine-tuning ( Dong et al., 2023 ) , ReST-EM alternates generation and feedback-based filtering ( Singh et al., 2023 ) , and advantage-weighted regression learns through weighted likelihood objectives ( Peng et al., 2019 ) . SimpleTES further studies learning from scientific exploration trajectories ( Ye et al., 2026 ) . These approaches motivate the use of assessed research histories as supervision. DiscoveryRSI addresses the attribution problem within multi-agent hypothesis development: an assessment concerns a scientific proposal, whereas learning targets belong to the histories that contributed to it. Hypothesis credit optimization links these levels through documented contributions, bounded credit allocation, and supervision restricted to supported assistant actions.

Reflexion, GEPA, and ACE retain experience through verbal memory, prompt evolution, and contextual playbooks ( Shinn et al., 2023 ; Agrawal et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2025b ) ; harness optimization extends adaptation to agent workflows and implementations ( Zhang et al., 2026a ; Lin et al., 2026 ) . Darwin Gödel Machine and Hyperagents study persistent program-level improvement ( Zhang et al., 2025a ; Zhang et al., 2026b ) , while MASS couples multi-agent workflow optimization with learning from selected executions ( Lee et al., 2026 ) . ScienceBuddy connects harness refinement with model learning ( Xue et al., 2026 ) , and RSI-Anything uses predictive world models to guide interaction and retained system updates ( Cui et al., 2026 ) . DiscoveryRSI brings these directions to hypothesis-centered research: evolving claims, evidence, and investigation decisions provide a shared record for contribution-based model learning, harness replay, and subsequent rediscovery. Its focus is how experience accumulated while developing scientific questions can improve later investigations.

DiscoveryRSI connects hypothesis development with learning from documented research contributions. The present work reaches concrete hypotheses and compiled investigation plans. The next step is to determine which proposals merit investigation, using solvers and frameworks such as RSI-Anything ( Cui et al., 2026 ) to execute, assess, and refine them. Closing the discovery loop further requires wet-lab validation and methods that turn supportive, negative, and inconclusive outcomes into improved research decisions. Extending hypothesis discovery and DFM-Bench to additional scientific domains will help establish how these capabilities generalize. Together, these directions advance the DFM vision ( Yang et al., 2026b ) of agents that formulate worthwhile questions, investigate them, and learn from the resulting evidence.

DiscoveryRSI develops hypotheses comprising a scientific claim, investigation plan, decision criteria, and alternative explanations. This appendix describes the roles and records connecting hypothesis development to task delivery and subsequent learning.

A hypothesis version is one recorded proposal; a revision links it to its predecessor. Crossover can introduce several parents, so the research tree is generally a directed acyclic graph. A development path follows ordered hypothesis versions; a research run contains the complete graph and its decisions. An agent trajectory records one agent invocation, including instructions, actions, and observations. Development paths and agent trajectories are distinct evaluation and training units.

Lead chooses directions, parents, operations, and selections. Generation proposes hypotheses; Evolution develops or repairs them. Critique provides blinded preferences, Progress assesses revisions against ancestry, and Compiler translates selected plans into Harbor tasks. Evolve and repair retain an existing direction, crossover combines directions, and restart opens a new question. Shared context can inform a restart without creating parentage.

The record layer links hypotheses to evidence, data, contributions, and execution. Ancestry records scientific development; evidence reuse and execution dependencies are separate relations. Table 2 summarizes the retained records.

Multiplication by the fixed coefficient − ω i / | U i | -\omega_{i}/|U_{i}| proves equality of the objectives and their gradients. Repeated context has zero loss. The conditioning assumption is stronger than target conservation. The implemented 256K export retains initial instructions and bounded overlap, so a window can omit earlier context from a longer trajectory. It preserves target counts and fixed normalization but does not generally preserve full-history likelihoods or gradients. The proposition establishes equivalence only when conditioning is unchanged; independently renormalizing each window would also violate its assumptions.

The hypothesis profile uses eight dimensions: importance, novelty, grounding, falsifiability, data resolvability, feasibility, alternatives, and actionability. Their weights are 20, 20, 15, 10, 10, 10, 10, and 5, respectively, on a 1–10 scale. These are frozen internal protocol conventions, not empirically established optimal weights.

A rubric judge, a domain-scientist role, and a methods-reviewer role assess the same evidence independently of one another’s grades. The current panel uses separate prompts to the same model family. It therefore measures correlated AI perspectives, not three independent expert opinions. Each dimension takes the median when at least two reviewers provide a known score. A complete quality score Q Q requires all three reviews, majority eligibility, and all eight dimension medians. The dimension weights sum to 100 and define a weighted mean on the 1–10 scale. Unknown evidence remains unknown rather than becoming zero.

Review packets use source-verbatim cards and traceable supporting material. They distinguish a proposal’s scientific importance from its maturity: an important question need not already have a positive result. Conversely, a completed script does not demonstrate a consequential finding. The packet’s available literature bounds novelty judgments; global novelty is not inferred from limited retrieval.

Revision evaluation retains the four categorical judgments used by the progress contract. Substantive-progress rates, useful-step rates including repairs, repetition, and insufficient-evidence coverage are reported separately. Numeric Δ ​ Q = Q child − Q parent \Delta Q=Q_{\rm child}-Q_{\rm parent} is defined when both automated quality scores are available.

Development-path evaluation assesses explicitly ordered paths. The recorded protocol selects one longest-depth path to each terminal node, breaking ties by identifier, and excludes singleton paths. At crossovers, off-path parents remain context. Coverage therefore counts evaluated paths rather than every possible route through the graph. The seven weights are 5/30/20/20/10/10/5. Research-run evaluation independently inspects the entire graph using weights 25/15/15/20/10/10/5. Both use 0–4 dimension anchors, converted to /100 only under the required completeness and applicability rules.

A legitimately inapplicable dimension differs from an unknown one. Restricted-scope composites are grouped by applicability mask; missing required evidence prevents a full composite. Research-run scores are not averages of development-path scores. Shared ancestors also make path-level measurements correlated, precluding naive independent-sample interpretations.

Scientific-contribution assessment groups related candidates into contribution families and records what was added beyond the starting evidence. Family membership does not imply confirmation; maturity cannot transfer automatically from one member to all others. Negative or inconclusive evidence can matter when it changes an important scientific decision. Human adjudication and independent evidence are needed for stronger claims of novelty and discovery.

Delivery is recorded alongside this scientific profile: proposed, selected, compiled, readiness-passed, executed, and independently reviewed. An unattempted check is distinct from a failure. The protocol does not combine delivery maturity and scientific scores into one overall number.

Calibration concerns the meaning and consistency of judgments. The implemented workflow freezes domain anchors, packets, aggregation, disagreements, and missingness. Same-packet comparisons against fresh-context AI references provide development diagnostics. Human calibration requires independent expert judgments, adjudication, and unseen acceptance cases; it remains pending. Prompt-role agreement alone does not establish validity.

The DFM framework’s distinction between current knowledge progress and future capability growth ( Yang et al., 2026b ) applies across all five objects. A good final hypothesis does not establish a sound development path, and a disciplined research run does not establish that the trained model improves on future research runs. Frontier plots are useful diagnostics, but a retrospective maximum over final-snapshot scores is monotone by construction and cannot demonstrate learning.

Each assessment identifies its object, source snapshot, rubric and protocol versions, supporting evidence, scores, rationale, and review status. Hypothesis assessments use a frozen proposal and its evidence. Revisions require actual parent–child pairs; development paths require ordered ancestry; research-run assessment requires the full graph and recorded decisions. Agent trajectories supply evidence about the work behind these objects.

Scores are recorded as known, unknown, or inapplicable under the rubric. Unknown values are not replaced by zero. An incomplete assessment is reported with its coverage and does not receive a complete composite. Resource-allocation judgments require recorded budgets and opportunity information; proposal counts do not reconstruct missing budgets. Contribution records additionally identify the scientific question, the increment beyond starting evidence, and its status as supported, contradicted, inconclusive, or unresolved. Evidence maturity distinguishes proposed, source-grounded, diagnostically examined, study-completed, and independently checked contributions.

Table 1 compares explicit evaluation constructs, not the capabilities of agents that happen to run on a benchmark. Hypothesis merit concerns scientific value and testability; contribution maturity distinguishes proposed, examined, and independently supported contributions. Evidence use concerns grounding analyses or claims in available evidence. Revision assessment compares a change against a predecessor; development-path assessment examines cumulative inquiry; whole-run allocation assesses decisions among research directions. A cross means no explicit assessment under these definitions, not that the benchmark lacks scientific value. Partial coverage identifies a related measure with narrower scope.

AgentIdeaBench scores final ideas with literature-verified critics; its grounding assessment is partial evidence-use coverage. Its generation-time refinement experiments do not constitute an explicit revision or ancestry-path evaluation object ( Mo et al., 2026 ) . DiscoveryBench evaluates reference-aligned hypotheses and analytical facets for supplied discovery goals; this provides partial hypothesis-merit and evidence-use coverage, rather than an open-ended merit or contribution-maturity rubric ( Majumder et al., 2024 ) . BLADE compares conceptual variables, transformations, and statistical models against expert analyses, providing partial evidence-use coverage through analytical decisions ( Gu et al., 2025 ) . ScienceAgentBench assesses generated programs, outputs, and task-stage rubrics; this is partial evidence-use coverage rather than evaluation of evolving scientific claims ( Chen et al., 2024 ) .

OEB explicitly assesses evidence, experiments, and responses to contradicted ideas. Its inquiry grouping and search-topology measures supply partial development-path coverage; its budget and concurrency profiles supply partial whole-run allocation coverage because they describe research style without ranking one style above another. It does not supply the proposal-merit or contribution-maturity assessments defined here ( Shi et al., 2026 ) . DFM-Bench’s checks indicate implemented rubric coverage conditional on sufficient records, not independent expert validation or empirical superiority over these instruments.

Human assessment examines the value and design quality of proposed investigations, with automated scores hidden. Overall value, criterion ratings, recommendations, and design-flaw verdicts are separate judgments.

The review involved 27 medical doctors, medical students, and bioscience PhD students with biomedical or clinical research backgrounds, under three senior supervisors. The fixed hypothesis-atlas cohort contains 292 proposals: HCC (52), MIMIC-IV (60), eICU (60), UK Biobank (55), GEO therapeutic targets (35), and GEO disease mechanisms (30). The groups span the four evaluated life-science domains.

English proposal packets included the scientific question, investigation plan, data requirements, alternative explanations, and citations. Model identity, execution provenance, and automated grades were hidden. Proposals were presented in randomized order, and reviewers assessed each packet without consulting its revision history. Figure 3 illustrates the interface.

Reviewers first recorded overall research value on a five-point scale from not worth pursuing to top priority, together with a recommendation to prioritize, revise, defer, or reject. They then rated six criteria on anchored four-point scales: importance, advance over known work, plausible and evidence-bounded claims, data fit, alternative explanations, and informative results. A separate verdict classified consequential design flaws as absent, fixable, or fatal. Reviewers could select “Can’t judge” when evidence or expertise was insufficient; flaw checklists and explanatory comments were optional.

The five-point overall judgment is distinct from the four-point criterion profile. Recommendations to revise or defer do not imply endorsement, and design-flaw verdicts concern the investigation plan.

The revision analysis compares human-rated quality for a revised hypothesis and its actual predecessor. Positive, zero, and negative score changes define improved, unchanged, and worsened revisions, respectively. The reported mean change and outcome proportions characterize the assessed parent–child pairs. They do not estimate the effect of a model update across independent investigations.

Shared ancestry makes revision observations dependent. This assessment is distinct from calibration of the automated evaluator against independently adjudicated, unseen cases.

This appendix defines the retrospective literature comparison used in RQ4. “Inspiration” denotes a concrete scientific connection: a reference after the recorded cutoff provides an experimental design, comparator, measurement, or testable evidence gap relevant to developing or testing a generated hypothesis. It does not imply that the publication’s authors encountered the hypothesis or were causally influenced by it.

One model-blind reference corpus was constructed from deterministic source-only anchors. A completed citation wave followed 420 backward and forward citation traversals, yielding 17,790 citation edges and 16,440 resolved works. The same corpus was available to all model groups within the publication window from 1 April 2025 through 14 October 2026.

References were filtered by each group’s recorded operational cutoff. Exact-day boundaries were retained for GPT serving identifiers. Month-level boundaries were used for Qwen (April 2025, a provisional family proxy), Claude Opus 5 (May 2026), DeepSeek V4.1 (September 2026, a release-month proxy), and GLM-5.3 (August 2026, a release-month proxy). These boundaries make the comparison reproducible; they do not identify internal model knowledge cutoffs.

Candidate retrieval required the best reference card to share at least three non-stopword terms with the hypothesis. This lexical gate determines retrieval eligibility, not semantic relevance. GPT-6 Luna supplied the semantic judgments, with 145 successful judgments from 149 sampled records. Four records lacked successful judgments. Exact duplicate hypothesis–reference inputs shared a judgment, which was propagated to 903 registered hypothesis versions. The resulting labels are correlated automated assessments rather than independent expert reviews.

The inspiration rate uses all generated hypothesis versions as its denominator, as defined in Eq. ( 7 ). Only versions receiving a positive judgment contribute to the numerator. Consequently, absence of a positive label may reflect retrieval or audit coverage as well as lack of a qualifying connection. The underlying audit calls this endpoint reference actionability; the report uses the operational term inspiration rate consistently.

The targeted analysis in RQ4 separates retrieval coverage from positive judgments conditional on retrieval. Only 15/131 Claude proposals passed the retrieval gate (11.5%), compared with 355/1,176 DiscoveryRSI proposals (30.2%). Among retrieved proposals, the positive fractions were similar: 20.0% and 20.8%, respectively.

Claude’s median proposal length was 41,134 characters, whereas retrieval used the first 6,000 characters and semantic review used the first 2,500. Provenance and revision notes could therefore displace scientific content. A diagnostic substituted proposal titles as queries while preserving the corpus, scoring method, date rule, and overlap threshold. Claude’s retrieval count increased from 15 to 95. This establishes sensitivity to representation, not additional positive semantic matches. A structured scientific representation and renewed judgments across all groups would be needed to assess the effect on the final comparison.

The audit observes literature correspondence, not researcher exposure, adoption, study initiation, or causal influence. Prospective evaluation would freeze generated hypotheses before researcher review and follow subsequent investigation plans, experiments, and publications.
