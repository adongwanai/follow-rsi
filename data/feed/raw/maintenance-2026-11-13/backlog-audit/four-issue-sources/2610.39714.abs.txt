Collins , Wengong Jin View a PDF of the paper titled MoLRSI: A Recursive Self-Improving AI agent for Molecular Discovery, by Yikun Zhang and 17 other authors View PDF HTML (experimental) Abstract: Molecular discovery requires navigating vast combinatorial search spaces while balancing competing biological objectives. AI agents can automate expert-defined molecular design protocols, but automation alone does not address how to optimize workflows for new tasks or improve their development through experience. Here we introduce MoLRSI, an AI agent that combines systematic optimization of workflows and model architectures with persistent cross-task memory to recursively improve its search policy for molecular discovery. Workflow performance, refinement outcomes, and execution feedback guide subsequent searches, allowing the agent to improve both its workflows and its strategy for developing them. We evaluate MoLRSI in two challenging molecular discovery settings that require balancing competing properties. In antibiotic discovery, MoLRSI developed a screening workflow balancing antibacterial activity, cytotoxicity and structural novelty, achieving an experimental hit rate of 15.9%, compared with 8.7% for a previous expert-led workflow using the same training data and screening library. In de novo peptide-MHC binder design, MoLRSI developed workflows that jointly consider target binding and specificity, producing eight experimentally validated hits against MART-1 and SARS-CoV-1 pMHCs with preferential binding over tested off-targets while Claude Science completely failed. These findings demonstrate how experience-guided workflow optimization can address competing discovery objectives while improving the search strategies used in subsequent campaigns.
