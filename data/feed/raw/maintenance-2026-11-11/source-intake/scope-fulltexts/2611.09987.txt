We propose SD-ViT, a training framework that gives a usable exit at every block depth by making every depth produce discriminative embeddings, for budget-selected fixed-depth exits. Standard ViTs supervise intermediate layers only through backpropagation, so their intermediate representations are not directly optimized to form usable identity embeddings. SD-ViT adds a stochastic auxiliary signal: at each iteration a randomly selected intermediate block is passed through the same embedding head as the final layer and trained with a weighted margin-penalty softmax loss. The objective is thus stochastically sampled deep supervision through a shared classifier. Because both loss terms share that head and classifier, intermediate tokens are trained to map into the same embedding space as final-layer tokens. This matters for FR, where matching is by similarity: a probe processed at an early exit can be compared directly against full-depth gallery embeddings, with no gallery recomputation or adaptation layers. On established benchmarks, SD-ViT stays within 0.4 0.4 IJB-C points of the same backbone trained conventionally at full depth, while its early-exit probes recover over 96 % 96\% of their own full-depth performance at 50 % 50\% of the computational cost without modifying the reference gallery. Sharing ℋ \mathcal{H} and W W across depths places all embeddings in one space, so at inference a probe may exit at any depth k k (Eq. 5 ) and match a gallery enrolled once at full depth. 1 Introduction FR accuracy is driven by deep networks [ 22 ] trained with margin-based softmax objectives [ 14 , 47 , 27 ] , and increasingly by ViTs [ 17 ] , which surpass convolutional backbones on FR benchmarks [ 13 , 30 , 31 ] . A ViT processes every input through a fixed stack of L L blocks at the same cost regardless of input difficulty or device budget, which is at odds with deployment: one model must run from edge processors to datacenter accelerators under budgets differing by orders of magnitude [ 15 ] . Compact architectures [ 10 , 5 , 51 ] , compression [ 7 ] and distillation [ 23 , 33 , 28 ] each yield another static model from a separate run, so covering several budgets means training, storing and switching whole networks. Early-exit networks are the flexible alternative: one model with intermediate classifiers, so inference stops shallow for easy inputs or tight budgets [ 46 , 24 ] . They were built for closed-set classification, where each exit has its own classifier over a fixed label set and the exits need not share a representation [ 46 , 24 , 29 ] . FR does not fit that template: it is open-set, matching a probe to a gallery of enrolled embeddings by cosine similarity, so independent per-exit heads produce incompatible spaces and a shallow embedding cannot match a full-depth gallery. The closed-set and distillation analogues, per-exit heads that would require re-embedding the gallery at every depth [ 3 ] and learned adaptation layers between depths [ 41 ] , are unattractive here. Galleries can be massive, distributed or privacy-constrained, and adaptation layers break the single-embedding-space assumption enrollment pipelines rely on. Early-exit FR therefore requires that every depth emit embeddings in one shared space, compatible with a gallery enrolled once at full depth. We address this with S tochastic D eep-supervision for ViTs (SD-ViT), a shared-classifier form of deep supervision (Fig. 1 ). At each iteration, besides the full-depth output, SD-ViT reads out one auxiliary embedding at a uniformly sampled intermediate block, decodes it with the same head, and scores it against the same prototypes under the same margin-penalty loss. The shared head maps tokens from every depth into one embedding space, and the shared prototypes give each identity a single center in it. Deep supervision is long established [ 32 , 45 ] . What distinguishes SD-ViT is not that intermediate depths are supervised but through what : the final exit’s own head and classifier instead of exit-specific ones, which makes the embeddings interchangeable across depth. Our controls show that the sampled exit must be trained by the margin loss itself, not by feature matching (Sec. 4.3 ). At inference a probe may exit at any depth and match a full-depth gallery by plain cosine similarity. Sampling a different block each step spreads supervision uniformly over depth, so every block depth becomes a usable exit after one training run. Sampling is a single-sample estimator of supervising all exits, and Sec. 4.3 finds it on par with deterministic all-exit supervision at one seventh of the auxiliary cost. We contribute: (i) the identification of gallery consistency , one shared embedding space across depths, as the central obstacle to early-exit FR, (ii) SD-ViT, in which a uniformly sampled auxiliary exit shares the final layer’s head and prototypes, making every depth gallery-compatible with no teacher and no adaptation layers, and (iii) evidence that one training run yields a usable exit at every block depth, with full-depth accuracy within 0.4 0.4 IJB-C points of conventional training, no gallery recomputation, and a single verification threshold usable from quarter depth onward. 2 Related Work Identity objectives for FR. Margin-penalty softmax losses [ 14 , 47 , 30 , 6 ] and auxiliary objectives [ 48 , 21 ] separate identities and compact each around its center, but only for the final-layer embedding. SD-ViT applies such supervision at a sampled intermediate depth as well, against the prototypes that define the final space. Deep supervision and knowledge distillation. Deeply-supervised nets [ 32 ] and GoogLeNet [ 45 ] add auxiliary losses at intermediate layers to aid optimisation and discard the auxiliary heads after training. Knowledge distillation [ 23 ] transfers from teacher to student via soft-label or feature-matching objectives, and born-again networks [ 20 ] and deep mutual learning [ 53 ] remove the fixed teacher. For FR it has used triplet [ 19 ] , rank-based [ 28 ] , angular [ 44 ] and reverse-feature [ 33 ] objectives, and AdaDistill [ 9 ] injects teacher-derived class centers into the student margin loss. BYOT [ 52 ] combines the two: it attaches auxiliary classifiers to intermediate CNN sections and distills from the deepest into the shallower ones. SD-ViT differs from both lines. Its auxiliary exit is not discarded after training but deployed as an early exit, and it is decoded by the final exit’s own head and scored against the same prototypes instead of a separate classifier. It uses no teacher and no adaptation layers, and couples the exits solely through this shared head and prototypes. Early-exit and budget-selected inference. Early-exit networks add intermediate classifiers so that easy inputs terminate early [ 46 , 24 , 29 , 56 ] . LayerSkip [ 18 ] is the closest precedent for our parameter sharing: it trains a language model with layer dropout [ 25 ] and an early-exit loss through a shared output head, so intermediate layers become decodable without per-layer heads. Among early-exit ViTs, LGViT [ 50 ] uses heterogeneous local-global heads and EnViT [ 16 ] exit-aware dropout, both trained by self-distillation. All target closed-set classification, where the label set ties the exits together and nothing forces their representations to be comparable. Open-set FR adds that requirement. Token pruning and merging [ 39 , 4 ] reduce the cost of each block, not the number of blocks, so they are orthogonal to SD-ViT and could be combined with it. Closest to our setting, ViT-FREE [ 12 ] performs training-free early exiting in pretrained ViT-FR models, exploiting constant token dimensionality with a synthetic-data adaptation step, and EX-FIQA [ 38 ] fuses intermediate representations for quality assessment. SD-ViT shares the observation that a ViT keeps one dimensionality at every depth, but instead of reading exits from a fixed backbone post-hoc it optimizes every block to produce gallery-consistent embeddings. We compare against both of its settings (Sec. 4.2 ). Training-free ViT-FREE is precisely our Baseline read-out. ViT-FREE FT trains an exit-specific projection and prototype set per depth. We report it under self-evaluation, the setting it was designed for, and under our gallery-fixed setting. ViTs for FR. TransFace [ 13 ] addresses ViT overfitting with patch-level augmentation and hard-sample mining, Part-fViT [ 43 ] adds landmark-driven part tokens, and KP-RPE [ 31 ] replaces learnable positional encodings with keypoint-relative ones. All treat the ViT as a fixed-depth backbone for a single full-depth pass, addressing neither multi-exit inference nor gallery-consistent retrieval. SD-ViT adds a stochastic shared-classifier deep-supervision objective that makes every block produce gallery-compatible embeddings, with no change to the inference architecture. At every training iteration we keep the full-depth (primary) embedding and additionally read out a secondary embedding at a randomly chosen block, e p = ℋ ⁡ ( z ( L ) ) , e s = ℋ ⁡ ( z ( k ) ) , k ∼ 𝒰 ⁡ { 1 , … , L − 1 } , e^{\mathrm{p}}=\mathcal{H}\!\left(z^{(L)}\right),\qquad e^{\mathrm{s}}=\mathcal{H}\!\left(z^{(k)}\right),\quad k\sim\mathcal{U}\{1,\dots,L-1\}, (3) with 𝒰 \mathcal{U} uniform over the first L − 1 L-1 blocks. Both exits are decoded by the shared head ℋ \mathcal{H} , including its LN \mathrm{LN} , and scored against the shared prototypes W W . Exposed to tokens from many depths, one LN \mathrm{LN} and one ϕ \phi must serve every block. Because a ViT keeps token dimensionality N × d N\times d at every block, unlike the changing width of CNN stages, the same head applies unchanged at any depth, with no dimensionality reduction and no per-exit adaptation layers. SD-ViT targets backbones of constant token width, and Sec. 4.3 verifies the objective on a second such backbone, KP-RPE [ 31 ] . Weighted dual objective. Both exits are supervised by the same margin-penalty loss, combined with a fixed weight ω \omega : ℒ = ω ​ ℒ cls ​ ( e p , y ) + ( 1 − ω ) ​ ℒ cls ​ ( e s , y ) . \mathcal{L}=\omega\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}\!\left(e^{\mathrm{p}},y\right)+(1-\omega)\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}\!\left(e^{\mathrm{s}},y\right). (4) The primary exit gets the larger weight, keeping full-depth accuracy the principal objective, while the secondary term supervises the sampled depth against the same W W . Why the exits become comparable. Eq. 4 never asks e s e^{\mathrm{s}} to match e p e^{\mathrm{p}} : there is no teacher, no detached target and no divergence term between the exits. Both terms are identity-label losses that update the backbone, ℋ \mathcal{H} and W W , and ω \omega only balances them. Whatever aligns the two exits must therefore come from what the two terms share, and we see two candidate effects. The first is the shared head: ℋ \mathcal{H} is one map, one LN \mathrm{LN} , one projection ϕ \phi and one set of batch-normalization statistics, that must turn the final tokens and the tokens of every sampled block into embeddings the margin loss can separate. At test time ϕ \phi is affine, so e L − e k e_{L}-e_{k} is the linear part of ϕ \phi applied to the difference of the two normalized token states. In a pre-norm ViT those states are two snapshots of one residual stream, z ( L ) = z ( k ) + ∑ l > k Δ l z^{(L)}=z^{(k)}+\sum_{l>k}\Delta_{l} . Making both discriminative through the same ϕ \phi favors later blocks that refine the identity information ϕ \phi already reads at depth k k , and does not re-encode it, which would keep e k e_{k} close to e L e_{L} . Per-exit heads remove this pressure, since each can read identity from a different subspace. The second is the shared prototypes. ℒ cls \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}} depends on e e only through the cosines { w ^ j ⊤ ​ e ^ } j = 1 C \{\hat{w}_{j}^{\top}\hat{e}\}_{j=1}^{C} and is minimized by driving e ^ \hat{e} toward w ^ y \hat{w}_{y} , so with one W W the embeddings of identity y y at every supervised depth are pulled toward one center. With the head shared, a separate prototype set for the intermediate exits costs about a point (Sec. 4.3 ). Both come from a single forward pass, since z ( k ) z^{(k)} is produced en route to z ( L ) z^{(L)} , so the auxiliary path adds one head-and-classifier evaluation. Shared statistics and prototype sampling. Three details follow from sharing ℋ \mathcal{H} and W W . (i) The batch-normalization layers of ϕ \phi are traversed twice per step. Each branch is normalized by its own batch statistics and both updates enter the same running estimates, so the deployed statistics mix the final and the sampled depths and one set serves every exit at test time. (ii) With PartialFC [ 2 ] both terms of Eq. 4 see the same positive prototypes, since the sampled subset always contains the batch labels. Negatives are redrawn per term, and both terms update the same W W . (iii) AdaFace’s running feature-norm statistics, which set its adaptive margin, are updated by both terms. 3.3 Early-Exit Inference At test time the model terminates at any depth k k and reads out an embedding through the same head, e k = ℋ ⁡ ( z ( k ) ) , k ∈ { 1 , … , L } , e_{k}=\mathcal{H}\!\left(z^{(k)}\right),\qquad k\in\{1,\dots,L\}, (5) where k = L k{=}L recovers the standard full-depth model. Gallery consistency. SD-ViT’s defining property for FR is that early-exit probes require no change to the gallery. Since ℋ \mathcal{H} and W W are shared, e ^ k \hat{e}_{k} lies in the same space as a full-depth gallery embedding, so cosine similarity stays meaningful between a shallow probe and deep references: no per-exit projection, no re-enrollment. The property holds within one model, with probe and gallery from the same network for the constant-width ViTs. An operational system also needs its threshold to survive the change of depth. A ROC-based metric does not establish this, since TAR at a target FAR may pick a different threshold per exit. Sec. 4.4 tests the stronger property: one threshold, calibrated on full-depth development scores, applied unchanged at every exit. Computational scaling. Exiting at block k k runs k k of L L blocks, cutting backbone computation to about k / L k/L , while the head runs on all N N tokens at a fixed cost. Choosing k k trades accuracy for latency within one model, with no retraining. Throughout, k k is selected by a compute budget, not per input: we evaluate budget-selected fixed-depth exits, not an input-adaptive policy. A per-image policy is left to future work. A uniformly sampled intermediate block is decoded by the same head and scored against the same prototypes as the final layer, with no teacher and no per-exit adaptation layers. An early-exit probe therefore matches a gallery enrolled once at full depth by plain cosine similarity, with a single verification threshold held fixed from quarter depth onward. Across ViT-S and ViT-B on WebFace4M and WebFace12M, SD-ViT stays within 0.4 0.4 IJB-C points of the identically trained baseline at full depth while recovering over 96 % 96\% of its own full-depth accuracy at half the computation. Our controls show that the sampled exit needs the margin loss itself, not feature matching. Future work includes input-adaptive per-image exit policies.
