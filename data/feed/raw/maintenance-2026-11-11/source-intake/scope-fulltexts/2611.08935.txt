However, conventional retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) typically relies on one-shot retrieval from flat corpora, limiting its ability to adapt retrieval granularity, explore multi-hop relations, and integrate multimodal clinical evidence. We introduce SkinMind , a multimodal agentic RAG framework that equips policy models with human-inspired graph probing for autonomous evidence seeking. SkinMind organizes retrieval as an iterative interaction between an orchestrator and a graph environment. Through eight hierarchical graph-probing tools, the orchestrator progressively identifies relevant entities, inspects node-level information, traverses relations and paths, and retrieves source-text and visual evidence. This progressive-disclosure design allows the model to adaptively determine the retrieval modality, exploration direction, and evidence granularity while constructing a query-specific evidence subgraph. To support autonomous graph probing, we construct SkinMind-KG from a 62M-token corpus of dermatology guidelines and textbooks and 423K images, guided by a dermatologist-informed schema and a conflict-resolution mechanism. The resulting graph contains over 1.0M nodes and 2.0M edges, unifying textual, relational, and visual evidence for retrieval. Across six dermatology VQA benchmarks and two policy-model settings, SkinMind improves the average score over direct answering by 8.70 8.70 and 8.89 8.89 points with GPT-5.4-mini and Gemini-2.5-Flash, respectively, while consistently outperforming competing RAG methods. Together, SkinMind and SkinMind-KG provide an effective foundation for evidence-grounded multimodal reasoning in dermatology. 1 Introduction Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in language understanding, visual perception, and multi-step reasoning, showing considerable potential for addressing complex real-world tasks [ 28 , 6 , 12 , 27 , 33 , 37 , 1 ] . Dermatology represents a particularly promising application: many skin conditions are visually observable, and patients increasingly seek convenient access to professional medical guidance through online consultations. Accordingly, medical and dermatology-specific MLLMs have shown potential across tasks such as visual question answering, diagnostic decision-making, and treatment recommendation [ 10 , 17 , 16 , 5 , 31 , 29 , 44 , 22 ] . Despite this promise, their deployment in safety-critical healthcare settings remains challenging. First, they may generate fluent but factually incorrect statements, potentially leading to harmful clinical decisions. Second, their parametric knowledge is acquired from static training corpora and cannot be readily updated as clinical evidence and guidelines evolve. Third, their responses often lack verifiable references to supporting medical evidence, substantially limiting their reliability and interpretability. Figure 1 : Performance comparison across six dermatology VQA benchmarks using Gemini-2.5-Flash as the policy model. SkinMind consistently outperforms direct answering and LightRAG. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) offers a promising means of mitigating these limitations by grounding model responses in external, authoritative, and traceable medical evidence [ 9 , 8 , 13 , 30 , 35 , 23 , 36 , 25 , 24 , 20 , 3 , 15 ] . Conventional RAG systems, however, typically perform a one-shot retrieval over a flat collection of textual chunks, presenting two limitations for complex medical reasoning [ 14 ] . First, one-shot retrieval relies solely on the initial query and cannot adapt to information needs that emerge during multi-step reasoning. Second, flat-chunk retrieval often provides fragmented evidence with insufficient relational context while introducing irrelevant information. Addressing the first limitation requires adaptive, multi-round retrieval, whereas addressing the second calls for a structured representation of external medical knowledge. Recent agentic RAG methods seek to address the first limitation by interleaving reasoning with iterative retrieval [ 8 , 4 ] . Rather than relying on a fixed set of documents retrieved upfront, the model dynamically determines when additional evidence is needed and formulates new queries based on its intermediate reasoning state. Nevertheless, because these methods typically operate over isolated text chunks, they do not fully resolve the fragmentation and loss of relational context inherent in flat-text retrieval.
Graph-based RAG improves upon flat-text retrieval by organizing knowledge into entities and relations [ 9 , 13 , 43 ] . However, many existing methods still treat the graph primarily as a retrieval index, selecting entities or subgraphs through embedding similarity or predefined expansion rules. Although the knowledge is graph-structured, the graph itself is rarely exposed to the model for interactive exploration. This contrasts with a human-inspired evidence-seeking process, in which a reasoner begins with a relevant concept, inspects its relations, follows informative paths, and adapts the exploration as new information emerges. This process follows the principle of progressive disclosure [ 32 ] , whereby lightweight structural cues guide navigation, while detailed descriptions or source content are accessed only when needed. Compared with retrieving a fixed candidate set, such navigation allows the retrieval path, scope, and granularity to evolve with the reasoning process, facilitating the flexible construction of query-specific evidence subgraphs while reducing irrelevant context. Inspired by this human-like evidence-seeking process, we propose SkinMind , a multimodal agentic RAG framework for autonomous and reliable dermatology assistance. Rather than treating the knowledge graph as a static retrieval index, SkinMind exposes it as an interactive environment and equips the agent with eight specialized graph-probing tools for interleaved reasoning and retrieval of textual, relational, and visual evidence. Given a clinical query, the agent autonomously identifies relevant entry nodes, inspects their relations, follows informative paths, and progressively accesses node descriptions, source content, and clinical images as needed. To provide a comprehensive and traceable foundation of domain-specific evidence, we construct SkinMind-KG , a large-scale heterogeneous dermatology knowledge graph containing over 1 1 million nodes and 2 2 million edges across 23 core entity types and 25 core relation types.
Notably, SkinMind-KG explicitly links 301,561 301{,}561 images to disease entities, providing rich visual–conceptual associations for multimodal evidence seeking. We conduct extensive experiments on six public dermatology VQA benchmarks spanning morphology recognition, disease diagnosis, and clinical reasoning in both closed- and open-ended settings. As illustrated in Fig. 1 , SkinMind consistently outperforms direct answering across all six benchmarks, with gains of 4.1 4.1 – 19.7 19.7 points under Gemini-2.5-Flash. More broadly, across both policy-model settings, it surpasses LightRAG by an average of 5.75 5.75 points with GPT-5.4-mini and 3.60 3.60 points with Gemini-2.5-Flash. These results suggest that adaptive graph exploration can support more effective use of external knowledge and improve multimodal clinical reasoning. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose SkinMind , a multimodal agentic RAG framework that treats a knowledge graph as an interactive environment, enabling interleaved reasoning and adaptive evidence seeking through eight graph-probing tools. • We construct SkinMind-KG , a large-scale heterogeneous dermatology knowledge graph comprising over 1 1 million nodes and 2 2 million edges, with more than 300,000 300{,}000 images explicitly linked to disease entities. • Extensive experiments on six dermatology VQA benchmarks across two policy-model settings demonstrate that SkinMind consistently outperforms direct answering and competing RAG methods, validating the effectiveness of adaptive graph exploration for evidence-grounded multimodal clinical reasoning. 2 Related Work Retrieval-Augmented Generation. The orchestrator adaptively explores SkinMind-KG through hierarchical graph-probing tools to construct query-specific evidence subgraphs for response generation. 3.1 Overview SkinMind is a multimodal agentic RAG framework equipped with a specialized suite of graph-probing tools for interleaved reasoning and evidence retrieval over a dermatology-specific graph environment. Given a clinical query, optionally accompanied by an image, SkinMind first routes the query through a slow–fast reasoning mechanism. Queries that can be answered reliably using the orchestrator’s internal knowledge are handled by the fast path, whereas queries requiring external evidence, multimodal retrieval, or multi-step reasoning are directed to the slow path. The slow path implements an iterative Agentic RAG loop with four components: an orchestrator , a suite of graph-probing tools , a graph-based environment , and a context manager . The orchestrator acts as the central policy model: it reasons over the current context, selects and invokes appropriate tools, and incorporates the returned observations into subsequent decisions. Once sufficient evidence has been collected, it terminates the interaction loop and generates an evidence-grounded response for the user. 3.2 Slow–Fast Thinking Let Q Q denote a textual clinical query and I I an optional clinical image. The ontology-guided pipeline integrates knowledge with conflict resolution, while UMAP projections of entity and relation embeddings reveal clusters across semantic categories. 4.1 Knowledge Graph Construction We construct the textual component of SkinMind-KG from a large-scale
dermatology corpus comprising 227 clinical guidelines and 542 textbooks
published since 2015. We collected dermatology guidelines and textbooks
from international online sources, selecting the latest available
guideline versions and textbook editions at the time of collection.
We parse the raw documents using
MinerU [ 26 ] and employ a lightweight LLM to extract
document-level metadata, including titles, authors, publishers,
publication years, and editions. To establish an authoritative disease taxonomy, we adopt the PanDerm
ontology [ 40 ] , which comprises 127 disease concepts and their
curated hierarchical relationships. We encode these relationships as IS_SUBTYPE_OF relations and preload the resulting hierarchy
into SkinMind-KG as its initial taxonomic backbone. Through iterative
consultations with senior dermatologists, we further define a schema
containing 23 entity types and 25 relation types to guide information
extraction.
Following LightRAG [ 13 ] , we segment the approximately
62M token corpus into chunks with a length of 1,200
tokens. GPT-5.4-mini is then used to extract entities, relations, and
their descriptions from each chunk according to the predefined schema.
During corpus extraction, the initial disease hierarchy can be extended
with additional disease entities and subtype relations. Entity and relation extraction is restricted to statements explicitly
supported by the source text. The source context guides the interpretation
of clinical qualifiers and the polarity of these statements. Extracted
entities and textual relations retain links to their supporting source
chunks, which preserve document-level provenance. Through these links,
the orchestrator can retrieve supporting passages and their document
identifiers using read_chunks when node-level descriptions are
insufficient, providing access to the original clinical context. Conflict Detection and Resolution. Integrating heterogeneous sources may introduce contradictory claims
about the same entity or relation. We therefore incorporate a detect-then-resolve procedure into the construction pipeline. A
conflict is identified only when two claims make contradictory
assertions about the same fact under the same clinical context. Claims
associated with different clinical contexts are retained as
context-specific evidence rather than treated as conflicts. For each detected conflict, Gemini-3.1-Pro [ 12 ] formulates targeted queries
and retrieves relevant evidence through Google Search. It then evaluates
the retrieved evidence to determine whether the conflict can be
reliably resolved. Resolvable cases are automatically reconciled,
whereas ambiguous cases are referred to domain experts. In total, the
procedure processed 1,375 description-level conflicts, automatically
resolving 1,366 cases (99.3%) and referring the remaining nine for
expert review. The overall procedure is illustrated in

We use SkinMind-KG as the retrieval corpus. To prevent benchmark leakage

We then exclude retrieved image nodes whose DermLIP cosine similarity to

duplicates. This two-stage procedure is applied identically to all RAG
methods to ensure a fair comparison. Baselines and Evaluation. We organize the baselines into three groups.
(i) Standard baselines comprise zero-shot inference without
retrieval ( Direct Answer ) and conventional single-round
retrieval augmentation ( Naive RAG ) [ 11 ] .
(ii) Graph-based RAG methods include GraphRAG [ 9 ] and LightRAG [ 13 ] , which leverage graph-structured knowledge for retrieval.
(iii) Agentic RAG methods include A-RAG [ 8 ] and RAGentA [ 4 ] , which interleave model reasoning with iterative retrieval.

DermoGPT-RL [ 29 ] , we report accuracy on the five
