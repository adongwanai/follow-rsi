When these models fail, there is no mechanism to correct their mistakes.
To address this limitation,
we introduce Click3R , a framework for interactive geometric correction of feed-forward models using sparse human correspondence clicks.
Given an image pair and a small set of cross-view point correspondences provided by a user, Click3R injects geometric constraints into the 3D reconstruction networks through a lightweight point correspondence adapter, enabling the model to resolve ambiguities in challenging scenarios such as repetitive textures, symmetric structures, large viewpoint changes, or textureless regions.

Our results highlight that sparse human interaction provides an effective and practical mechanism for correcting geometric reconstruction errors. † † footnotetext: ∗ Equal contribution. † Project lead. 1 Introduction Feed-forward stereo 3D reconstruction methods [ 44 , 16 , 29 , 49 ] have transformed multi-view geometry by replacing iterative optimization [ 25 , 34 ] with direct regression.
Given two images, these models predict dense 3D pointmaps in a single forward pass, achieving remarkable speed and generalization across diverse scenes.
This paradigm has recently been extended to multi-view settings [ 42 , 45 , 53 , 40 , 3 ] .
Despite their impressive performance, the efficiency comes at a fundamental cost: when the prediction is wrong, there is no simple mechanism to correct it. Unlike optimization-based methods [ 34 ] that iteratively refine their estimates using geometric constraints, feed-forward models produce a single, irrevocable output and fail silently, particularly on challenging scenes with repetitive textures, symmetric structures, or extreme viewpoint changes (Fig. 1 a,b). Several approaches aim to improve the reliability of feed-forward reconstruction models through test-time optimization [ 24 , 5 ] .
For instance, LoRA3D [ 24 ] introduces a self-calibration pipeline that fine-tunes a pretrained DUSt3R model [ 44 ] to a target scene.
Although these methods improve performance, iterative test-time optimization is computationally expensive and, more importantly, cannot resolve failures when the model encounters genuinely ambiguous scenes at inference time.
Another line of work improves robustness by incorporating auxiliary geometric priors.
For example, Pow3R [ 10 ] shows that conditioning on camera intrinsics, relative camera poses, or depth maps can significantly improve reconstruction performance.
However, such auxiliary information is often unavailable in practice.
Meanwhile, classical reconstruction methods have long relied on explicit correspondences as geometric constraints [ 34 , 31 , 22 ] .
Unfortunately, automatically estimated correspondences remain unreliable in scenarios with repetitive textures or large viewpoint changes. Figure 1 : Overview. Given (a) an image pair and sparse correspondences ( colored dot clicks ), Click3R conditions DUSt3R [ 44 ] to produce accurate 3D reconstruction. (b) DUSt3R fails on a scene with limited overlaps.
(c) With as few as two clicks, Click3R recovers correct geometry. Nevertheless, cross-view correspondences, even just a handful of point pairs, carry powerful geometric information: they constrain the epipolar geometry, resolve depth ambiguities, and disambiguate symmetric structures.
This motivates a simple question: can sparse human-provided correspondences guide feed-forward 3D reconstruction models without sacrificing the efficiency of the feed-forward paradigm? Instead of relying solely on feed-forward models or automatically estimated correspondences, we allow the user to manually provide a small set of cross-view point correspondences (Fig. 1 a),
when the model produces unsatisfactory reconstructions. To unlock this capability, we introduce Click3R , a lightweight framework that incorporates user-provided correspondences into feed-forward 3D reconstruction, enabling interactive geometric correction without test-time model fine-tuning or optimization .
Specifically, Click3R employs a simple correspondence adapter that injects the geometric constraints imposed by user clicks into the reconstruction network via a cross-attention mechanism (Fig. 2 ).
We train the base model and the adapter jointly end-to-end on widely used 3D datasets [ 28 , 2 , 57 ] using simulated human clicks.
During training, the number of clicks is randomly sampled to expose the model to varying levels of user interaction, enabling it to operate under different levels of click sparsity at test time. To systematically evaluate the proposed interactive reconstruction, we further introduce Hard3DBench , a benchmark designed to expose failure modes of state-of-the-art feed-forward reconstruction models.
The benchmark contains image pairs and 5-view tuples with ground-truth 3D reconstructions, exhibiting challenging conditions (large viewpoint changes, low overlap, large baselines, extreme depth ranges) where feed-forward reconstruction models often struggle.
Our experiments demonstrate that Click3R consistently outperforms baseline methods, particularly on challenging scenarios from Hard3DBench (Fig. 1 c). Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose Click3R , which injects sparse human correspondence clicks into feed-forward stereo 3D reconstruction networks via a lightweight correspondence adapter. • We curate Hard3DBench, a diagnostic benchmark with a detailed failure taxonomy under which current feed-forward reconstruction models degrade substantially relative to standard evaluation samples. • We show that Click3R dramatically improves reconstruction accuracy on challenging cases.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work on interactive stereo feed-forward 3D reconstruction using sparse correspondence clicks. 2 Related Work Feed-Forward Stereo 3D Reconstruction. DUSt3R [ 44 ] pioneered dense 3D pointmap regression from image pairs using a CroCo-pretrained [ 48 , 47 ] ViT encoder-decoder, bypassing explicit matching and triangulation.
MASt3R [ 16 ] extended this with dense local feature heads.
The paradigm has rapidly advanced toward multi-view: Spann3R [ 41 ] and MUSt3R [ 3 ] use spatial memory across frames; CUT3R [ 43 ] employs a recurrent model with persistent state; Fast3R [ 53 ] scales to 1000+ images in a single forward pass; and VGGT [ 42 ] and Pi3 [ 45 ] predict from arbitrary views with a single Transformer.
Despite their diversity, all these methods rely solely on learned visual priors and cannot incorporate external geometric evidence at inference.
Our work is orthogonal: Click3R augments feed-forward stereo reconstruction with sparse correspondence guidance. Conditioned 3D Reconstruction. Several concurrent methods condition feed-forward 3D models on external geometric signals: Pow3R [ 10 ] on camera intrinsics, depth, or pose, G-CUT3R [ 14 ] on camera and depth modalities, Rig3R [ 17 ] on multi-camera rig metadata, and Mono3R [ 18 ] on monocular depth.
MapAnything [ 13 ] unifies a flexible mix of conditioning inputs (intrinsics, poses, depth, partial reconstructions) into a single feed-forward model, while Any4D [ 12 ] extends conditional reconstruction to dynamic 4D scenes with multi-modal sensor inputs.
In the depth estimation domain, PromptDA [ 21 ] prompts Depth Anything [ 54 ] with sparse LiDAR points.
All these methods condition on scene-level priors that improve metric accuracy but cannot resolve perceptual aliasing.
To our knowledge, Click3R is the first to introduce correspondence prompting , which conditions feed-forward 3D reconstruction on cross-view point correspondences that directly disambiguate geometric ambiguities, analogous to SAM’s [ 15 , 27 ] click-based segmentation prompting. Interactive Vision System. Interactive segmentation has a long history of incorporating user guidance into visual recognition.
Early methods use bounding boxes [ 30 ] or scribbles [ 20 ] , while recent approaches adopt click-based prompting [ 51 , 4 , 23 , 39 ] , culminating in SAM [ 15 , 27 ] .
Beyond segmentation, point-based interaction has been extended to image editing: DragGAN [ 26 ] and DragDiffusion [ 37 ] enable users to manipulate content by dragging points.
Recent work has also explored interactive guidance for 3D reconstruction; for example, Toon3D [ 46 ] uses sparse user annotations for cartoon 3D reconstruction.
However, these interactive paradigms have largely remained confined to 2D tasks or stylized 3D settings.
Early interactive systems also explored correspondence-based 3D reconstruction [ 9 ] . Click3R brings this idea to feed-forward stereo models, with point correspondences as geometric constraints rather than semantic cues. 3 Methodology Figure 2 : Click3R architecture. A shared ViT encoder processes the two input images. Correspondence prompts are tokenized using our correspondence tokenizer ( Section 3.2 ). A symmetric point correspondence adapter, implemented as a lightweight transformer with self-attention and cross-attention ( Section 3.3 ), updates the decoder-input features for each view. The asymmetric decoder then interprets the updated embeddings and passes them to the original DUSt3R [ 44 ] heads to predict 3D pointmaps and confidence. The tokenizer is shared across both view adapters. 3.3 Point Correspondence Adapter We inject correspondence information into DUSt3R’s decoder using a two-way attention adapter inspired by SAM [ 15 ] , enabling bidirectional exchange between sparse correspondence tokens and dense image tokens ( Figure 2 ). Two-Way Attention. The adapter has L = 2 L=2 layers. Each layer applies: (1) self-attention over prompt tokens 𝐓 v \mathbf{T}_{v} ; (2) prompt-to-image cross-attention, Attn ​ ( 𝐓 v , 𝐆 v , 𝐆 v ) \text{Attn}(\mathbf{T}_{v},\mathbf{G}_{v},\mathbf{G}_{v}) ; (3) an MLP on prompt tokens; and (4) image-to-prompt cross-attention, Attn ​ ( 𝐆 v , 𝐓 v , 𝐓 v ) \text{Attn}(\mathbf{G}_{v},\mathbf{T}_{v},\mathbf{T}_{v}) . This bidirectional design lets prompts condition image features with correspondence constraints while allowing prompt tokens to absorb local visual context. Image Positional Encoding. For cross-attention, image positional encodings are computed by applying the same Fourier PE to normalized DUSt3R token-grid positions. See more training and prompt generation details in B . 4 Hard3DBench The primary value of Click3R is on challenging scenarios where DUSt3R fails.
Standard evaluations report only aggregate metrics, masking catastrophic failures on geometrically ambiguous scenes.
To enable systematic analysis, we introduce Hard3DBench , a curated benchmark for both 2-view stereo and 5-view multi-view settings, accompanied by a failure taxonomy that exposes the specific failure modes of feed-forward 3D reconstruction.
The 2-view setting contains 404 in-distribution and 300 out-of-distribution pairs, and the 5-view setting 400 in-distribution and 300 out-of-distribution tuples.
In-distribution samples come from held-out test/validation splits of four datasets used to train DUSt3R and Click3R : BlendedMVS [ 56 ] , CO3D [ 28 ] , Habitat [ 32 ] , and WildRGB-D [ 50 ] .
Out-of-distribution samples come from five datasets absent from training: 7Scenes [ 38 ] , DTU [ 11 ] , ETH3D [ 35 ] , NRGBD [ 1 ] , and ScanNet [ 6 ] .
Full curation details are in Appendix C . Failure Taxonomy. We identify five primary failure modes in the 2-view subset ( Figure 3 ): large viewpoint change ( > 60 ∘ >60^{\circ} ), moderate viewpoint change ( 30 ∘ 30^{\circ} – 60 ∘ 60^{\circ} ), low overlap ( < 20 % <20\% ), large baseline , and extreme depth range . These categories capture geometric conditions that make correspondence estimation and pose recovery difficult, such as reduced visual matches, amplified triangulation error, and increased ambiguity. We compare against state-of-the-art pairwise 3D reconstruction methods: (1) DUSt3R [ 44 ] , the original pretrained model; (2) DUSt3R (re-trained), the same architecture re-trained on the same data as Click3R to isolate the effect of prompting from additional training data; (3) MASt3R [ 16 ] , which extends DUSt3R with dense local feature matching heads; (4) VGGT [ 42 ] , a feed-forward vision foundation model for 3D geometry tasks; and (5) Pi3/Pi3X [ 45 ] , a permutation-equivariant multi-view reconstruction model.
VGGT and Pi3/Pi3X use larger architectures and are trained on much more data, including proprietary internal datasets, with much higher computational budgets. We therefore include them as reference rather than apples-to-apples baselines. Datasets. For standard evaluation, we use held-out splits of three benchmarks: CO3D [ 28 ] , ScanNet++ [ 57 ] , and ARKitScenes [ 2 ] . For each dataset, we sample 1,000 image pairs with a fixed random seed and use the same pairs across all methods. We additionally evaluate on the Hard3DBench benchmark ( Section 5.3 ). Metrics. Following DUSt3R [ 44 ] , we report two metrics: scale-and-shift-invariant pointmap error for 3D reconstruction quality (lower is better) and mean Average Accuracy at threshold θ ∘ \theta^{\circ} (mAA@ θ \theta , higher is better) for relative camera pose accuracy. Tables report mAA@30°; results at 5° and 15° are in Appendix E.2 . Implementation details are in Appendix A . Prompt selection strategies. We evaluate several prompt selection strategies. (a) Random oracle ( K K p_best B B ): sample K K correspondences B B independent times and select the set that minimizes reconstruction loss. If there’s no best B B mentioned, K K correspondences are sampled randomly. (b) Grid oracle : partition view 1 into a 16 × 16 16\times 16 grid of 14-pixel cells ( ∼ \sim 256 cells), select one random correspondence per populated cell, run a batched forward pass for each candidate, and keep the single correspondence whose cell yields the greatest pose improvement (reduction in max ⁡ ( R err , T err ) \max(R_{\mathrm{err}},T_{\mathrm{err}}) ) over the no-prompt baseline. Samples without valid correspondences fall back to the no-prompt result. 5.2 Evaluation on Standard Benchmark Table 1 presents results on standard benchmarks. Without prompts, Click3R performs on par with DUSt3R (retrained). Adding even a single well-chosen prompt (1p_best5) consistently improves over the unprompted baseline. The grid oracle reveals substantially larger headroom, with a striking + 8.5 % +8.5\% mAA@30° on ARKitScenes (from 84.27% to 92.75%). This confirms that prompt location matters significantly : the model can leverage even a single correspondence to meaningfully reduce pose error when placed in the right region. Additional qualitative results are provided in Appendix Figure 13 . 5.4 Evaluation with Human-Labeled Correspondences The previous experiments use ground-truth correspondences derived from known 3D geometry. To evaluate Click3R under realistic annotation conditions, we test on 100 image pairs with human-labeled correspondences. The pairs are drawn from out-of-distribution set of Hard3DBench and cover challenging geometric configurations. A human annotator, not specifically familiar with the evaluated datasets or scenes, freely chooses sparse correspondences (3.8 per pair on average, from 1 to 7) via a labeling interface (Figure 12 ), without access to ground-truth depth or camera parameters. Table 3 demonstrates that human-provided prompts drastically reduce median rotation error compared to the unprompted model (8.78° vs. 17.30°) and raise mAA@30 from 36.2 to 56.2, along with significant improvement in Relative Rotation Accuracy. Compared to the random ground-truth oracle ( K = 4 K\!=\!4 ), human labels recover about 96% of the medRRE improvement gap, showing that sparse, imperfect human clicks capture most of the benefit of perfect correspondences. These results confirm that Click3R translates effectively from synthetic oracle prompts to practical human interaction. We compare three training strategies for Click3R : (i) freezing the DUSt3R backbone and training only the adapter, (ii) finetuning DUSt3R jointly with the adapter, and (iii) training the full pipeline from CroCo v2 [ 47 ] initialization. Averaged over the three standard benchmarks and both Hard3DBench 2-view splits with one GT click (Appendix E.1 ), training from CroCo v2 performs best (mAA@30 75.1%), followed by DUSt3R finetuning (72.3%), while freezing DUSt3R gives the weakest results (71.2%). This suggests that Click3R requires substantial end-to-end adaptation to align backbone features with correspondence-conditioned reasoning; training the adapter alone is insufficient. Correspondence perturbation analysis. To study how the model uses sparse correspondence hints, we perturb a single correspondence by fixing the click in view 1 and shifting its matched point in view 2 along a random direction. As perturbation increases, model confidence consistently decreases, indicating sensitivity to correspondence reliability (Fig. 5(a) ). We further measure whether predictions follow the true or perturbed correspondence by computing the 3D distance between predicted points at the ground-truth correspondence locations in both views. The model remains largely robust to small perturbations, but becomes more sensitive once perturbations reach half a patch (8 pixels), leading to larger degradation (Fig. 5(b) ). For more analysis on how correspondences affects the adapters and downstream tasks, see Appendix F . (b) 3D distance between the predicted points at the ground-truth correspondence (ARKitScenes): the model remains robust under small perturbations, but larger perturbations degrade matching performance. 6 Conclusion We presented Click3R , an interactive stereo 3D reconstruction framework that bridges fully automatic feed-forward methods, correspondence-based classical pipelines, and recent prompt-based vision systems.
By injecting sparse point correspondences into a feed-forward stereo 3D reconstruction model through a lightweight correspondence adapter, Click3R enables users to guide the reconstruction process without test-time model fine-tuning or optimization, resolving geometric ambiguities that often cause catastrophic failures.
Experiments on our curated Hard3DBench demonstrate that even a handful of correspondences can dramatically improve reconstruction quality in challenging scenarios.
While Click3R has limitations (Appendix J ) and failure cases (Appendix H ), we hope this work inspires further exploration of correspondence prompting as a promising direction for interactive 3D reconstruction, analogous to how SAM [ 15 ] advanced interactive prompting for 2D segmentation. Appendix A Implementation Details Click3R follows the DUSt3R [ 44 ] training recipe (loss function, optimizer, data augmentation, symmetric batching) and inherits its CroCo v2 [ 47 ] ViT-Large encoder and dual asymmetric decoder.
Below we detail the components specific to Click3R . Correspondence adapter. We insert two SAM-style [ 15 ] Two-Way Transformer adapters (depth 2, 8 heads, MLP dim 2048, embedding dim 768) between the encoder and decoder ( Section 3.3 ).
The two adapters share a single correspondence tokenizer ( Section 3.2 ) that encodes each correspondence point as the sum of a positional encoding (random Fourier features [ 15 ] ), an image identity embedding, and a pair identity embedding (vocabulary size 256).
When prompts are provided, the adapted features replace the encoder output; otherwise, the adapter is bypassed and the model reduces to standard DUSt3R.
The adapters and tokenizer are randomly initialized; all other weights are initialized from the CroCo pretrained checkpoint.
All parameters are trained jointly end-to-end. Training data. We train on nine datasets, each contributing 100,000 image pairs:
BlendedMVS [ 56 ] ,
MegaDepth [ 19 ] ,
CO3D [ 28 ] ,
StaticThings3D [ 36 ] ,
Waymo Open [ 52 ] ,
ARKitScenes [ 2 ] ,
WildRGB-D [ 50 ] ,
Habitat [ 32 ] , and
ScanNet++ [ 57 ] .
Validation uses held-out splits from six of these datasets with 1,000 pairs each. Ground-truth prompt generation. During training, correspondence prompts are generated by projecting view 1’s 3D pointmap into view 2 and accepting correspondences where the 3D distance to the nearest view 2 surface point is below 5 cm (which implicitly rejects occluded points).
Per sample, 30% receive zero prompts (adapter bypassed), 40% receive 1–4, and 30% receive 5–12 correspondences. Training configuration. We train for 100 epochs on 4 GPUs with per-GPU batch size 16, using AdamW ( β 1 = 0.9 \beta_{1}{=}0.9 , β 2 = 0.95 \beta_{2}{=}0.95 , weight decay 0.05 0.05 ), a learning rate of 10 − 4 10^{-4} with 10 epochs of linear warmup followed by cosine decay to 10 − 6 10^{-6} , and float16 mixed precision. Baseline image preprocessing. DUSt3R-family models (DUSt3R, MASt3R, Click3R ) use images normalized to [ − 1 , 1 ] [-1,1] . The VGGT and Pi3 wrappers internally denormalize images to [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] before forwarding to match each model’s expected input range. Standard benchmark data. The held-out splits are the CO3D test sequences, the ScanNet++ validation scenes, and the ARKitScenes validation videos, each processed with the DUSt3R pipeline. Evaluation metric details. The scale-and-shift-invariant pointmap error uses the normalization of DUSt3R: we divide the predicted pointmap by its mean point distance, shift both pointmaps by their median depth, and rescale the prediction to the median distance of the ground-truth points to their median point. We then average the per-pixel L2 distance over all valid pixels (i.e., L2 within each 3D point, mean across pixels).
For mAA@ θ \theta , relative camera pose is first estimated from each model’s predicted pointmaps via PnP-RANSAC ( cv2.solvePnPRansac with the SQPNP solver), recovering per-view camera-to-world poses.

During training, we generate prompts from ground-truth 3D geometry.
For each image pair with known cameras and pointmaps, we first sample the number of correspondence prompts according to a curriculum: 30% of samples use no prompts ( K = 0 K=0 ), 40% use a small number of prompts ( K ∈ [ 1 , 4 ] K\in[1,4] ), and 30% use more prompts ( K ∈ [ 5 , 12 ] K\in[5,12] ). Given the sampled value of K K , we:
(1) sample K K 3D points from the first view;
(2) project them into both views using known intrinsics and extrinsics;
(3) retain only points that are mutually visible, verified by comparing the projected 3D position against the second view’s GT pointmap within a distance threshold. Training Protocol. We initialize from CroCo v2 [ 47 ] pretrained weights and train all parameters end-to-end, including both the base model and the adapter.
Training uses 4 GPUs, AdamW ( lr = 10 − 4 \text{lr}\!=\!10^{-4} ) with linear warmup and cosine decay.
We train on the same dataset mixture as DUSt3R: CO3D [ 28 ] , ARKitScenes [ 2 ] , ScanNet++ [ 57 ] , WildRGB-D [ 50 ] , BlendedMVS [ 56 ] , MegaDepth [ 19 ] , Habitat [ 32 ] , Waymo [ 52 ] , and StaticThings3D [ 36 ] (100K pairs each), at 224 × 224 224\times 224 resolution.
With batch size 16 per GPU on A100s, a training step takes 0.60 s for Click3R and 0.55 s for DUSt3R (retrained), which amounts to about 234 and 215 hours for 100 epochs on 4 GPUs, with a peak memory of 29,069 and 28,731 MiB per GPU. Appendix C Hard3DBench Details Hard3DBench has two subsets: a 2-view stereo subset of 404 in-distribution and 300 out-of-distribution pairs, and a 5-view multi-view subset of 400 in-distribution and 300 out-of-distribution tuples.
Both subsets use pretrained DUSt3R’s pose error as the difficulty signal, rather than Click3R or our retrained baseline, so the benchmark is defined independently of the methods under evaluation.
They share the same source datasets: the in-distribution split uses held-out test/val splits of four DUSt3R training datasets, while the out-of-distribution split uses five datasets entirely absent from training ( Table 6 reports the 2-view per-dataset statistics).
DTU uses the cameras of MVSNet [ 55 ] and the depth maps rendered by Spann3R [ 41 ] , 7Scenes depth is registered to the color camera as in SimpleRecon [ 33 ] , ScanNet is prepared following MonST3R [ 58 ] , and ETH3D following Pi3 [ 45 ] . 2-view subset. To obtain hard cases, we evaluate the pretrained DUSt3R model on up to 20,000 randomly drawn image pairs per dataset ( ∼ {\sim} 69K in-distribution, ∼ {\sim} 68K out-of-distribution), pre-filtering pairs with insufficient co-visibility via depth reprojection in DUSt3R’s × 224 224\!\times\!224 crop space.
We rank pairs by rotation error and select the top- N N worst per dataset ( N = 1,000 N\!=\!1{,}000 for BlendedMVS, CO3D, WildRGB-D, DTU, and ETH3D; N = 300 N\!=\!300 for 7Scenes, NRGBD, and ScanNet). For Habitat, only the 406 of its top 1,000 pairs from scenes not used in training are kept. This yields 3,406 in-distribution and 2,900 out-of-distribution candidates.
Each candidate is then classified into one or more geometric failure categories ( Table 5 ) from ground-truth poses and depth.
We uniformly subsample 404 in-distribution and 300 out-of-distribution pairs with a fixed seed. 5-view subset. For each scene, we sample up to 30 5-frame tuples by combining stride-based windows (strides 1, 2, 5, 10, 20) with random index sampling and deduplication; tuples are kept only if all five views form a connected graph under pairwise depth-reprojection overlap ≥ 5 % \geq 5\% .
We then run DUSt3R on all C ⁡ ( 5 , 2 ) = 10 C(5,2){=}10 symmetrized pairs and compose them via the standard global alignment optimizer (300 iterations, msp initialization), yielding ∼ 16 ​ K {\sim}16\text{K} in-distribution and ∼ 4 ​ K {\sim}4\text{K} out-of-distribution candidate tuples.
We rank tuples by mean pairwise rotation error and select the top-100 per in-distribution dataset (400 total) and top-60 per out-of-distribution dataset (300 total). Difficulty for recent models. Compared with random pairs from the same datasets with the same per-dataset counts, VGGT and Pi3 at 518 resolution drop in RRA@5 from 82.6% and 85.1% to 54.7% and 57.9% on the in-distribution pairs of the 2-view subset, and from 98.2% and 97.0% to 88.7% and 81.3% on the out-of-distribution pairs, confirming that Hard3DBench remains challenging for more recent 3D reconstruction models. Partly due to this cost, our experiments focus on two-view reconstruction; extending to multi-view models [ 45 , 42 , 53 , 40 ] with trajectory prompts across N N views is an important direction. Architecturally, the adapter treats correspondences as soft feature conditioning via cross-attention rather than enforcing hard geometric constraints. Although eight correspondences suffice to determine relative pose algebraically [ 8 ] , the adapter cannot replicate such reasoning: it modulates all image tokens through learned attention at a single injection point, and this signal is progressively diluted across twelve subsequent decoder cross-attention blocks. Deeper injection at multiple decoder layers and explicit geometric reasoning modules may be worth future inquiry to help close this gap. Finally, due to computational constraints, the model is trained only at a resolution of 224 224 px in a stereo setting, although it has demonstrated multi-view capability through global alignment. In addition, prompts must be co-visible in both views, so the two inputs need a shared visible region; non-overlapping inputs could be connected through intermediate views by chaining correspondences. Severely incorrect correspondences can degrade reconstruction quality ( Table 11 ). Appendix K Broader Impacts Our work enables more efficient 3D reconstruction from sparse user-provided correspondences. By allowing a small number of clicks to anchor pairwise reconstruction, Click3R can lower the barrier to 3D capture in diverse, in-the-wild scenes, which may benefit downstream applications such as robotics, AR/VR content creation, and scientific imaging where dense ground-truth geometry is unavailable. Our model and benchmark are built from publicly available datasets (CO3D, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes, BlendedMVS, MegaDepth, Habitat, WildRGB-D, Waymo, and standard evaluation benchmarks), and therefore do not raise privacy concerns. While our algorithm itself is scene-agnostic and does not introduce explicit bias, the underlying training datasets may reflect societal or geographic biases present in the source data (e.g., predominantly indoor scenes from North American buildings, or object categories skewed toward consumer products).
