Yet visual plausibility alone does not constitute successful world modeling: compelling outputs may lack persistent state, physical dynamics, or causally consistent responses to interactions. The absence of a shared operational basis, together with perception-centric benchmarks and fragmented workflows, obscures the distinction between generative foundation models and generative world models and hinders reproducible assessment of dynamics, controllability, and interactive utility. We define a generative world model as a predictive dynamical system that maintains a persistent, history-conditioned representation of an environment, updates that representation in response to actions or interventions, and generates temporally coherent trajectories that reflect outcomes under observed and counterfactual interventions. From this definition, we derive an intelligence-oriented framework spanning perceptual grounding, persistent state modeling, controllable transformation, dynamics-aware prediction, and closed-loop interaction. We further present WorldFoundry , open-source infrastructure for reproducible inference and evaluation across video, 3D and 4D, embodied action, and interactive world systems. WorldFoundry unifies model and benchmark discovery, runtime and asset preparation, execution orchestration, artifact management, and interactive visualization, while modality-specific adapters preserve native inputs, execution semantics, output representations, and evaluation protocols. Explicit manifests, durable artifact contracts, and evidence-aware scorecards separate execution readiness from evaluative claims, making each claim traceable to its supporting artifacts. The current catalog contains 270 model entries, 74 benchmarks, and 62 registered metric entries, with integration readiness and execution evidence recorded separately for each entry. By coupling world modeling with evidence-grounded executable infrastructure, WorldFoundry enables reproducible comparison of persistent, controllable dynamics across heterogeneous systems, advancing the field from perceptual synthesis toward measurable world intelligence. Recent advances in generative modeling are moving beyond synthesizing individual objects or scenes toward modeling worlds that evolve over time and respond to interventions. This shift calls for a broader notion of world intelligence , in which systems must perceive observations, infer structured states, predict their evolution, express resulting consequences through task-specific outputs, and exploit these predictions for interaction [ worldmodels , planet , dreamerv3 , vjepa2 ] . Video generators, neural scene representations, and embodied policies address complementary parts of this problem. However, incompatible representations, runtimes, interfaces, and evaluation protocols make capabilities difficult to compose, results difficult to reproduce, and progress toward genuine world models difficult to measure. The bottleneck is therefore not only algorithmic, but also infrastructural. Recent efforts such as OpenWorldLib [ openworldlib ] address complementary aspects of this integration problem. Progress therefore requires shared infrastructure grounded in a common functional abstraction that makes heterogeneous models composable, reproducible, and comparable across modalities while preserving their native semantics. To establish common ground without constraining how different systems represent the world, we formulate the abstraction at the level of function. Building on insights from earlier definitions in reinforcement learning [ worldmodels , planet , dreamerv3 ] and recent efforts to delineate their scope [ openworldlib ] , we identify three tightly coupled components. State inference estimates a latent environmental state from the observation history; forward simulation predicts its evolution under endogenous dynamics and external interventions; and interactive response produces distinct, action-conditioned futures as visual, geometric, or task-specific outputs. This decomposition is functional rather than architectural and defines a shared interface, not a requirement that every system implement all three operations. Static 3D methods emphasize persistent-state inference and geometry decoding, video models primarily learn implicit transitions and observation generation, 4D methods explicitly represent time-varying structure, and embodied models connect state estimation and action prediction to decision-making. Characterizing systems by the operations they implement and the capability profiles they support provides a common vocabulary for comparing them. These operations alone, however, describe what a system does without determining whether it qualifies as a world model. Qualification is decided not by any single operation but by the state that persists across all of them: we define a generative world model as a learned, history- and control-conditioned dynamical system that infers and maintains a persistent environmental state, evolves that state under endogenous dynamics and external interventions, and assimilates observational feedback into coherent future trajectories. Persistence in this sense is operational rather than nominal: an intervention must leave a trace in the state that constrains later predictions, and each incoming observation must in turn revise that state. Only when both hold is the world-modeling loop closed. Visual plausibility, by contrast, reflects only the observation decoder; it is neither necessary nor sufficient evidence that the underlying state persists, a gap that recent physics- and world-oriented benchmarks repeatedly expose [ vbench2 , videophy2 , worldmodelbench , worldarena ] . It is closure, not plausibility, that draws a principled boundary between foundational generative models , which synthesize or transform observations without closing the loop, and generative world models , which couple generation to an evolving environmental state through closed-loop updates. Measuring progress from foundational generation toward world intelligence therefore calls for evidence of closure, which in turn depends on shared infrastructure for reproducible execution and comprehensive, fair evaluation. We realize this infrastructure in WorldFoundry , an open-source system for integrating, executing, and evaluating video, 3D, 4D, interactive world, and embodied-action models. WorldFoundry spans the research lifecycle, from model and benchmark discovery and runtime and asset preparation to execution orchestration, artifact management, evaluation, and interactive visualization. Modality-specific adapters preserve native execution and evaluation semantics; declarative manifests specify dependencies, assets, and interfaces; durable artifact contracts decouple execution from evaluation; and evidence-aware scorecards separate declared support, operational readiness, and validated performance. The current release integrates 270 models and over 100 benchmark and metric implementations, with every model runner validated through end-to-end execution of a documented reference workflow. By combining our theory of world modeling with the proposed infrastructure, WorldFoundry transforms isolated generative demonstrations into cumulative, auditable evidence and advances the field from perceptual synthesis toward measurable world intelligence. Our key contributions can be summarized as follows: • World Model Definition. We formally define world models through a minimal abstraction comprising state inference, forward simulation, and interactive response. We then characterize the core capabilities of perception, modeling, manipulation, dynamics, and interaction within a structured functional framework, thereby organizing and differentiating existing generative and world-modeling systems. • Unified Inference and Evaluation Infrastructure. We introduce WorldFoundry, an open-source infrastructure that standardizes execution and evaluation across video, 3D, 4D, embodied-action, and interactive world systems, making inference reproducible and configurable without overriding native model semantics, and placing heterogeneous systems under a common operational definition of world modeling. • Validation at Scale. We integrate 270 generative models and over 100 benchmark and metric implementations.
