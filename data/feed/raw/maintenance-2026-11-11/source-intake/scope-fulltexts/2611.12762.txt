Through explicit monitoring and control, the system revises its understanding of evidence, assumptions, and unresolved questions, then uses these assessments to adapt research targets and select reusable reasoning methods. On Taiyi-Bench, an AI co-scientist ideation benchmark comprising 265 tasks from 50 papers across ten disciplines, Sage-Taiyi ranks first on all eight metrics of historical rediscovery and open-ended ideation, achieving an Overall score of 54.0 compared with 47.9 for the strongest baseline, AI Scientist-v2. It also performs well on two external scientific ideation benchmarks. Five expert-guided collaborations further connect its proposals to experimental and computational validation across chemistry, materials science, and engineering. In chemistry, reaction-condition optimization raises the yield of cobalt-catalyzed carbonylation from trace amounts to 84% and photocatalytic allylic amination from 13% to 69%. These results support metacognitive regulation as an approach to scientific ideation and its progression toward practical validation. Homepage Contents 1 Introduction As Carl Sagan observed, “Science is a way of thinking much more than it is a body of knowledge.” Scientific discovery involves more than producing new hypotheses or accumulating findings. It unfolds through an iterative loop: scientific search generates and tests ideas, scientific understanding develops through the interpretation of the resulting findings, and this understanding guides subsequent search [ 1 ] . Researchers move within and between hypothesis and experiment spaces around a scientific question, proposing explanations, designing tests, and interpreting experimental, computational, or observational feedback [ 2 ] . Interpreting this feedback develops a working understanding of the problem: which judgments are supported, which conflicts remain unresolved, under which conditions findings apply, and which regularities may transfer across cases. As understanding changes, researchers revise hypotheses, reformulate questions, redesign experiments, and reassess which direction merits further effort. Classical problem-solving research describes how representations, operators, and heuristics organize this search and guide subsequent action [ 3 , 4 ] . The central design question is how to retain the understanding developed during search and use it to select and adapt representations, operators, and heuristics, so that search experience informs subsequent research decisions. Recent AI systems have extended one-shot idea generation into iterative scientific workflows. The AI Scientist connects ideation with experiments, analysis, and paper writing [ 5 ] ; Co-Scientist organizes hypothesis generation, critique, and improvement [ 6 ] ; and Robin combines multi-agent reasoning with experimental feedback [ 7 ] . Iteration alone, however, does not ensure that findings from different branches are retained with their evidence and applicability conditions, or that these findings affect later method selection. When iteration depends mainly on feedback from the current round, earlier failures, unresolved conflicts, and shared dependencies can be overlooked, leading to repeated errors or further search along unproductive paths. Search can also become overly responsive to recent outcomes, delaying recognition of key uncertainties or a change in targets, representations, or reasoning methods. A scientific agent therefore needs to preserve understanding across the search and use it to regulate what to investigate next and how to investigate it. This need motivates drawing on metacognition, a concept rooted in the study of human cognition. Often described as “thinking about thinking,” metacognition involves monitoring, and regulation of one’s own cognitive processes [ 8 ] . Nelson and Narens distinguish an object level that performs a task from a meta level that maintains a dynamic model of the object-level cognition and regulates its process through monitoring and control [ 9 ] . Prior work examines how artificial systems can monitor and adjust their own problem-solving processes [ 10 ] , complementary research on strategy selection motivates matching reasoning methods to the task and current state [ 11 ] . Viewed through the lens of metacognition, object-level search develops scientific understanding, while the meta level selectively represents this understanding and assesses its implications for further search, guiding decisions about which premises to examine, representations to revise, methods to apply, or branches to stop. We adopt this functional distinction as architectural inspiration for explicit search regulation. We introduce Sage-Taiyi, a metacognition-inspired architecture that uses accumulated scientific understanding to guide what to investigate next and how to investigate it. Through explicit monitoring and control, the system revises its understanding of evidence, assumptions, and unresolved questions, then uses these assessments to adapt research targets and select reusable reasoning methods. Sage-Taiyi achieves leading performance on Taiyi-Bench, AgentIdeaBench, and IG-Arena. On Taiyi-Bench, an AI co-scientist ideation benchmark comprising 265 tasks from 50 papers across ten disciplines, it ranks first on all eight metrics of historical rediscovery and open-ended ideation, achieving an Overall score of 54.0 compared with 47.9 for the strongest baseline, AI Scientist-v2. Five expert-guided collaborations further connect its proposals to experimental and computational validation across chemistry, materials science, and engineering. In chemistry, reaction-condition optimization raises the yield of cobalt-catalyzed carbonylation from trace amounts to 84% and photocatalytic allylic amination from 13% to 69%. These results support metacognitive regulation as an approach to scientific ideation and its progression toward practical validation. 2 Method Sage-Taiyi is a metacognition-inspired architecture for scientific discovery in which the search for ideas and the understanding of the scientific problem develop together. Section 2.1 defines the object and meta levels and how they interact. At the object level, scientific search constructs and transforms ideas across target branches (Section 2.2 ). At the meta level, a Cognitive State selectively represents object-level scientific understanding and records monitoring assessments, while a Cognitive Repertoire retains reusable methods (Section 2.3 ). Monitoring and control connect the two levels (Section 2.4 ), and a research harness links ideas to planning, tool use, and external validation (Section 2.5 ). 2.1 System Overview Sage-Taiyi organizes scientific discovery into two levels, drawing on accounts of discovery as problem solving [ 3 , 12 ] and of metacognition as monitoring and control of thinking [ 13 , 14 ] . Monitoring uses these inputs to update the Cognitive State, assessing evidential support, uncertainty, shared dependencies, and their implications for search. A task ends with reviewed ideas or executable plans, or continues to execution and validation, whose results return to the Evidence State (Figure 2 ). 2.2 Object-Level Scientific Search 2.2.1 Target Tree: Decomposing the Research Question The Target Tree decomposes the research question into more specific targets and subtargets. Its root is q q , and each node is a target or subtarget that records its scientific requirements and completion criteria. Under a methodological heuristic selected by control, a target is decomposed into subtargets; each subtarget is paired with local strategies for addressing it, and each strategy yields subideas, partial solutions that can later be assembled into complete ideas. This structure follows problem-space accounts in which a problem is reduced to subproblems that are solved by applying operators [ 3 , 12 ] . The tree is not fixed at the outset: as ideas develop, subtargets are added or revised while the root question and its constraints are retained (Section 2.2.2 ). 2.2.2 Idea Graph: Developing Ideas across Branches Ideas are the units that Sage-Taiyi evaluates, transforms, and outputs. An idea is a complete proposal specifying a mechanism or design, supporting references, constraints, testable consequences, and the uncertainties to be examined. The Idea Graph records ideas as nodes and their transformations as edges. Ideas enter the graph by assembling subideas from the Target Tree or by direct generation from the research question, with or without a selected heuristic. Because the graph spans target branches, ideas developed for different targets can be compared and recombined. Five transformations, informed by population-based search and conceptual combination [ 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 ] , produce new ideas. Merge consolidates ideas that take similar approaches; Fuse combines complementary mechanisms from different ideas after checking that their assumptions and interfaces are compatible; Diverge keeps an idea and opens a substantively different branch from it; Repair fixes a defective dependency or a missing interface; and Evolve improves an idea while retaining its core mechanism. Over successive iterations, these transformations can refine ideas and combine strengths developed for different targets. As ideas develop, they can expose requirements that no current target covers, motivating new subtargets in the Target Tree. To keep this development traceable, each new idea is linked to its parents and to the evaluation that motivated it. 2.2.3 Evidence State: Grounding Scientific Understanding The Evidence State grounds scientific search in external information. It records experimental and computational results, literature knowledge, and expert input, linking each to its source, relevant ideas, and conditions. Using this evidence, the object level forms scientific judgments, for example which mechanisms are supported, under which conditions findings hold, and which explanations conflict. Each judgment retains its evidential grounds, allowing new evidence to inform revisions to relevant judgments and their dependencies while preserving unaffected alternatives. Judgments formed in one branch can also inform reasoning in others. Integrated across branches, these evidence-grounded judgments and their relations form the system’s current scientific understanding of the problem [ 19 , 20 ] . 2.3 Meta-Level Representations and Resources While the object level develops targets and ideas, the meta level keeps track of how this search is proceeding and adjusts its course. The meta level regulates scientific search with two resources. The Cognitive State represents the current search and is revised by monitoring as new findings or assessments warrant an update. The Cognitive Repertoire retains reusable method knowledge: methodological heuristics, which organize research paths, and Cognitive Operators, which transform knowledge states. 2.3.1 Cognitive State: Representing the Current Search Following Nelson and Narens’ account of the meta level as maintaining a dynamic model of object-level cognition [ 9 ] , the Cognitive State models the current search. It contains two kinds of content. The first is a selective, source-linked representation of object-level scientific understanding, retaining key findings, competing explanations, necessary assumptions, applicability conditions, and unresolved questions. The second consists of monitoring-derived assessments of the evidential status of key judgments, unresolved conflicts and uncertainties, and dependencies shared across ideas. The former selectively represents what the system currently understands about the scientific problem; the latter assesses how this understanding should guide further search. For example, when several ideas propose different mechanisms but depend on the same untested premise, monitoring records that they share a potential failure condition and that their agreement does not provide independent support. The two kinds of content describe functions of the state rather than separate storage modules. Monitoring revises both as new evidence and evaluations of ideas emerge (Section 2.4.1 ), preserving sources and keeping working hypotheses distinct from external evidence. Control uses the revised state to select methods from the repertoire and determine subsequent research actions; their application updates targets and ideas (Section 2.4.2 ). 2.3.2 Methodological Heuristics: Organizing Research Paths Methodological heuristics organize complete research workflows. Since the space of possible research paths is too large to search exhaustively, scientists in different disciplines develop characteristic research paradigms that lead them to workable solutions more efficiently [ 3 , 4 , 21 , 22 ] . Drawing on these accounts, Sage-Taiyi organizes related reasoning principles into ten methodological heuristics for reusable research workflows (Figure 3 and Table 1 ). Each heuristic specifies when it applies, how it decomposes a target into subtargets and local strategies, coordinates cognitive operators, and evaluates the resulting ideas. Figure 3 : Methodological heuristics. The ten heuristics organize recurring strategies for scientific research grounded in evidence, models, analogies, representations, goals, and practical constraints. These general heuristics are further instantiated with discovery methods summarized from the literature of specific research domains (Section 2.3.4 ). Control selects heuristics according to the research question and the current Cognitive State [ 11 ] , and the selected heuristics guide the construction of the Target Tree (Section 2.2.1 ). Backward reasoning infers conditions or subgoals from an outcome; inverse design also constructs and validates a design. Mechanism integration aligns existing mechanisms’ interfaces and assumptions to form a candidate; compositional synthesis also decomposes the target and evaluates the system. Operators act on targets in the Target Tree, ideas and evaluations in the Idea Graph, representations and assessments in the Cognitive State, and methods in the Cognitive Repertoire. Informed by process accounts of creative thinking [ 35 , 36 ] , we organize operators by their principal function: Framing specifies targets and their requirements; Constructing develops and transforms ideas; Evaluating examines their evidential support and testable consequences; and Learning uses search outcomes to revise the Cognitive State and refine reusable methodological knowledge in the Cognitive Repertoire. These categories allow overlap, and an operator may act on multiple structures. Each operator specifies applicability conditions, a reasoning procedure, and expected outputs; control selects applicable operators using the task and Cognitive State. Figure 5 summarizes their development through domain instantiation and feedback learning. Domain instantiation analyzes classic and representative papers to map general reasoning procedures to domain objects, mechanisms, constraints, and validation requirements. This mapping preserves relational structures while grounding their application in disciplinary knowledge and experimental conditions [ 24 , 34 ] . For example, active hypothesis testing becomes a concrete chemistry procedure when two mechanisms predict different sources for a product atom: isotope-labeling one reactant and examining the product provides a test of their predictions. Entries retain their sources and applicability conditions, distinguishing reported procedures from analytical reconstruction. Figure 5 : Domain instantiation and feedback learning. General heuristics and operators are specialized using domain literature. Task feedback refines domain entries; comparisons across cases and disciplines yield reusable knowledge that revises the general repertoire. Transferred experience retains its sources and scope and is re-examined in each new domain. Feedback learning uses successes, failures, and validation outcomes to refine these entries, drawing inspiration from case-based learning through reuse, testing, revision, and retention [ 37 ] . Comparisons across cases and disciplines identify recurring reasoning patterns that inform revisions to general heuristics and operators, following the principle of schema induction from concrete examples [ 19 ] . Revised methods can then be instantiated in further domains, with their operating conditions re-examined during transfer, closing the loop. 2.4 Regulating Search through Monitoring and Control Inspired by the monitoring–control framework of Nelson and Narens [ 9 ] and its extension to reasoning and problem solving [ 13 ] , Sage-Taiyi uses monitoring to update the Cognitive State from search progress and feedback, and control to decide what to investigate next and how. The resulting decisions guide object-level search and, through the research harness, execution and validation. 2.4.1 Monitoring When targets, ideas, evaluations, or evidence change, monitoring reads the current Cognitive State together with relevant object-level judgments, evaluations, and evidence. It updates the selective representation of scientific understanding and assesses what the changes imply for subsequent search, such as whether current support is sufficient or further work would repeat an unresolved limitation. This provides ongoing verification of evidential support and assumptions, with the assessments recorded in the Cognitive State. Applicable operators from the repertoire support these updates: C t + 1 = Monitor ⁡ ( q , X t , C t , ℛ ) . C_{t+1}=\operatorname{Monitor}(q,X_{t},C_{t};\mathcal{R}). Control therefore selects Assumption and boundary audit and Discriminative prediction and calls for a subtarget to examine the premise. Object-level search makes the premise explicit and derives predictions that distinguish whether it holds, providing a basis for validation. Validation results enter the Evidence State and return to monitoring, which revises the state to inform subsequent decisions. It refines the selected task into an executable plan with specified inputs, constraints, and evaluation methods, and selects appropriate tools and skills (Figure 6 ). During ideation, the Target Tree and Idea Graph guide subagents to explore multiple promising reasoning paths in parallel. Returned results are linked to relevant targets, ideas, and conditions in the Evidence State. Monitoring uses this evidence to verify intermediate conclusions and update the Cognitive State, which then guides control over subsequent search. This combination of parallel exploration and ongoing verification aligns with the Agentic Orchestration and Agentic Verification capabilities of Sage-A1.5 [ 38 ] . In our research collaborations, this loop connects Sage-Taiyi’s candidate solutions and search formulations to experiments and simulations, with domain experts participating in idea selection and validation. Tools such as Taiyi-Optimizer support searches within the specified condition or design spaces, while measurements and simulation results inform subsequent research decisions. Physical experiments and expert participation follow the corresponding research workflow. 3 Experiments We study Sage-Taiyi through benchmark comparisons and real-world research applications. Taiyi-Bench assesses its scientific ideation capabilities as an AI co-scientist at different knowledge states, through historical rediscovery and open-ended evaluation of idea quality and diversity (Section 3.1 ). We also evaluate ideation quality and the use of prior research on AgentIdeaBench and IG-Arena (Section 3.2 ). We then present five applications in metal catalysis, photochemistry, metamaterials, alloy design, and compressor engineering , showing how Sage-Taiyi helps address practical scientific and engineering problems (Section 3.3 ). 3.1 Evaluating Scientific Ideation with Taiyi-Bench 3.1.1 Experiment Setup Taiyi-Bench. Unsymmetrical ketones are widespread in pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals and functional materials, and carbonylation with carbon monoxide offers an atom-economical route to assemble them from two different carbon fragments and a C1 unit. The central challenge is chemoselectivity, because the two fragments must be joined at the same carbonyl carbon in a cross-selective manner while the formation of symmetrical ketones is suppressed. Earth-abundant metal catalysts would improve the practicality of this approach, but achieving high cross-selectivity with such catalysts remains difficult when both carbon partners are activated through similar pathways at the same metal centre. For this scientific question, Sage-Taiyi proposed several activation strategies (Figure 13 a). One of them adapts the synchronous recognition strategy previously reported for the synthesis of unsymmetrical ureas [ 53 ] , in which the two coupling partners are differentiated by distinct activation modes. Sage-Taiyi proposed that metal-mediated carbonylation and radical carbonylation could operate cooperatively at a cobalt centre to achieve this differentiation in ketone synthesis. In the metal-mediated branch, a carbon nucleophile such as an organometallic reagent attacks a CO-ligated Co(III) centre, forming an acyl–cobalt intermediate through nucleophilic carbonylation. In the radical branch, a carbon radical generated from the second coupling partner couples with this intermediate to deliver the unsymmetrical ketone. To access the carbon radical required in the radical branch, Sage-Taiyi further proposed a polarity-reversal strategy , in which an electron-deficient radical adds to an alkene to generate a nucleophilic alkyl radical. A subsequent literature analysis by Sage-Taiyi showed that 1,3-enynes, which form C(sp 2 )-centred allenyl radicals upon radical addition, have rarely been employed as radical acceptors in oxidative carbonylation. On this basis, Sage-Taiyi recommended a cobalt-catalysed redox-neutral carbonylation in which organozinc reagents serve as nucleophiles, 1,3-enynes as radical acceptors and electron-deficient halides as radical precursors, affording allenyl ketones (Figure 12 ). This case illustrates how Sage-Taiyi narrows a general activation strategy to a specific, underexplored reaction design through successive rounds of literature comparison. Literature searching and mechanistic reasoning mapped the proposed activation model onto an alkylzinc reagent, an allenyl-radical pathway, a cobalt catalyst and CO, and identified the catalyst, organozinc reagent, ligand, solvent and temperature as the variables for condition generation. Sage-Taiyi proposed five routes addressing different aerodynamic and mechanical tradeoffs. The first combined a boreless Ti–6Al–4V backswept splitter impeller with a hybrid diffuser, targeting an efficiency greater than 0.76 while retaining aerodynamic and mechanical margins. The second combined a high-solidity impeller with Z = 18 Z=18 blades, a larger vaneless diffuser gap, and a boreless architecture to prioritize slip and structural margin. The third combined stronger backsweep and high solidity to reduce impeller-exit absolute Mach number and improve matching to a vaned diffuser. The fourth used inlet prewhirl, a wide-range vaneless or long-gap diffuser, and collector-gap injection to broaden the stable operating range, with tradeoffs in peak efficiency and mechanical margins. The fifth reduced the impeller outlet tip speed, U 2 U_{2} , to reduce centrifugal loading and fatigue risk, while accounting for diffuser matching and surge margin. Experts selected the first route for further development. Initial design considerations covered outlet diameter and width, inlet shroud diameter, blade angles, and main-blade count. Across the five collaborations, Sage-Taiyi develops scientific ideas and executable search formulations, while the research harness connects them to expert selection, optimization, and experimental or simulation-based evaluation. The resulting feedback guides subsequent research decisions, linking scientific exploration to practical advances in reactions, materials, and engineering designs. 4 Related Work AI Scientists. AI research agents connect literature review, hypothesis generation, and experimentation in workflows that span computational research [ 54 , 47 , 55 , 56 ] and laboratory discovery [ 7 , 57 , 58 , 59 , 60 ] . These systems refine hypotheses or intermediate results through critique [ 6 , 61 ] , carry findings forward through persistent memory and shared representations [ 46 , 62 , 63 , 64 , 65 ] , and use execution results or surrogate models fitted to experimental observations to guide further search [ 16 , 66 , 67 ] .
Work focused on scientific ideation integrates evidence from prior research, retrieves and recombines concepts, and checks proposals against existing work [ 68 , 69 , 70 , 71 , 72 ] . Search methods further develop promising ideas by exploring the literature, tracking evolving idea lineages, or progressively specifying experimental details [ 73 , 74 , 75 ] . Evaluations examine complementary aspects of this process, including the breadth of exploration [ 76 ] , how experts assess ideas before and after implementation [ 77 , 78 ] , and how new proposals build on prior research [ 50 ] .
Sage-Taiyi focuses on how scientific judgments formed along one branch can inform decisions elsewhere in the search. Its revisable Cognitive State retains these judgments together with their supporting evidence and the conditions under which they apply, allowing the agent to revisit them when choosing research targets and methods. Cognitive Science-Inspired Agent Design. Classical research describes problem solving as search over representations and operators [ 3 ] ; metacognition concerns how this process is monitored and controlled [ 9 , 10 ] . CoALA organizes memory, internal and external actions, and decision cycles into a cognitive architecture for language agents [ 79 ] . Generative Agents uses experience retrieval and reflection to support planning [ 80 ] . Reflexion retains task feedback as verbal reflections to improve subsequent attempts [ 81 ] .
Recent work also studies which reasoning methods agents use and how they select them. Cognitive Foundations for Reasoning and Their Manifestation in LLMs analyzes reasoning in terms of cognitive elements, including metacognitive controls, knowledge representations, and transformation operations, and finds that models underuse some elements associated with successful reasoning [ 82 ] . Chain of Mindset uses a meta-agent to select spatial, convergent, divergent, or algorithmic reasoning modes according to the evolving reasoning state [ 83 ] .
Together with cognitive-science research on creative thinking, metacognition, and scientific reasoning [ 35 , 36 , 24 , 19 , 8 , 9 , 13 , 11 , 14 , 1 , 12 , 21 ] , these approaches inform the design of Sage-Taiyi’s Cognitive Repertoire and state-dependent method selection. Sage-Taiyi maintains methodological heuristics and Cognitive Operators in a Cognitive Repertoire and instantiates them for research domains. Monitoring updates the Cognitive State from search outcomes; control uses that state and the repertoire to select targets and methods, revise the Target Tree, and guide idea evolution in the Idea Graph. 5 Conclusion We presented Sage-Taiyi, a metacognition-inspired architecture that uses accumulated scientific understanding to regulate scientific search. Through monitoring and control, a revisable Cognitive State and a reusable Cognitive Repertoire guide target selection, idea development, and validation. On Taiyi-Bench, Sage-Taiyi leads all eight metrics, achieving an Overall score of 54.0 compared with 47.9 for the strongest baseline. Five expert-guided collaborations further illustrate how its ideas and search formulations connect to experimental and computational validation across chemistry, materials science, and engineering. Future work will refine the Cognitive Repertoire through accumulated research experience and examine its reuse across domains. Building on this repertoire, Sage-Taiyi will scale multi-agent collaboration to explore a broader range of high-value solutions within limited time and iteration budgets, aiming to improve the efficiency and quality of scientific discovery.
