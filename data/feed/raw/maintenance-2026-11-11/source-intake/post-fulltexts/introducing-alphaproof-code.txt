How we verified Signal AlphaProof Code is a multi-LLM agentic framework that splits the work of formal verification the way a research team would: human experts work with AI to decide what to prove, and a group of AI agents does it. First, our researchers collaborated with a mix of models to translate Signalâs security goals into formal mathematical specifications, with human experts reviewing every one. From there, agents powered by Gemini Carbon did the proofs, generating over six million lines of Lean, a computer language that mathematically proves the accuracy of code. Because Lean automatically checks the rest, our experts could focus their review where human judgment matters most. Cryptographic protocols underpin nearly all digital communication, from online banking to web browsing, and a single flaw can expose millions of people at once. Because real-world vulnerabilities often hide in the gap between design and implementation, bridging this gap makes these proofs vastly more complex. To confirm that AlphaProof Code can tell correct code from buggy code, we reintroduced two real, previously fixed bugs into an isolated version of Signal's code. In AlphaProof Code, AI agents help users write a specification, then autonomously prove that the implementation meets it. Every proof is checked by Lean to verify the agents' work.
