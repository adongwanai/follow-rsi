To help Japan meet this change, Sakana AI today announces that Admiral Michael Rogers, U.S. Navy (Ret.), former Director of the National Security Agency (NSA) and Commander of U.S. Cyber Command, and Tom Moran, a former senior officer of the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA), have both joined as Senior Advisors. They will advise the company on two fronts: strengthening defense capabilities and strengthening cybersecurity. Our two advisors have each stood on the front lines of different domains of intelligence. Admiral Michael Rogers, U.S. Navy (Ret.) Michael Rogers spent a 37-year career in the U.S. Navy on the front lines of military operations, intelligence, and cybersecurity. Specializing in cryptology, he held a series of senior intelligence posts, including Director for Intelligence (J2) at U.S. Indo-Pacific Command (INDOPACOM), and went on to serve as Commander of U.S. Fleet Cyber Command / U.S. Tenth Fleet, among other posts, before serving simultaneously as Director of the NSA and Commander of U.S. Cyber Command from 2014 to 2018. He led both the largest intelligence agency in the United States and the unified command responsible for defending U.S. military networks and conducting cyber operations worldwide, making him an expert with an unparalleled command of the cyber domain. After retiring in 2018 as a four-star admiral, he has served since 2019 as Senior Advisor to the Brunswick Group, advising on cybersecurity, geopolitics, and crisis management. “AI is fundamentally changing the assumptions of national security, on both offense and defense in cyberspace. Throughout my career, I have seen how much risk emerges when institutions fail to keep pace with advances in technology. Sakana AI combines world-class research with a distinctive approach that originates in Japan. I look forward to contributing my experience to strengthening cybersecurity and defense.” — Admiral Michael Rogers, U.S. Navy (Ret.) Tom Moran, Former CIA Chief of Station Tom Moran spent close to 30 years at the CIA, on the front lines of intelligence operations in the field around the world. He served six times as Chief of Station across East Asia, Western Europe, the Middle East, and Africa, and held senior leadership positions at headquarters in Washington, D.C. A member of the Senior Intelligence Service, the CIA’s senior executive cadre, he was awarded the Distinguished Career Intelligence Medal in 2023. Since 2024, he has also been passing on his security expertise to the next generation as Director of the Intelligence Project in the Institute for Security and Governance at UC Berkeley’s Goldman School of Public Policy. “What matters most in intelligence work is reaching the right judgment from an enormous amount of information in a limited amount of time. AI has the potential to dramatically strengthen that ability. At the same time, reliability and responsible use are essential.
