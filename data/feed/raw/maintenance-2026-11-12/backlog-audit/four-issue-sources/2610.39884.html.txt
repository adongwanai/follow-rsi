Industrial advertising agents must continually adapt their decision knowledge to changing product offerings, traffic patterns, and operating policies. We present KARMA ( K nowledge- A tomic, R egression-Gated M odular A daptation), a training-free framework that evolves six typed knowledge modules while keeping model weights and executable harness code frozen. During our evolution, a deterministic Trace Verifier maps observed errors to candidate modules, and a Critic compares successful and failed trajectories to guide a tool-constrained Evolver. The Evolver applies atomic knowledge edits, which a fixed validation gate retains only when their measured gain reaches a threshold; otherwise, the previous knowledge state is restored. We evaluate KARMA with three LLM families on cases sampled from a corpus of approximately half a million real-world operation logs across six e-commerce domains. Across 18 domain–model settings, KARMA raises the average hold-out score from 30.19 to 39.15 (+8.96 points, +29.7%). Ablations show that removing either successful or failed trajectories more than halves the average hold-out gain. Evolved knowledge also transfers across domains without further adaptation, yielding an average hold-out gain of 8.54 points across all source–target pairs. By linking retained patches to their diagnostic evidence and validation outcomes, KARMA supports inspectable, reversible knowledge adaptation for agents in Web marketplaces. Introduction Web marketplaces allocate merchant spending and product exposure through advertising systems. Merchants must decide which products to promote, how much budget to allocate, and how aggressively to bid under changing demand and competition. LLM agents can retrieve business evidence and combine it with operating knowledge ( Cai et al., 2025 ) , but their recommendations depend on rules that may become incomplete or outdated. Updating those rules can change both the selected control (budget, bid, or item) and the direction of intervention. The research challenge is to improve this decision knowledge while making changes inspectable and screening for regressions across marketplace contexts. We study this challenge through an advertising agent that queries telemetry from a global e-commerce platform and emits structured decisions in an offline evaluation. Figure 1 . Motivation. Whole-prompt rewriting obscures individual changes; broad harness editing complicates validation and rollback. The left panel shows a monolithic system prompt containing role, domain, and constraints, with the warning that opaque rewrites hinder attribution and risk regressions. The middle panel shows an agent harness containing code, tools, and orchestration, with the warning that broad code changes complicate validation and rollback. The right panel shows KARMA: six typed knowledge modules, atomic knowledge edits derived from failures and successes, and a validation step that accepts an update if gains are at least theta and otherwise rolls it back. Repeated fine-tuning is costly, while manual prompt maintenance is difficult to scale as products and policies change. Whole-prompt rewriting can entangle changes to domain knowledge, decision heuristics, and output instructions. Broad harness-code editing provides more flexibility but also changes the tools or control flow that must be validated (Figure 1 ). This motivates a narrower adaptation object: external knowledge that can be revised while the executable decision process remains fixed. We therefore ask: can an industrial advertising agent improve its decision knowledge from evaluated trajectories without changing model weights or executable harness code? Existing self-improving agents revise prompts, memories, workflows, or harnesses, mostly in coding and related environments ( Agrawal et al., 2026 ; Lin et al., 2026a ; Zhang et al., 2026b ; Lee et al., 2026a ; Chen et al., 2026 ; Zhang et al., 2026c ; Esakkiraja et al., 2026 ) . Advertising introduces three considerations for this process. First, a wrong action type differs from a wrong direction within the correct type, so feedback should preserve this structure. Second, interacting rules can overgeneralize: a repair that helps failed cases may disrupt correct decisions. Third, tool and protocol failures must be distinguished from semantic errors before proposing knowledge changes. These considerations motivate structured diagnosis, evidence from both successes and failures, and an outcome-based acceptance gate. We introduce KARMA ( K nowledge- A tomic, R egression-Gated M odular A daptation), a training-free framework that externalizes knowledge into six typed modules and restricts adaptation to schema-checked, journaled mutations. Rule modules preserve stable identifiers and provenance, allowing an edit to be inspected alongside the evidence that motivated it. The full knowledge context remains available during inference, while model weights, business tools, and executable code stay fixed. Each edit has an explicit target, and validation measures its effect on agent decisions. A deterministic Trace Verifier maps observed failures to candidate knowledge modules and retains successful trajectories as context for repair. A Critic compares these evidence channels, and a tool-constrained Evolver translates suitable proposals into a small patch. The outer loop retains a patch only if its gain on a fixed validation set reaches θ \theta , and otherwise restores the full knowledge snapshot. An untouched hold-out set measures final generalization. Each cycle records the diagnostic evidence, knowledge changes, and validation outcome. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • To our knowledge, we present the first training-free harness self-evolution framework designed and evaluated for an industrial advertising decision agent. • We make typed, rule-addressable knowledge the object of evolution. Schema-checked atomic edits, provenance, mutation journals, and full-directory snapshots support inspection and rollback while preserving the executable harness. • We combine deterministic component attribution with full success/failure ingestion: failures guide repair, while successes provide evidence for preserving effective behavior. • Across six domains and three LLM families, KARMA improves the average hold-out score by 8.96 points (+29.7%). Ablations, engine comparisons, and cross-domain transfers examine the design and its generalization. Figure 2 . KARMA overview. Related Work 2.1. Training-Free Agent Adaptation Tool-using agents combine language-model reasoning with external observations ( Yao et al., 2022b ; Schick et al., 2023 ) . Reflexion and Self-Refine use verbal feedback to improve subsequent attempts or iteratively revise outputs without additional weight updates ( Shinn et al., 2023 ; Madaan et al., 2023 ) . Prompt optimizers update instructions using task feedback: ProTeGi combines textual critiques with beam search ( Pryzant et al., 2023 ) , OPRO generates candidates from previously evaluated solutions ( Yang et al., 2024 ) , and GEPA combines reflective prompt updates through Pareto selection ( Agrawal et al., 2026 ) . Persistent memories and skills offer another adaptation mechanism. ExpeL extracts reusable insights from successful and failed experiences ( Zhao et al., 2024 ) , while Voyager accumulates executable skills through environment feedback and self-verification ( Wang et al., 2023 ) . ACE maintains structured context playbooks through generation, reflection, and curation, using incremental updates to preserve accumulated knowledge ( Zhang et al., 2026a ) . RHO revisits challenging past tasks and selects harness updates through pairwise self-preference without labeled validation data ( Pan et al., 2026 ) . RSIAgent constructs environment-specific memory through curriculum, actor, and verifier roles ( Zhu et al., 2026 ) , while MetaSkill-Evolve adapts both task skills and the procedure that evolves them ( Wang et al., 2026 ) . However, linking feedback to specific knowledge components and preserving effective behavior during revision remain challenging when decision rules interact. 2.2. Harness Evolution and Repair Beyond individual prompts, DSPy optimizes modular language-model pipelines against task metrics ( Khattab et al., 2024 ) , and TextGrad propagates textual feedback through computation graphs ( Yuksekgonul et al., 2025 ) . ADAS uses a meta agent to search code-defined agent designs ( Hu et al., 2025 ) ; AFlow searches executable workflows using Monte Carlo Tree Search and execution feedback ( Zhang et al., 2025 ) . Harness evolution extends adaptation to the runtime surrounding a frozen model. AHE exposes editable components, organizes execution evidence, and checks predicted edit outcomes in subsequent iterations ( Lin et al., 2026a ) . Self-Harness mines failure patterns, proposes minimal modifications, and accepts them after regression testing ( Zhang et al., 2026b ) . Meta-Harness searches executable harness programs using the code, scores, and traces of prior candidates ( Lee et al., 2026a ) ; RHI instead refines prompt-level specifications of the agent loop through pairwise feedback over revision history ( Lee et al., 2026b ) . Several methods explicitly address diagnosis and generalization. HarnessCompass combines constrained edits, proactive agent feedback, and component-wise optimization ( Zhang et al., 2026c ) . StarHarness stratifies evolution tasks and separates proposer-visible search from hidden selection tasks ( Esakkiraja et al., 2026 ) . HarnessFix aligns traces with implementation artifacts to guide scoped repairs and regression-aware validation ( Chen et al., 2026 ) . HarnessBank maintains diverse candidate harnesses in a gene bank and screens offspring through gated verification ( Luo et al., 2026 ) . Related analyses distinguish the ability to generate useful harness updates from the ability to benefit from them during execution ( Lin et al., 2026b ) . Editing executable harnesses expands the search space and the dependencies that must be validated. Even accepted changes may generalize unevenly across tasks, making evaluation beyond the optimization setting essential. 2.3. Commerce and Advertising Agents Commerce agents span product search, customer interaction, and advertising decisions. WebShop evaluates grounded shopping agents that navigate product pages to satisfy user instructions ( Yao et al., 2022a ) . In advertising, RTBAgent combines click-through-rate estimates, expert strategies, memory retrieval, and daily reflection to inform bidding decisions ( Cai et al., 2025 ) . HAT addresses changing harness configurations for live-commerce digital avatars through harness-state augmentation, supervised fine-tuning, distillation, and reinforcement learning ( TaoLive et al., 2026 ) . These studies focus on specific interaction or bidding tasks; evidence on reusable knowledge updates spanning budget, bid, and item decisions across product domains remains limited. 3. Method 4.1. System Overview Figure 2 separates frozen decision execution from knowledge evolution. The inner agent renders current knowledge into its context and response schema, retrieves evidence, and emits a scored decision. The outer loop verifies trajectories, proposes and applies atomic edits, and retains them only after validation. Diagnosis, editing, and acceptance have distinct roles: the Critic proposes changes, the Evolver operates within the knowledge interface, and the deterministic outer loop controls validation and restoration. Accepted knowledge serves the next training batch. 4.2. Frozen Decision Agent The context builder renders knowledge into a prompt π K \pi_{K} and response schema 𝒴 K \mathcal{Y}_{K} that remain fixed within each trajectory: (5) ( π K , 𝒴 K ) = Render ⁡ ( K ) . (\pi_{K},\mathcal{Y}_{K})=\mathrm{Render}(K). A bounded two-stage protocol first collects evidence through white- listed read-only business tools, then requires a standalone finish_ analysis call before structured output. Each trace records the knowledge fingerprint, tool queries and observations, decision, termination reason, and protocol-error codes P P . Evidence identifiers E E must refer to successful tool responses, and the decision must conform to 𝒴 K \mathcal{Y}_{K} . Violations trigger bounded corrective feedback. The trace links the output to the knowledge version and observations used during inference, supporting subsequent inspection of semantic and protocol errors. No advertising action is executed; tool and protocol details appear in Appendix A . 4.3. Experiments The experiments address five questions: RQ1 , whether knowledge evolution improves held-out decisions across domains and models; RQ2 , whether both evidence channels contribute; RQ3 , how proposal engines compare under a shared gate; RQ4 , whether knowledge transfers across domains; and RQ5 , how gated evolution relates to hold-out generalization. 5.1. Experimental Setup 5.1.1. Dataset We sample 800 cases per domain from approximately half a million July 2026 operation logs of verified merchants on a global e-commerce platform (Table 2 ). With seed 42, each domain has 480 training cases in six batches of 80, 160 validation cases, and 160 untouched hold-out cases. Logged actions provide behavioral supervision for action selection. The data source and industrial relevance of this study are further discussed in Appendix F . 5.1.2. Models and Baselines We evaluate Qwen3.8-Max, GLM-5.3, and Kimi-K3, using the same family for the inner agent, Critic, and Evolver. Each main setting is run once from shared initial knowledge K 0 K_{0} , with θ = 0.01 \theta=0.01 and at most three mutations per cycle. Baselines replace the evolution engine under the same protocol (Section 5.4 ); implementation details are in Appendix A . 5.1.3. Metrics We report average action-agreement scores (Equation 3 ), absolute gains in points, and relative gains in percent. Scores and absolute gains are scaled by 100; θ = 0.01 \theta=0.01 is one point. Hold-out gains compare K T K_{T} with K 0 K_{0} on the same cases. Ablations and engine comparisons use the main-run Base as their common reference; aggregate relative gains use the corresponding mean scores. 5.2. The improvement spans all model families and multiple domains, supporting the usefulness of external knowledge evolution under a fixed executable harness. Automotive Anomaly The small aggregate changes on 𝒜 \mathcal{A} conceal improvements and regressions across operation types (Figure 3 ). Stable rule identifiers make potential interactions easier to inspect. Gate Dynamics Figure 4 shows stepwise improvements separated by plateaus. Rejected patches leave the active knowledge and cached score unchanged, so these curves track the validation scores of retained knowledge states. Useful updates occur at different stages across models and domains: some runs improve early, while others gain after longer plateaus. Appendix D illustrates accepted and rejected cycles, and Appendix C.2 examines alternative thresholds. Validation and hold-out improvements are broadly consistent (Table 3 ), but agreement is weaker on 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . The gate selects patches using observed validation scores and does not guarantee hold-out improvement. Final evaluation on H H provides a separate assessment of generalization. 6. Limitations and Future Work Logged actions may encode historical policy biases; agreement does not establish gains in revenue, welfare, or fairness. KARMA is a research system evaluated offline without executing actions. Future work will consider deployment with business guardrails, monitoring, human oversight, and prospective evaluation. Experiments use access controls and anonymized reporting; direct merchant and campaign identifiers are not released. Deterministic attribution may miss complex interactions, and repeated selection on a fixed validation set can favor changes that do not carry over to hold-out or transfer evaluation. Each main configuration is run once; additional seed, threshold, and comparison studies cover selected settings. Generalization beyond the sampled cases, period, and platform remains open. The framework addresses external knowledge adaptation and cannot repair faulty tools or orchestration code. 7. Conclusion We presented KARMA, a training-free framework for evolving typed, rule-addressable knowledge in advertising agents. Deterministic verification, success/failure reflection, constrained mutation, and validation-gated rollback link retained updates to diagnostic evidence and measured outcomes. Across six domains and three model families, KARMA raises the average hold-out score from 30.19 to 39.15 (+29.7%). Ablations support using both evidence channels, while cross-domain transfer yields an average gain of 8.54 points. KARMA supports inspectable, reversible knowledge adaptation while keeping model weights and executable harness code fixed. References Agrawal et al . Critic and Evolver Guidance The fixed Critic instructions ask for reusable decision criteria supported by the batch evidence. Each candidate identifies a failure mode and a reflection perspective, explains the diagnosis, proposes a change, names a target module, and states why the proposal should apply beyond the observed cases. The four perspectives focus on missing knowledge, conflicting rules, protocol timing, and ambiguous wording. The Critic is instructed to compare failures with successful cases and to omit candidates when the evidence offers no useful revision. The Evolver is instructed to inspect the relevant modules before editing and to assess each proposal against the existing knowledge. It may adopt a proposal, adapt it, or reject it with an explanation. Its guidance favors reusable evidence requirements and decision priorities, excludes case-specific identifiers from rule text, and calls for checking knowledge validity after changes. Tool feedback reports invalid arguments or schema violations so that the Evolver can revise its requests. The tool interface enforces the permitted operations and mutation budget; the outer loop determines whether the resulting patch is retained. A.4. Engine Implementations GEPA reflects on the verifier report and retains a candidate only if it outperforms its parent on eight training cases. AHE requires failure evidence and a predicted effect for each edit, then checks that prediction in the next cycle. Self-Harness proposes a minimal edit for the largest failure cluster and discards it if a second LLM call predicts changes to correctly handled cases. All use the same knowledge interface and outer gate. Token accounting includes engine calls and inference on the 80 training and 160 validation cases of a full cycle; runtime includes final hold-out evaluation. A.5. Appendix E Discussion The evidence ablations and engine comparisons examine two design choices: which trajectories inform an update and how proposals are generated. Full ingestion performs best among the evaluated evidence variants, and proposal engines produce different outcomes under a common acceptance rule. Transfer evaluation then measures how the retained knowledge performs in other product domains. These comparisons connect the design of the update process to its observed effectiveness and generalization. For Web advertising, this separation offers a way to maintain operating knowledge across heterogeneous product categories while preserving the surrounding executable system. Transfer can make a learned rule set useful beyond its source, but the outcomes involving 𝒜 \mathcal{A} show why reuse still requires evaluation in the target setting. Versioned rules and mutation records provide a basis for inspecting both improvements and regressions as those policies evolve. Evaluation remains offline and measures agreement with logged actions. Experiments use access-controlled data and anonymized reporting. Deployment with monitoring and human oversight is a direction for future work; broader limitations and governance considerations appear in Section 6 . Appendix F Industry Relevance We submit this work as an Industry Paper because it addresses the practical maintenance of advertising-agent knowledge using real merchant data from AliExpress. Our dataset comprises historical merchant operation logs across six e-commerce domains, covering budget, bid, and advertised-item decisions. These records ground the task and evaluation in actual marketplace operations. KARMA enables operating knowledge to be revised without retraining the model or changing executable code, while traceable edits, validation, and rollback support inspection and controlled updates. The experiments provide offline evidence of improved action agreement and cross-domain knowledge reuse in this industrial setting. Future work will explore deployment with business guardrails, monitoring, and human oversight.
