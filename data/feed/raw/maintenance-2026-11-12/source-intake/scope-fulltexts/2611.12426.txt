Extending this paradigm to vision allows visual and language models to converge on a unified framework, thereby fostering shared methodology and infrastructure. This work investigates a central question: how far can a finite discrete vocabulary go in representing the richness of the visual world? We present dNaViT, a Discrete Native-resolution Vision Transformer that produces discrete visual primitives analogous to language tokens. Built upon a frozen pretrained encoder, dNaViT applies lightweight residual modulation with a dual-branch objective to inject reconstruction-aware cues into semantic features. By leveraging grouped product quantization, it scales discrete capacity without inflating spatial sequence lengths, while a balanced training recipe maintains both semantic and pixel-level information. Consequently, dNaViT functions as a versatile discrete visual tokenizer, enabling unified encoding across downstream tasks alongside high-fidelity image decoding. Extensive evaluations show that dNaViT matches or exceeds task-specific baselines across 12 visual understanding benchmarks, 4 generation benchmarks, and an image editing benchmark. These findings demonstrate that a compact discrete vocabulary can serve as a universal visual currency and unlock the full potential of fully unified, native multimodal methodology. From the perspective of compression as intelligence ( Delétang et al., 2023 ) , discrete tokens provide a unified substrate for representation learning and autoregressive modeling. In contrast, modern multimodal models frequently rely on modality-specific representations and heterogeneous learning objectives ( Caffagni et al., 2024 ) . This paradigm divergence disconnects vision from the established techniques and scalable infrastructure to language, raising a foundational question: can visual information be represented losslessly like language using a learned discrete vocabulary? Discrete visual representations offer a promising bridge, naturally aligning visual inputs with autoregressive language architectures under a unified token-prediction objective ( Xie et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2024b ; Zou et al., 2026b ; Peng et al., 2026 ; Meituan LongCat Team et al., 2026 ; Cui et al., 2025 ) . However, discretizing dense, continuous visual signals into a finite vocabulary presents a severe information bottleneck ( Ma et al., 2025 ; Yu et al., 2026 ) that hinders performance. Crucially, harmonizing high-level semantic abstraction for understanding with fine-grained pixel details for high-fidelity reconstruction within a single discrete bottleneck remains an unresolved tension ( Wu et al., 2024 ; Qu et al., 2025 ) . To answer the question posed above and address these challenges, we introduce dNaViT , a Discrete Native-resolution Vision Transformer that extends our preliminary work in LongCat-Next ( Meituan LongCat Team et al., 2026 ) . Following the representation encoder paradigm ( Zheng et al., 2025 ) , dNaViT enriches the semantic features of a frozen pretrained encoder with pixel details through a Residual Modulator (ResMod), without compromising its underlying semantic capability. The resulting features are discretized into a shared vocabulary using Grouped Product Quantization (PQ) ( Jégou et al., 2011 ) under dual-branch representation and pixel reconstruction loss. Training progresses through continuous initialization, discrete feature alignment, and final joint optimization, which balance theses objectives and suppress gradient interference. These design choices yield a shared discrete interface for tasks that conventionally rely on separate visual encodings, extending the utility of pretrained representations beyond their original objectives. By rotating and translating pretrained features, residual modulation reorganizes their geometry to accommodate reconstruction-relevant information, making more effective use of the available representational capacity. Combined with sufficient factorized code capacity, this allows semantic structure and fine-grained detail to coexist within a shared discrete bottleneck, mitigating information loss during quantization. The resulting tokenizer brings the flexibility of text tokenization to vision: images can be encoded into discrete sequences and decoded into faithful reconstructions, while the codes retain semantic information closely aligned with language. We evaluate the generality of dNaViT across four core capabilities: image reconstruction, classification, multimodal understanding, generation, editing. Driven by a single shared discrete vocabulary with a spatial downsampling factor f = 32 f=32 , dNaViT achieves 28.12 ​ dB 28.12\text{\penalty\ dB} PSNR and 0.81 0.81 SSIM at 512 × 512 512\times 512 resolution without sacrificing semantic representation capability. When paired with Qwen3-8B, dNaViT surpasses prior discrete baselines and rivals Qwen3-VL-8B across key document and reasoning benchmarks, scoring 885 885 on OCRBench. For image generation and editing, dNaViT achieve impressive performance on GenEval2 ( 49.5 49.5 ) and outperforms open-weight baselines on Qwen-Image-Bench ( 52.2 52.2 ), while excelling at bilingual text rendering. Crucially, dNaViT establishes these comprehensive understanding and generation capabilities under a substantially reduced data budget relative to existing models. These findings suggest that the gap between continuous visual signals and discrete tokens is a challenge of representation learning rather than an inherent limitation. With appropriate modeling, discrete codes can serve as general-purpose visual primitives, bringing vision and language into a shared token-based paradigm. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: 1. This work explores the question of whether discrete codes can serve as general-purpose visual primitives , analogous to the role of tokens in language modeling. To investigate this possibility, we introduce dNaViT, a native-resolution visual tokenizer designed to capture both high-level semantic structure and fine-grained pixel information within a shared vocabulary for different tasks. 2. dNaViT uses residual modulation to aggregate multi-level features from a frozen vision encoder and grouped product quantization to encode the enriched representations. Progressive training coordinates dual-branch semantic and pixel reconstruction objectives. Experiments demonstrate that this design balances semantic preservation and reconstruction fidelity within the discrete bottleneck. 3. Comprehensive evaluations across visual understanding, image reconstruction, generation, and editing demonstrate dNaViT’s generality. With a substantially smaller training data budget than the compared models, dNaViT achieves strong understanding and generation performance, highlighting the effectiveness and data efficiency of its unified, language-aligned representation. 4. This work demonstrates that a shared discrete token space can serve as a universal visual currency , providing a general-purpose intermediate representation across complementary tasks and a foundation for fully unified, native multimodal modeling. We will describe how the architecture supports these capabilities while mitigating conflicts between semantic abstraction and fine-grained detail preservation, as well as information loss during discretization. 2.1 Background Visual modeling has largely relied on distinct continuous representations for different downstream tasks: pretrained semantic features for multimodal understanding ( Tschannen et al., 2025 ) and reconstruction-oriented latents for diffusion-based synthesis ( Rombach et al., 2022 ) . Representation autoencoders bridge these approaches by pairing pretrained semantic encoders with reconstruction decoders ( Zheng et al., 2025 ) . By contrast, language models use discrete tokens as a uniform representation of text, supporting a unified, scalable next-token prediction framework. Meanwhile, recent benchmarks ( An et al., 2026a ; Yan et al., 2026 ) highlight the growing need to integrate linguistic and visual intelligence to solve real-world tasks. Extending this framework to vision is challenging: unlike the discrete symbols of language, visual signals encode dense, continuous variation. Discrete visual tokenization addresses this by mapping images to sequences of codebook indices. For example, vector-quantized autoencoders assign continuous features to learned codebook entries and reconstruct images from the corresponding embeddings ( van den Oord et al., 2017 ) . These indices can be embedded and predicted like text tokens, enabling a shared autoregressive interface and the reuse of language-model architectures and objectives. Chameleon ( Chameleon Team, 2024 ) and Emu3 ( Wang et al., 2024b ) demonstrate this formulation for multimodal understanding and generation. However, information loss during tokenization can constrain downstream performance and the benefits of scaling. A shared token interface alone does not guarantee that the learned codes jointly preserve semantic information and fine-grained visual detail, both of which are essential to general-purpose visual modeling. Prior studies have examined the distinct information requirements of understanding and reconstruction ( Qu et al., 2025 ) and identified limited discrete capacity as a key bottleneck in supporting both objectives ( Ma et al., 2025 ) . Motivated by these findings, dNaViT aims to preserve complementary task-relevant information within a shared discrete vocabulary and match the performance of task-specific representations. 2.2 Overall Architecture Motivated by the exploration of information recovery through residual connections ( Meituan LongCat Team et al., 2026 ) , dNaViT further propose residual modulation to aggregate complementary cues and enrich the features of a frozen native-resolution vision encoder with reconstruction-relevant detail (Fig. 3 ). To preserve both semantic structure and visual detail within a shared discrete bottleneck, we quantize the enriched features using grouped product quantization and supervise the resulting codes through dual reconstruction branches. Progressive training coordinates these objectives to mitigate interference during optimization. 2.3 Representation Encoder Unified visual tokenization must reconcile appearance invariance for understanding with sensitivity to detail for reconstruction. RAEs demonstrate reconstruction from continuous semantic features ( Zheng et al., 2025 ) , while LongCat-Next suggests that residual connections can carry low-level information across a semantic encoder ( Meituan LongCat Team et al., 2026 ) . This motivates us to directly leverage complementary cues by recombining multiple encoder depths, without even training the encoder itself. Meanwhile, quantization may collapse weakly expressed appearance differences into the same code. It therefore requires retaining the final semantic representation as an explicit anchor throughout quantization to preserve representation quality. Motivated by evidence that multi-codebook quantization with a sufficient bit budget preserves semantic information at a level competitive with continuous representations ( Zou et al., 2026a ) , we adopt grouped product quantization ( Jégou et al., 2011 ) to expand this capacity while keeping the spatial token grid unchanged. discriminator loss. 3 Implementation 3.1 Training Stage Our optimization strategy separates learning complementary visual detail from adapting the enriched representation to a discrete bottleneck. These sources are grouped into three complementary categories according to visual content. Tokenizer training uses images exclusively for dual-branch supervision, with a carefully controlled resolution distribution to support arbitrary-resolution training. General-Domain Images. LAION ( Schuhmann et al., 2022 ) , COYO ( Byeon et al., 2022 ) , and DataComp ( Gadre et al., 2023 ) , together with images from Recap-DataComp-1B ( Li et al., 2024 ) and CapsFusion ( Yu et al., 2024b ) , provide broad coverage of objects, scenes, and visual styles. An in-house image corpus further expands the diversity of the training data. Text-Rich Images and Documents. Selected subsets of TextAtlas5M ( Wang et al., 2025a ) include book covers, images containing long words, academic papers, and presentation slides. Chinese- and English-language book and news pages, along with additional OCR-oriented collections, extend coverage to dense text, small characters, and complex document layouts. Infographic collections and ChartGalaxy ( Li et al., 2025 ) further contribute information-dense content, including charts and tables, where spatial relationships among visual elements are critical to interpretation. Fine-Grained and High-aesthetic Images. Face-focused LAION subsets, images derived from SA-1B ( Kirillov et al., 2023 ) , and an in-house collection of high-aesthetic-quality images enrich the corpus with detailed facial structures, local textures, and varied image compositions. Training begins at a fixed resolution of 256 × 256 256\times 256 using a limited set of image sources to facilitate rapid convergence, then progressively transitions to native-resolution training at resolutions up to 2K on a broader data mixture. During this transition, sampling maintains a balanced distribution across resolution ranges. Images are resized to approximately preserve their aspect ratios, align spatial dimensions with the encoder stride, and keep sequence lengths within the token budget. Across all stages, the model is trained on several hundred billion tokens. 4 Experiment We evaluate dNaViT across five complementary tasks to assess the generality of its shared discrete representation. Image reconstruction measures the preservation of fine-grained detail, while frozen-feature classification and multimodal understanding assess semantic discriminability, transfer robustness, and visual reasoning. while keeping the visual representation and input resolution fixed. The ViT
pixel decoder maps the discrete code embeddings directly to pixel values.
This path reliably preserves the global layout, color distribution, and
semantic content, but tends to over-smooth high-frequency information.
Consequently, small text, fine boundaries, and repetitive textures often
appear blurred or partially merged. The flow-based Refiner, used in dNaViT-G16-R, conditions the decoder on the same
discrete representations while leveraging a pretrained generative prior.
Rather than relying solely on direct pixel regression, it recovers sharper
letter contours, finer textures, and more convincing high-frequency details.
These improvements are obtained without changing the underlying codes, and
the generated results remain consistent with the structure and semantic
content specified by the tokenizer. The advantage becomes particularly evident under high spatial compression.
Even with a compact token representation, the Refiner continues to recover
legible text and boundary details that the pixel decoder leaves ambiguous.
This indicates that the discrete representation retains substantial
information beyond what is recovered by direct pixel decoding, and that a
generative decoder can realize this information more effectively. The
comparison therefore separates the capacity of the learned tokenizer from
the limitations of the decoding head, highlighting the benefit of our
code-conditioned refinement under highly compressed representations. Figure 9 : Qualitative reconstruction comparison at the same input
