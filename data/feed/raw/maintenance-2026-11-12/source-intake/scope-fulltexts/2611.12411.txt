Figure 1: By internalizing structured physical privileges, PhysOPSD empowers base models to generate more realistic motion trajectories and causal dynamic interactions across diverse physical regimes. 1 Introduction Recent video generation models [ 40 , 10 , 15 , 1 ] have made impressive strides in visual fidelity and temporal coherence, yet they remain noticeably brittle when generating physically plausible dynamics and complex interactions [ 28 , 2 , 3 ] . Targeted post-training has thus emerged as a vital route to reinforce physical capabilities beyond pre-training [ 18 , 32 ] . However, existing paradigms involve clear trade-offs: ➀ supervised fine-tuning (SFT) offers dense supervision [ 42 , 29 , 17 , 49 ] , but operates on offline data that mismatches the states visited during inference; ➁ reinforcement learning (RL) optimizes directly over on-policy rollouts [ 14 , 8 , 12 ] , yet suffers from sparse, ill-defined rewards and credit-assignment bottlenecks. Recent ➂ on-policy distillation (OPD) [ 22 , 34 ] provides a natural middle ground by querying a teacher for dense supervision on student-visited rollout states. Building upon this, on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) [ 50 ] further removes the need for an external teacher by casting the same model into asymmetric roles: the student acts on inference-time inputs, while the teacher accesses additional privileged context to supervise the student rollouts. The central question of OPSD therefore becomes how to leverage extra information to turn the same model into a stronger self-teacher , as shown in Figure 2 ( a ). An effective privileged context should directly expose the information required by the target capability and reliably enhance teacher predictions on student rollout states. This principle has shown strong promise in language reasoning via ground-truth answers and rationales [ 50 , 31 , 47 ] , as well as in image generation via target-image multimodal representations [ 13 ] . Extending this paradigm to video generation, however, is far less straightforward, as the primary bottleneck lies in modeling physically plausible dynamics, a capability that also defies evaluation by standard reward models [ 18 , 42 , 3 ] .
Yet, designing effective privileged context remains non-trivial: text expansion provides richer descriptions but remains fundamentally declarative, whereas real reference videos capture complete dynamics but heavily entangle them with irrelevant appearance, identity, and background clutter without controllable structure ( Figure 2 ( b )).
These bottlenecks lead to our core research question: Research Question How can we construct privileged context that unlocks a video generator as its own physics teacher? To answer this question, we introduce PhysOPSD , which proposes executable code as privileged physical context for video generation OPSD. Our key insight is that an executable physical scene instantiates an editable world rather than a fixed visual outcome.
This executability inherently provides two structural properties absent in raw video data: ❶ abstraction , the ability to express dynamics across varying information densities; and ❷ controllability , the ability to isolate and systematically perturb underlying physical parameters. To operationalize this insight, we first develop PhysOPSD-Engine , which converts diverse real-world scenarios into verifiable simulation code and renders synchronized physical drafts via a coding agent. PhysOPSD then systematically translates these two properties into OPSD:
♣ dual-level physical privilege exploits abstraction by pairing dense drafts with abstract drafts, establishing an intermediate physical information bottleneck to bridge the privilege gap and prevent appearance shortcuts;
♠ controlled physical variation exploits controllability by altering specific physical parameters across paired scenes with shared rollout noise, explicitly supervising how physical shifts dictate dynamic outcomes.
By internalizing structured code privilege through on-policy self-distillation, PhysOPSD empowers base video generators to generate more physically plausible dynamics and stable interactions directly from text prompts. To summarize, this work contributes threefold: • We propose code as privileged physical context for video generation, and introduce PhysOPSD , an on-policy self-distillation framework incorporating dual-level physical privilege to bridge the information gap and controlled physical variation to capture causal dynamic shifts. • We develop PhysOPSD-Engine to translate real-world physical scenarios into verifiable and controllable simulation programs, and construct PhysOPSD-3K , an executable physical scene dataset featuring multi-abstraction drafts and paired physical variants. • Extensive experiments demonstrate that PhysOPSD consistently improves physical plausibility across diverse backbones, outperforming prior SFT and RL baselines with only a small fraction of their training data, without compromising general generation quality. 2 Related Work On-Policy Distillation. On-policy distillation (OPD) mitigates train-test distribution shifts by supervising the student directly on its self-generated states with dense feedback [ 22 , 34 ] .
This paradigm has been applied to consolidate domain experts in language models such as MOPD [ 27 ] , fuse visual capabilities in diffusion and flow models [ 20 , 7 ] , and optimize sampling trajectories in video generation [ 9 ] .
To eliminate the reliance on external teachers, on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) constructs a stronger self-teacher from the same model under asymmetric privileged information [ 50 ] .
While ground-truth solutions and rationales serve as privileged context in language reasoning [ 50 , 31 , 47 ] , D-OPSD [ 13 ] employs target-image multimodal representations to tune few-step image diffusion models.
For video models, OPSD-V [ 26 ] replaces older KV-cache history with real video frames to mitigate long-horizon drift in few-step autoregressive (AR) generators.
Unlike OPSD-V, which focuses on causal AR cache stabilization, PhysOPSD explores on-policy self-distillation for general video generation, introducing executable code as privileged physical context. Code-for-Vision. Empowered by modern foundational vision-language models (VLMs) [ 33 , 37 ] , executable code is increasingly utilized for visual scene synthesis and understanding.
Early efforts such as 3D-GPT [ 35 ] synthesize Blender scripts to procedurally build 3D environments [ 16 ] .
Recent studies further leverage coding agents for programmatic visual reconstruction, editing, and quantitative physical reasoning [ 48 , 21 , 36 , 41 ] .
More recently, researchers have explored coding agents to assist visual generation by delegating spatiotemporal processes to code execution [ 6 ] , either as physics-aware drafts in Blender [ 19 ] or cinematic previsualizations in Three.js [ 23 ] .
While existing generative approaches largely seek to replace part of generative modeling with coding agent capabilities, PhysOPSD explores how executable code can empower the generative model itself. Executable Code as Privileged Context. PhysOPSD bridges this gap by introducing executable code as privileged physical context, as shown in Figure 3 .
Unlike static text or video observations, an executable program explicitly specifies physical laws, object states, and dynamic parameters within an editable simulation, thereby uniquely providing explicit parametric control.
To operationalize this, PhysOPSD-Engine first translates real-world physical references 𝐕 ref \mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{ref}} into an executable program 𝒫 \mathcal{P} .

Here, code 𝒫 \mathcal{P} generates the underlying physical dynamics, while the rendered visual draft 𝐝 \mathbf{d} conditions the self-teacher. Structured Physical Supervision. The subsequent Section 4.2 details PhysOPSD-Engine and PhysOPSD-3K , while Sections 4.3.1 and 4.3.2 elaborate the structured self-distillation mechanisms of PhysOPSD . 4.2 Executable Physical Context Engine To systematically synthesize verifiable and controllable privileged physical context, we develop PhysOPSD-Engine , an agentic pipeline that translates real-world physical references into executable scene programs and rendered physical drafts. Reference-Guided Scene Construction. To seed simulations with natural diversity, PhysOPSD-Engine ingests real-world physical scenarios from WISA [ 42 ] and public video collections.
Rather than attempting to simulate arbitrary physical phenomena, the physical scope is deliberately restricted to simulation-groundable physical dynamics , categorized into three domains: (I) rigid-body and contact dynamics, (II) elastic and deformable solid mechanics, and (III) fluid dynamics.
Phenomena such as turbulent gas diffusion or thermodynamic phase shifts are excluded due to numerical instability and the difficulty of isolating causal physical parameters in standard simulators. Given a reference video 𝐕 ref \mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{ref}} , a coding agent 𝒜 \mathcal{A} then extracts the dominant physical event and formulates a structured physical scene specification: 𝒮 = ( 𝒪 , 𝒳 0 , 𝚽 , ℰ , 𝒞 ) , \mathcal{S}=(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{X}_{0},\bm{\Phi},\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C}), (6) where 𝒪 \mathcal{O} defines object geometries, 𝒳 0 \mathcal{X}_{0} specifies initial kinetic states, 𝚽 \bm{\Phi} collects physical properties ( e.g . , friction, elasticity, mass), ℰ \mathcal{E} indicates interaction mechanisms, and 𝒞 \mathcal{C} configures the camera trajectory.
The coding agent then compiles 𝒮 \mathcal{S} into an executable simulation program 𝒫 = 𝒜 ⁡ ( 𝒮 ) \mathcal{P}=\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{S}) .
Standard simulation backends such as MuJoCo [ 39 ] , Genesis [ 38 ] , and Blender [ 4 ] are employed purely for their deterministic contact solvers and numerical stability, without asserting the superiority of any specific engine.
Crucially, the governing principle is to reconstruct the physical scenario rather than visually reproduce the reference video : geometries and textures are simplified as long as the underlying physical mechanics are faithfully instantiated. Execution-Guided Verification. The synthesized program 𝒫 \mathcal{P} is then refined through an iterative execution-observation-revision loop.
Rather than optimizing for pixel-level trajectory similarity to 𝐕 ref \mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{ref}} , verification enforces four strict criteria: • Execution validity , ensuring code executes and renders without runtime divergence; • Physical validity , verifying that dynamic events emerge from simulation mechanics rather than hard-coded keyframe animations; • Visual observability , confirming that primary dynamic interactions are clearly visible within the viewport; • Parameter controllability , ensuring that systematically perturbing key physical parameters produces physically consistent and observable dynamic responses; Once verified, the target prompt is grounded directly on the executed outcome, 𝐩 = Describe ⁡ ( 𝒮 , 𝐝 ) \mathbf{p}=\operatorname{Describe}(\mathcal{S},\mathbf{d}) , guaranteeing semantic and causal alignment across 𝐩 ↔ 𝒫 ↔ 𝐝 \mathbf{p}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{P}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{d} . PhysOPSD-3K Dataset. Using PhysOPSD-Engine , we further construct PhysOPSD-3K , an executable physical scene dataset for video generation.
Each sample is represented as a structured tuple: 𝒟 i = { 𝐩 i , 𝒫 i , 𝐝 i , ℳ i } , \mathcal{D}_{i}=\{\mathbf{p}_{i},\mathcal{P}_{i},\mathbf{d}_{i},\mathcal{M}_{i}\}, (7) where 𝒫 i \mathcal{P}_{i} is the executable program, 𝐝 i \mathbf{d}_{i} is the rendered visual draft, 𝐩 i \mathbf{p}_{i} is the grounded prompt, and ℳ i \mathcal{M}_{i} denotes structured metadata detailing physical parameters and execution configurations.
Unlike static video collections, this executable formulation endows PhysOPSD-3K with intrinsic abstraction and controllability .
Encompassing the base scenes alongside their derived multi-abstraction drafts and parameter-controlled variants, PhysOPSD-3K comprises 3 3 K total instances.

We thus introduce an abstract physical draft 𝐝 abs \mathbf{d}^{\mathrm{abs}} as an intermediate physical information bottleneck , isolating motion and contact topology while filtering out fine-grained visual details. Appendix A More Details of PhysOPSD A.1 PhysOPSD-Engine Details Synthesizing executable physical scenes requires translating abstract dynamic descriptions into syntactically valid and numerically stable simulation code.
While frontier LLMs/VLMs exhibit proficient code generation and spatial reasoning, the non-linear nature of contact dynamics makes single-pass zero-shot generation unreliable for complex physical phenomena.
To guarantee a verifiable physical basis, PhysOPSD-Engine combines a frontier coding agent with an automated execution-guided refinement loop. Iterative Scene Refinement Workflow. We adopt GPT-6-Astra as the backbone coding agent.
Upon generating an initial scene program 𝒫 \mathcal{P} , PhysOPSD-Engine executes it in the target backend ( e.g . , MuJoCo [ 39 ] or Blender [ 4 ] ) and aggregates diagnostic feedback across three modalities: ❶ execution logs ( e.g . , syntax errors or solver convergence warnings), ❷ kinematic telemetry ( e.g . , penetration anomalies or constraint forces), and ❸ rendered preview keyframes.
When physical implausibility, solver divergence, or poor camera observability is detected, the coding agent inspects the diagnostic trace to revise geometric configurations, contact parameters, or camera trajectories.
This execution-observation-revision cycle iterates until all four verification criteria in Section 4.2 are satisfied. Multi-Level Draft Generation. To prevent dynamic discrepancies across privilege levels, dense drafts 𝐝 dense \mathbf{d}^{\mathrm{dense}} and abstract drafts 𝐝 abs \mathbf{d}^{\mathrm{abs}} for a given variant are rendered from an identical, frozen simulation state trajectory .
Specifically, once a simulation execution succeeds, PhysOPSD-Engine records the full kinematic trajectory of all bodies and contact events. 𝐝 dense \mathbf{d}^{\mathrm{dense}} is rendered with full surface meshes, material shaders, and realistic lighting, whereas 𝐝 abs \mathbf{d}^{\mathrm{abs}} renders simplified wireframe primitives and centroid trajectories from the exact same camera viewpoint.
This trajectory freezing guarantees that both privilege levels share identical underlying dynamics, completely eliminating confounding dynamic drift caused by re-simulation.
For controlled variants, non-target attributes and initial conditions are frozen to isolate the causal parameter shift. Figure 9: Comparison of PhysOPSD-Engine vs. Codex-Harness. Outcome-Grounded Dual Prompt Annotation. In the main text, we denote the target text condition uniformly as 𝐩 \mathbf{p} for mathematical clarity.
In practice, accommodating our omni-capable video generator entails producing two complementary text conditions via Qwen3-VL-32B [ 46 ] , conditioned on the dense draft alongside a structured fact card containing measured physical telemetry: • T2V Prompt : a self-contained description detailing the scene context, physical conditions, observable dynamic trajectory, and final state, used for deployment-time text-only student generation. • V2V Instruction : an imperative prompt instructing the model to perform photorealistic visual refinement while strictly preserving the underlying spatiotemporal dynamics and spatial layout, shared by the teacher and draft-conditioned student. Both text modalities are grounded strictly in the executed simulation telemetry rather than speculative prior descriptions.
The concrete system prompts utilized for annotation are detailed below. System Prompt for Dual Prompt Annotation ⬇ """ You are an expert video annotation assistant specializing in physical dynamics , object interactions , and temporally grounded video descriptions . Your task is to generate accurate training prompts for text - to - video ( T2V ) generation and video - to - video ( V2V ) editing . Treat them as independent physical outcomes , not as a before - and - after sequence . Prioritize factual accuracy , physical consistency , and temporal grounding over narrative richness . 1. VISUAL AND PHYSICAL GROUNDING - Identify the principal objects , initial configuration , physical interactions , motion progression , and actual final states . - Examine the full temporal sequence . Changing a physical parameter does not guarantee a specific result . - Use the verified fact card to identify physical conditions , affected objects , measured states , and camera settings . Ground the actual motion and outcomes in the provided visual evidence . - Do not infer screen - space directions from numerical coordinates . Do not confuse physical speed with video playback speed . - Omit uncertain details rather than inventing events or outcomes . 2. ANNOTATION REQUIREMENTS Generate two English training descriptions for EACH physical variant . A . Baselines. As a post-training framework, our primary evaluation focuses on assessing relative capability gains against the respective base models.
Furthermore, because physics-oriented post-training methods for video generation remain relatively scarce and are typically coupled with disparate backbones, we select and adapt three baselines to represent three prevailing post-training paradigms onto the same backbones: • PhyT2V [ 45 ] : an iterative test-time refinement strategy. We preserve its physical chain-of-thought reasoning and prompt revision workflow, adapting its generator to our respective base models for direct paradigm comparison. • SFT [ 42 ] : standard supervised fine-tuning on physics-specific demonstrations. We fine-tune each base model on WISA-80K, which comprises approximately 80 80 K curated text-video pairs spanning 17 physical categories across dynamics, thermodynamics, and optics. • PhysHPO [ 5 ] : an offline hierarchical preference optimization framework. We adopt its original 21 21 K curated real-world videos from OpenVidHD [ 29 ] as positive demonstrations, paired with base model rollouts as negative samples, tailored to OmniWeaving and UniVideo. OPSD Variants. For the privileged context analysis in Section 5.3 , all variants are evaluated under an identical budget of 3 3 K samples.
❶ For w/ Refined Text , we utilize the physical reasoning and prompt revision pipeline from PhyT2V on the real reference videos and their original prompts to synthesize enriched prompts with detailed physical descriptions, which serve as the privileged text context.
❷ For w/ Real Video , the teacher receives both the text prompt and raw reference videos as privileged context, supplemented using PhysHPO’s data selection pipeline to match the 3 3 K budget. B.2 Evaluation Benchmarks VideoPhy-2. VideoPhy-2 [ 3 ] evaluates action-centric physical commonsense across 200 action categories under the T2V setting.
Following [ 42 ] , we assess Semantic Adherence (SA) and Physical Commonsense (PC) by using GPT-5.5, reporting mean scores, the proportions of videos achieving SA ≥ 4 \text{SA}\geq 4 and PC ≥ 4 \text{PC}\geq 4 , and the joint metric (All) satisfying both criteria simultaneously. PhyGround. PhyGround [ 24 ] benchmarks text- and initial-frame-conditioned generation (TI2V) across 250 diverse scenarios spanning 13 physical laws in solid mechanics, fluid dynamics, and optics.
Following its official benchmark protocol, we evaluate General plausibility (covering temporal coherence and object permanence) and law-specific Physics compliance, reporting their equal-weighted Overall score. VBench. We employ VBench 1.0 [ 11 ] to benchmark general video generation capabilities under the T2V protocol.
Following the official evaluation suite across 16 fine-grained dimensions, we report normalized Quality, Semantic, and Total scores using standard prompt sets.
