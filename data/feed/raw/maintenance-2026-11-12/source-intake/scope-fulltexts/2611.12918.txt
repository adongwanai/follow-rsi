These measurements show why support composition must be controlled alongside
candidate accuracy when offline results are used to reason about deployment. 1 Introduction Web interaction traces contain a prefix of observations and actions, but an
offline benchmark often supplies the gold action as an item in the candidate
set. Consider a target such as click “Blue 256GB” . In an oracle
evaluation it is guaranteed to appear among 15 distractors; in deployment, a
system must first discover and name that action from the current page and
history. Ranking correctness is therefore conditional on candidate
availability. A second issue appears when candidate availability is correlated with the
evaluation label. In the original target-anchored construction, the logged
action is inserted into the candidate set while every negative is sampled from
the frozen training vocabulary. For a target-OOV row, the gold action is thus
the unique out-of-support candidate. A support-only rule can identify the
target from candidate membership alone; we call this candidate-support confounding . We therefore separate behavioral
discrimination from the information revealed by the support composition of the
candidate set. We study this distinction with a target-free context c t c_{t} and a structured
action key a t = ( type , target , value ) a_{t}=(\mathrm{type},\mathrm{target},\mathrm{value}) . The protocol
freezes the candidate support using training targets, keeps sessions
disjoint, and stores an ordered row ledger containing candidates,
probabilities, ranks, NLLs, and provenance. A test target whose exact key is
absent from the training support is marked target-OOV; it is never added to the
training vocabulary or the model input. pseudo-OOV calibration, and complete candidate-level ledgers; and • a target-free dual-path proposal evaluation that reports proposal
recall, covered reranking, and end-to-end exact action selection on
OPeRA and Mind2Web. 2 Related Work Offline decision modeling. Maximum-entropy inverse reinforcement learning (MaxEnt IRL) models a
demonstration as a distribution over trajectories rather than as a deterministic
label ( Ziebart et al., 2008 ) . Our scorer uses a finite conditional
candidate-level softmax objective rather than a trajectory partition function.
We therefore refer to this family as factorized candidate scoring below; some
implementation artifacts retain the historical “IRL” label. The objective is
an IRL-inspired finite-choice energy surrogate: it does not solve a
trajectory-level inverse problem, occupancy-matching problem, or reward-
identification problem. Offline RL methods address distribution shift
by constraining learned values to the support of logged data, with implicit
value learning providing a useful reference point for our candidate-ranking
setting ( Levine et al., 2020 ; Kumar et al., 2020 ; Kostrikov et al., 2022 ) . Dataset and
protocol design are central to offline evaluation, as emphasized by D4RL ( Fu et al., 2021 ) . Diffusion-based trajectory models offer a
complementary way to represent multi-step behavior, but their offline gains
depend strongly on the quality of the latent and action interfaces ( Janner et al., 2022 ) . We borrow the probabilistic decision view while
making the candidate support and preference factors explicit. Web and tool-using agents. ReAct interleaves reasoning and acting ( Yao et al., 2023 ) , while Toolformer
and Reflexion study tool use and verbal feedback ( Schick et al., 2023 ; Shinn et al., 2023 ) . WebArena and Mind2Web provide
realistic browser tasks and traces ( Zhou et al., 2024 ; Deng et al., 2023 ) ;
AgentBench broadens evaluation across agent environments ( Liu et al., 2024 ) . These benchmarks motivate our focus on action
semantics and session-level context. Mind2Web in particular uses candidate
generation and top- k k recall, whereas our narrower contribution is the
train-only candidate contract, row-level likelihood ledger, and explicit
target-OOV analysis for a closed-choice factorized scorer. Preference learning and explanations. Direct preference optimization and human-feedback fine-tuning ( Rafailov et al., 2023 ; Ouyang et al., 2022 ) are useful future baselines for our
language-model arm, whereas the present mainline isolates a symbolic candidate
scorer. Self-Refine and Voyager show how feedback and self-generated memory can
improve agent behavior ( Madaan et al., 2023 ; Wang et al., 2023 ) ; our focus
is the preceding measurement contract that makes such improvements auditable.
Finally, the faithfulness literature warns against treating textual rationales
as explanations without independent validation ( Turpin et al., 2023 ) . 3 Method 3.1 Evaluation contract and candidate support Let a session be a sequence of observed steps τ = ( o 1 , a 1 , … , o T , a T ) \tau=(o_{1},a_{1},\ldots,o_{T},a_{T}) , where each action a t a_{t} is serialized as an

The negative-action vocabulary is frozen from training action keys. For each
row, the logged target is inserted once as an evaluation anchor and 15
deterministic negatives are drawn from that vocabulary; the configured ratio
selects up to eight hard negatives (sharing the action type or target when
possible) and seven random non-duplicates, with a deterministic small-pool
fallback. Thus a test target outside the
training vocabulary is scoreable but marked target-OOV: it does not update the
global vocabulary or supply input context. This target-anchored design has
100% candidate-anchor coverage by construction and evaluates conditional
reranking under a controlled choice set; it is not open-vocabulary action
generation. The evaluator inserts the logged target only after the model has
formed the target-free context, so the reported Top- k k values measure
conditional reranking given an oracle candidate anchor rather than the
probability of proposing that action in deployment. The builder deduplicates the train action vocabulary by the ( type , target , value ) (\mathrm{type},\mathrm{target},\mathrm{value}) key before sampling. With 16
slots and a hard-negative ratio of 0.5, it requests up to eight same-type or
same-target negatives and fills the remainder with random train-vocabulary
actions. A SHA-256 digest of session_id:step_id:seed initializes the per-row RNG, and a small
pool falls back to unused hard negatives without duplicating the anchor. This
makes the candidate list byte-reproducible while exposing a controlled source
of difficulty: the held-out rows contain 777 click rows with 15 same-type
negatives, 65 with 14, 3 with 13, 107 input rows with two, and 40 terminate

information contracts. (1) Oracle candidate reranking inserts the
logged target into a fixed 16-way set and measures Top-1, NLL, MRR, exact-action
accuracy, action-type accuracy, and Macro-F1. (2) Closed-vocabulary
proposal removes the target anchor and original candidate list, then ranks
the complete frozen V train V_{\mathrm{train}} ; Recall@ k k and Exact@1 measure the
proposal boundary, and target-OOV recall is structurally zero. (3) Observation recoverability extracts named actions from the current
serialized observation without reading the target or source candidate list.
Its coverage is an information ceiling, not a learned generator. We never
multiply metrics across regimes: each number is conditional on its own

support is a lexical audit variable. The Mind2Web audit covers official
cross-task, cross-website, and cross-domain splits; it measures candidate
coverage and ranking rather than online browser interaction. The released
protocol records candidate composition, support status, proposal provenance,
and row-level hashes so future work can extend the audit to additional
DOM-grounded proposers. 5 Conclusion We identified candidate-support confounding in target-anchored offline web
evaluation: when OOV targets are paired with in-support distractors, support
membership alone reveals the gold action. SIR and the support-only baseline
make this shortcut directly measurable. Support-balanced candidates reduce SIR
