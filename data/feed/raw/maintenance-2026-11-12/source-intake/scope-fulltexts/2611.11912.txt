Email: pingwei@xjtu.edu.cn Kai Mao Shuaijia ChenNational Key Laboratory of Human-Machine Hybrid Augmented IntelligenceInstitute of Artificial Intelligence and Robotics, Xi’an Jiaotong University Abstract LiDAR point clouds and 3D semantic occupancy represent two distinct modalities capturing fine-grained metric geometry and dense volumetric semantics, respectively, holding natural potential for mutual complementarity. However, existing world models typically forecast these modalities independently or cascade them heuristically, missing the opportunity for reciprocal cross-modal regularization. To address this, we propose LO-World , a unified 4D world model that forecasts both modalities within a shared BEV latent space. This single-stream formulation enables dense semantics and metric geometry to mutually reinforce each other, while eliminating multi-pipeline redundancy. Specifically, LO-World couples an efficient hybrid range-voxel autoencoder with a flow-matching generator that caches historical context to reduce iterative sampling latency. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LO-World outperforms prior state-of-the-art baselines, improving occupancy forecasting IoU by 10.9 % 10.9\% and reducing LiDAR Chamfer Distance by 30.1 % 30.1\% . Meanwhile, it sustains a real-time throughput exceeding 25 25 FPS on a single RTX 4090. Autonomous-driving world models [ 12 ] forecast future 3D scenes to guide downstream tasks [ 13 , 14 , 7 , 6 , 21 ] . Within these frameworks, 3D semantic occupancy [ 27 , 28 , 42 , 19 ] and LiDAR point clouds [ 2 , 26 , 17 , 40 ] offer complementary strengths: occupancy yields dense volumetric semantics but suffers from voxel discretization blur, whereas raw LiDAR captures precise metric geometry despite spatial sparsity. We hypothesize that jointly modeling both modalities enables reciprocal spatio-temporal regularization. Specifically, sharp metric geometry holds the potential to anchor voxel boundaries and alleviate spatial discretization blur, while rich volumetric semantics may guide and stabilize the temporal evolution of sparse point distributions across future frames. Despite this promising potential, two common paradigms in existing 4D world models [ 12 , 42 , 19 , 40 , 17 ] remain limited in exploiting such bidirectional synergy. As illustrated in Fig. 1 a, the first paradigm treats occupancy and LiDAR forecasting as separate tasks, using independent modality-specific pipelines [ 42 , 8 , 35 , 19 , 17 , 40 , 36 , 22 ] . When combined to forecast both modalities, these pipelines require separate generative inference and do not explicitly exploit cross-modal structural regularization. The second paradigm, exemplified by UniScene [ 13 ] , adopts an occupancy-first cascade, generating semantic occupancy sequences before synthesizing LiDAR point clouds. However, its stage-wise training prevents downstream LiDAR reconstruction supervision from shaping the upstream occupancy representation, leaving reciprocal geometric and semantic learning underexploited. These limitations raise a pivotal question: Can we establish an intrinsic connection between dense semantics and fine-grained metric geometry to enable mutual spatio-temporal regularization through a cohesive generative process? To answer this question, we present LO-World , a unified generative framework for 4D world modeling. Rather than relying on late-stage attention fusion across isolated branches, LO-World shifts the modeling focus to an intrinsically shared latent space. At the core of our framework is LO-VAE , a multimodal autoencoder [ 11 ] designed to bridge the structural gap between the two modalities. Specifically, LO-VAE projects both semantic occupancy and point clouds into a single BEV latent space. Within this pipeline, the LiDAR encoder introduces a hybrid range-voxel architecture to capture native sensor scan patterns before aggregating them into an ego-aligned BEV plane. By embedding metric geometry directly alongside dense semantics, LO-VAE enables deep cross-modal feature synergy, executing joint forecasting through a single generative stream rather than isolated pipelines. Building upon this shared latent space, we propose LO-Former , an efficient flow-matching generator [ 20 ] tailored for 4D temporal forecasting. Rather than redundantly encoding historical observations during iterative generation, LO-Former caches the spatio-temporal context once, keeping the repeatedly evaluated flow vector field remarkably lightweight. Coupled with the efficient sampling dynamics of flow matching, this design achieves high-fidelity future rollouts in merely four steps. As shown in Fig. 1 a, LO-World predicts a sequence of unified latents that are decoded into paired semantic occupancy and LiDAR point clouds through a shared decoder with modality-specific heads. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We explore joint 4D forecasting of 3D semantic occupancy and raw LiDAR point clouds. Empirical results demonstrate that joint modeling can improve forecasting accuracy for both modalities over their unimodal counterparts. • We propose LO-World, integrating a hybrid range-voxel autoencoder to forecast both modalities within a single generative stream, alongside a flow-matching generator that reduces iterative sampling overhead through context caching. • Extensive evaluations demonstrate the clear superiority of LO-World over prior state-of-the-art baselines. Specifically, it achieves a 10.9 % 10.9\% relative gain in occupancy forecasting IoU and a 30.1 % 30.1\% error reduction in LiDAR CD, while operating at over 25 25 FPS on a single RTX 4090. 2 Related Work 2.1 LiDAR-Based World Models Existing LiDAR world models primarily process point clouds through either range-view projections or 3D voxelization [ 10 , 40 , 36 , 45 , 24 , 32 , 22 , 17 ] . Range-view methods [ 45 , 24 , 43 , 22 , 17 ] , such as LiDARCrafter [ 17 ] and R2DM [ 22 ] , unfold point clouds onto compact 2D spherical planes to match native beam distributions and alleviate input sparsity. However, pixel proximity on the range image fails to preserve genuine 3D spatial adjacency, introducing geometric distortions. Conversely, voxel-based frameworks [ 10 , 40 , 36 , 32 ] , such as Copilot4D [ 40 ] discretizes 3D space into structured grids to maintain metric neighborhood relations. Nevertheless, faithfully capturing physical structures requires fine-grained voxelization, which incurs prohibitive computational and memory overheads. While multimodal world models such as BEVWorld [ 41 ] explore a unified BEV latent, they rely on standard voxelization for LiDAR and jointly model camera and LiDAR observations. In contrast, LO-World targets the 4D co-forecasting of occupancy and LiDAR through a hybrid range-voxel design that aggregates range-derived features into spatially aligned voxels for subsequent BEV encoding. 2.2 Occupancy-Based World Models Occupancy provides a dense, semantics-aware representation for 3D world modeling. Following OccWorld [ 42 ] , subsequent methods [ 33 , 38 , 30 , 8 , 29 , 19 ] improve occupancy forecasting through advances in spatial tokenization, temporal modeling, and multi-condition steering. Beyond standalone forecasting, occupancy also serves as an intermediate structural prior for other modalities [ 39 , 34 , 10 , 13 ] . For example, 4DOcc [ 10 ] forecasts occupancy from historical LiDAR and renders future point clouds, whereas UniScene [ 13 ] first generates occupancy to condition downstream LiDAR and video generators. Although these pipelines highlight the structural benefit of occupancy, they adopt an occupancy-first formulation rather than jointly forecasting a shared representation of both modalities. In contrast, LO-World jointly encodes spatially aligned occupancy and LiDAR into a shared BEV latent, enabling joint forecasting of both modalities through a single latent-space generative process. 3 Method Figure 2 : Overall architecture of LO-World. Our framework consists of two components: (1) LO-VAE, which jointly tokenizes occupancy and LiDAR into a unified BEV latent space; and (2) LO-Former, a flow-matching-based generation framework that efficiently generates future unified BEV latents from Gaussian noise. Overall Architecture. As illustrated in Fig. 2 , LO-World comprises two key modules: LO-VAE and LO-Former. At each timestamp, LO-VAE aligns and fuses synchronized occupancy and LiDAR features through a shared encoder into a unified BEV latent. Conditioned on past latents and future navigation priors, LO-Former models temporal dynamics via conditional flow matching to generate future latent sequences. Finally, each forecasted latent is decoded through a shared trunk and modality-specific heads to reconstruct both future modalities in a single pass. 3.1 Unified Tokenization of Occupancy and LiDAR Unified BEV Representation. Asymmetric Flow Architecture. Standard diffusion and flow-matching models [ 9 , 20 , 23 ] suffer from considerable inference latency due to the repeated evaluation of deep backbones across iterative sampling steps. To resolve this computational bottleneck, LO-Former adopts a decoupled architecture that separates historical context encoding from recurrent generation via two specialized sub-networks: a deep context encoder and a lightweight flow network. Specifically, the L ctx L_{\mathrm{ctx}} -layer context encoder processes historical latents in a single forward pass, producing a cached conditional representation that is computed only once per rollout. Conversely, the L flow L_{\mathrm{flow}} -layer flow network serves as an iterative generative engine, repeatedly executed at each sampling step to refine future latent trajectories. By amortizing the computational cost of extracting complex spatiotemporal history onto the single-pass context module, the recurrent flow network can be kept remarkably shallow, with L flow = 4 L_{\mathrm{flow}}=4 compared to L ctx = 8 L_{\mathrm{ctx}}=8 . This design substantially curtails per-step sampling latency while preserving high generative fidelity. Context Block and Flow Block. The historical latents 𝐳 τ \mathbf{z}_{\tau} are patchified and augmented with spatial and temporal embeddings. The context block applies deformable spatial attention (DSA) [ 31 ] and deformable temporal attention (DTA) [ 15 ] to aggregate spatiotemporal historical cues into a compact context 𝐇 \mathbf{H} . Within the flow block, we propose Cross-frame Deformable Temporal Attention (CDTA) as a dedicated bridge connecting cached context to future flow updates. Rather than computing costly dense cross-attention, CDTA uses future tokens as queries to sparsely sample historical features from 𝐇 \mathbf{H} across time. Together with spatial DSA within each future frame and temporal DTA across future frames, CDTA effectively conditions the iterative flow on past dynamics with minimal computational overhead. Primary evaluations are conducted on nuScenes [ 4 ] with 32-beam LiDAR and Occ3D-nuScenes [ 27 ] at a 0.4 ​ m 0.4m voxel resolution. To evaluate cross-sensor transferability and data-efficient adaptation, we further benchmark on the 64-beam Waymo Open Dataset [ 26 ] using Occ3D-Waymo [ 27 ] . Specifically, models are fine-tuned on a tiny split of merely 50 training sequences, representing under 7 % 7\% of the standard training data, and comprehensively evaluated on the entire 202 validation sequences. We benchmark three variants: LO-World-T with trajectories, LO-World-TBM with additional 3D boxes and HD maps, and LO-World-STC incorporating camera-derived STC occupancy [ 18 ] . LO-VAE maps occupancy and LiDAR into a shared BEV latent.
Occupancy embeddings are collapsed along height, while normalized LiDAR range and validity features are aggregated into voxels and arranged in BEV.
Modality-specific convolutional stems process these representations before a shared encoder fuses their concatenated features.
A shared decoder then recovers scene features for both output heads.

Training targets assign each valid return to the nearest candidate in metric depth.

These loss settings are shared by LO-World-T and LO-World-TBM. Training sequences. When the four-frame history is incomplete, we repeat the first observation.
Unavailable future targets are replaced with the last observation and assigned weight 0.1 in both forecasting losses, without renormalizing the remaining terms.
Evaluation instead requires complete future windows (Sec. B.1 ). Sampling. LO-Former jointly generates six future latents from Gaussian noise using four Euler steps and a single encoding of the history.
LO-VAE decodes the resulting latents into occupancy grids and LiDAR range images. Long-horizon rollout. For a 6 s rollout, we generate two consecutive six-frame chunks.
Before the second chunk, we re-encode the updated history using predictions from the first chunk rather than future ground-truth observations.
Generation remains joint within each chunk. A.4 Conditioning and Cross-Dataset Evaluation Conditioning variants. Both LO-World-T and LO-World-TBM forecast from paired historical occupancy and LiDAR, with relative ego rotation and translation as frame-wise conditions.
LO-World-TBM also uses box- and map-derived layouts as spatial conditions.
These layouts are supplied externally rather than predicted by LO-World.
For 12-frame prediction, LO-World-TBM is fine-tuned from its six-frame counterpart.
It follows the 12-frame settings in Table A3 , with a tenfold learning rate for the layout embedding and adapter. Camera-derived occupancy. In the STC setting, camera-predicted occupancy replaces the annotated occupancy input, while LiDAR remains an observed modality.
Occupancy forecasts are still evaluated against the ground-truth annotations. Waymo evaluation. We evaluate both zero-shot transfer to Waymo and adaptation using 50 target-domain training sequences on the same 202 validation sequences described in the main paper.
The LiDAR output uses a 64 × 2048 64\times 2048 range image with vertical projection bounds of − 17.734 ∘ -17.734^{\circ} and 2.274 ∘ 2.274^{\circ} . A.5 Downstream Video Generation Occupancy conditioning. To provide occupancy conditions for video generation, we apply LO-World-TBM zero-shot at 12 Hz using the supplied 12 Hz ego trajectories and box- and map-derived layouts.
This changes the prediction frequency without further training the occupancy model, unlike the 12-frame fine-tuning above.
The predicted occupancy sequences are fed to GenieDrive [ 37 ] following its occupancy-input setup.
The 12 Hz ground-truth occupancy reference comes from UniScene [ 13 ] . Video training and evaluation. We fine-tune the video model using the first 81 frames of each training scene and evaluate on the first 81 frames of each validation scene.
FVD, mIoU, and mAP are computed following the evaluation protocol of MagicDrive [ 7 ] . A.6 Modality-Specific Baselines Single-modality autoencoders. The separate-latent baselines in Table 6 of the main paper use single-modality variants of LO-VAE.
The occupancy-only variant follows the same encoder and decoder architecture as LO-VAE.
The LiDAR-only variant retains the LiDAR pathway but removes the occupancy-context skip connection to the LiDAR decoder. Token concatenation (a). The occupancy and LiDAR latent tokens are concatenated into a single sequence before being processed by LO-Former.
This differs from LO-World, which forecasts a shared latent representation rather than separately encoded modality tokens. Cross-modal attention (b). The two modality-specific sequences remain separate and are processed in parallel, exchanging information through cross-modal attention in both the context and flow blocks of LO-Former. B.4 Distributional Metrics We assess distributional fidelity by comparing collections of predicted and reference point clouds, rather than individual prediction–reference pairs. Point-cloud features.
