Abstract Automated segmentation in multi-view echocardiography is essential for comprehensive cardiac assessment, yet remains challenging due to substantial anatomical and appearance variability across standard acoustic windows. In this paper, we present P2Echo, a unified framework capable of segmenting diverse cardiac structures across multiple standard echocardiographic views using a single model controlled by structured view-aware text prompts. Unlike recent foundation model adaptations that rely on implicit latent priors, P2Echo explicitly conditions the segmentation process via a novel Dynamic Image-Text Alignment (DyITA) module. DyITA integrates a differentiable routing mechanism and token differential operators to dynamically modulate decoder features according to the requested view and target structure. This allows the network to adaptively switch its processing pathways for different views without architectural changes. Extensive experiments on a large-scale internal dataset (seen during training) and held-out evaluation on three external datasets demonstrate that P2Echo matches or exceeds both view-specific specialists and state-of-the-art universal models at a fraction of their computational cost, offering an efficient, controllable solution for multi-plane cardiac analysis. Code is available at: https://github.com/moeinheidari7829/P2Echo . † † aftertitle: Highlights • P2Echo is a single, language-prompted model that segments diverse cardiac structures across echo views, using a DyITA module to align text and image features inside each decoder stage. • Across five internal and three external datasets, P2Echo matches or exceeds view-specific specialists and foundation models while running at a fraction of their compute (6.90 GFLOPs, 64.68 FPS), with a median gain of 2.33% Dice across evaluated datasets over the strongest baseline. • A single prompt-controllable network replaces multiple view-specific specialists, cutting development and maintenance costs and enabling consistent, efficient multi-plane cardiac assessment. Index Terms — cross-modal alignment, echocardiography, multi-view segmentation, prompt-driven segmentation, ultrasound image segmentation, vision–language models (VLMs) I Introduction Echocardiography is the primary non-invasive modality for cardiac diagnosis, relying on a standard set of acoustic windows (e.g., the Apical 2-, 3-, and 4-Chamber (A2C/2CH, A3C/3CH, A4C/4CH) and Parasternal Long/Short Axis (PLAX, PSAX) views) to visualize complex anatomy [ 1 ] . Although multi-view echocardiographic examinations provide complementary information, manual interpretation is time-consuming and prone to inter-observer variability. To enable objective and reproducible quantification of cardiac structure and function, automated analysis is essential, with accurate cardiac segmentation serving as a key foundational step. However, current automated solutions are largely siloed due to the dramatic anatomical appearance shifts between views; traditional deep learning models fail to generalize across planes [ 2 ] . This necessitates training separate specialist models for each view [ 3 ] , leading to redundant development, high maintenance costs, and inconsistent performance across the clinical workflow. As summarized in Figure 1 , multi-view echocardiography segmentation is complicated by several interacting sources of variability. First, the appearance and geometry of the same cardiac structure can change substantially across acoustic windows [ 1 , 3 ] , while only a subset of structures is visible in each view. Second, differences in scanners, acquisition settings, and clinical centers introduce additional domain shifts that alter image appearance even within the same view [ 4 ] . Inter-subject anatomical variability further changes chamber size, shape, and relative position. Finally, the characteristic speckle pattern of ultrasound, together with acoustic shadowing and signal dropout, can substantially weaken anatomical boundaries. Consequently, a unified model must simultaneously accommodate view-conditioned anatomy, heterogeneous acquisition domains, and locally ambiguous boundaries rather than learning a single view-agnostic appearance representation. Fig. 1 : Key challenges in multi-view echocardiography image segmentation: (a) cross-view appearance disparity, (b) acquisition-domain shift, (c) inter-subject anatomical variability, (d) view-specific anatomical visibility, and (e) boundary degradation from speckle, shadowing, and signal dropout. Colored contours denote the annotated cardiac structures. To address this redundancy, recent research has moved toward unified, multi-view segmentation. Early attempts utilized multi-branch architectures, where dedicated encoders process specific views before fusing information through cross-view dependency modeling [ 5 , 6 ] . While effective, these designs increase model complexity and remain architecturally rigid, as adding a new view requires adding a new parallel branch. Alternatively, universal models attempt to learn a single view-agnostic representation from pooled data. However, empirical evidence shows that when classic architectures are trained on combined multi-plane data, their performance significantly decreases compared to plane-specific training. Without explicit conditioning, these models often converge to an average solution that degrades per-view accuracy, failing to capture the unique geometric details of conflicting views (e.g., the circular left ventricle in short-axis vs. the bullet-shaped ventricle in apical views) [ 1 ] .
The rise of Foundation Models has introduced new paradigms for universal segmentation. SAM-based approaches, such as SAMUS [ 7 ] and EchoONE [ 1 ] , adapt the Segment Anything Model (SAM) [ 8 ] for ultrasound by incorporating adapter layers or latent prompt generators. While EchoONE achieves multi-view capabilities, its dense prompts are derived from clustering-based structural prototypes, making the resulting guidance dependent on how well these learned priors represent the input anatomy and difficult to control explicitly. Parallel to this, text-guided segmentation methods have shown that natural language can serve as an explicit control signal, from MedCLIP-SAMv2 [ 9 ] to Grounding DINO-US-SAM [ 10 ] , which pairs a LoRA-tuned Grounding DINO detector with SAM2 to segment multiple organs across 18 ultrasound datasets from free-form prompts. In these methods, language primarily guides target localization or visual-prompt generation before mask decoding, failing to continuously modulate decoder features at multiple spatial resolutions, as required to resolve the cross-view appearance variance of the same anatomical class in multi-view echocardiography. In this work, we propose P2Echo (Prompt2Echo), a unified framework that leverages structured textual prompts to explicitly condition and control a single segmentation model across diverse echocardiographic views. Unlike prototype-derived latent priors, which are learned implicitly and cannot be directly specified by users, P2Echo employs view-specific text descriptions (e.g., “Apical 4 Chamber Left Ventricle”) as explicit conditioning signals. The operator can directly define the desired view and target structure, while P2Echo dynamically modulates decoder features according to the provided textual guidance. Central to our approach is the Dynamic Image-Text Alignment (DyITA) module. Rather than simple post-hoc fusion, DyITA integrates text guidance directly into the decoder blocks via a novel dynamic routing mechanism, allowing the model to "switch" its attention patterns based on the requested anatomy and view.
Our main contributions are: ➊ We present P2Echo, a text-prompted unified segmentation framework for multi-view echocardiography. It replaces multiple specialist models with a single network that is explicitly controllable via structured textual prompts. ➋ We introduce the DyITA module. By employing differentiable routing and token differential operators, DyITA dynamically aligns image features with text prompts inside the decoder, effectively mitigating domain shifts between views. ➌ We demonstrate that P2Echo achieves strong performance on a large-scale internal dataset and competitive-to-superior held-out results on three external datasets at substantially lower computational cost, matching or exceeding both specialist models and recent foundation-model adaptations. II Method II-A Model Architecture P2Echo enables unified multi-view segmentation by conditioning a single network on view-aware text prompts. As illustrated in Figure 2 , the architecture comprises three stages: (i) a dual-encoder backbone extracting multi-scale image features and prompt embeddings; (ii) a prompt decoder that grounds prompts in the visual bottleneck to yield image-conditioned mask embeddings; and (iii) a multi-stage decoder natively fusing these embeddings at every spatial resolution via Image-Text Alignment (ITA) blocks. To construct a unified multi-view training set, we first harmonize the heterogeneous annotation protocols used across the source datasets. As illustrated in Figure 3 (a), source annotations may be represented using dataset-specific categorical pixel values, binary masks, or contour coordinates. We convert all annotations into a common categorical label space, with background assigned to 0 and the cardiac structures indexed as 1 for Left Ventricle (LV), 2 for Myocardium (MYO), 3 for Left Atrium (LA), 4 for Right Ventricle (RV), and 5 for Right Atrium (RA). Dataset-specific pixel values are deterministically remapped to the corresponding unified class identifiers, while contour-based annotations are rasterized and filled to obtain dense segmentation masks. This harmonization enables samples originating from different annotation conventions to be jointly optimized within a single multi-class segmentation framework.
Some source annotations require additional processing before they can be represented in this common label space. In particular, HMC_QU [ 20 ] provides myocardial annotations without an explicit LV cavity mask. Following the annotation harmonization strategy of EchoONE [ 1 ] , we identify the basal endpoints of the myocardial annotation, close the open contour between these landmarks, and fill the resulting enclosed region to derive the LV cavity while retaining the original MYO annotation, as illustrated in Figure 3 (b). After harmonization, all datasets share a consistent semantic representation despite differences in their original annotation formats. The resulting unified masks are subsequently used for training and evaluation across the supported echocardiographic views. {\color[rgb]{0,0,1}6.99}\pm 3.74 III-B Implementation Details We train and internally evaluate on five datasets and reserve three datasets for held-out external evaluation. Each development dataset is split into 80 % 80\% training, 10 % 10\% validation, and 10 % 10\% internal test data. P2Echo is implemented in PyTorch with 256 × 256 256\times 256 inputs and an ImageNet-pretrained PVTv2 B2 visual encoder [ 11 ] . The model is trained for 200 epochs using SGD with a batch size of 32, an initial learning rate of 0.01 0.01 , momentum of 0.9 0.9 , and weight decay of 10 − 4 10^{-4} . The learning rate follows a polynomial decay schedule with power 0.9 0.9 , and gradients are clipped to a maximum norm of 1.0 1.0 . We employ mixed precision training and standard spatial and intensity augmentations. The checkpoint achieving the highest mean validation Dice is retained for evaluation. Text prompts are encoded using the frozen Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B model [ 27 ] , the smallest member of the Qwen3 embedding family, chosen so that the language pathway contributes negligibly to inference cost; as the encoder is frozen and the prompt set is fixed by the view–structure template, its embeddings are computed once and reused. Identical view-aware prompts are used across all text-based comparison methods. We optimize P2Echo using the Boundary Difference over Union (BDoU) loss [ 28 ] , which extends region overlap optimization by placing greater emphasis on mismatched regions near object boundaries. Given a predicted mask Y ^ \hat{Y} and its corresponding ground truth G G , the BDoU loss is formulated as ℒ BDoU = | G ∪ Y ^ | − | G ∩ Y ^ | | G ∪ Y ^ | − α ​ | G ∩ Y ^ | , \mathcal{L}_{\text{BDoU}}=\frac{|G\cup\hat{Y}|-|G\cap\hat{Y}|}{|G\cup\hat{Y}|-\alpha|G\cap\hat{Y}|}, (5) where α \alpha controls the relative emphasis placed on boundary agreement, following [ 28 ] ; α \alpha is adaptively determined from the ratio between the target boundary and its foreground area. We additionally apply deep supervision to the main prediction and two intermediate decoder outputs, encouraging accurate segmentation throughout the decoding process. III-C Evaluation Metrics Segmentation performance is evaluated using the Dice Similarity Coefficient (mDice), Intersection over Union (mIoU), and the 95th percentile Hausdorff Distance (HD95). For a predicted binary mask Y ^ \hat{Y} and its corresponding ground-truth mask G G , Dice, and IoU are defined as Dice ( Y ^ , G ) = 2 ​ | Y ^ ∩ G | | Y ^ | + | G | , IoU ( Y ^ , G ) = | Y ^ ∩ G | | Y ^ ∪ G | . \displaystyle\mathrm{Dice}(\hat{Y},G)=\frac{2|\hat{Y}\cap G|}{|\hat{Y}|+|G|},\qquad\mathrm{IoU}(\hat{Y},G)=\frac{|\hat{Y}\cap G|}{|\hat{Y}\cup G|}. (6) HD95 measures boundary disagreement and is computed as the 95th percentile of the bidirectional minimum distances between the predicted and ground-truth boundaries. Higher Dice and IoU indicate greater regional overlap, whereas lower HD95 indicates more accurate boundary localization. It is noteworthy that Dice scores of 0 0 from failed class predictions are retained in the average, whereas infinite HD95 values are excluded. Thus, methods that completely miss a structure, such as MYO on HMC_QU, are penalized in Dice but not equivalently in HD95, which slightly favors the baselines relative to P2Echo. III-D Results III-D 1 Internal Multi-View Evaluation We evaluate the performance of P2Echo on the internal test split and compare it with traditional models such as nnUNet [ 21 ] , SAM-based models such as SAMUS [ 7 ] and EchoONE [ 1 ] , and text-guided approaches such as SAM3 [ 26 ] , MedCLIP-SAMv2 [ 9 ] , and Grounding DINO-US-SAM [ 10 ] ; see Table III . P2Echo achieves strong segmentation accuracy across all views, with improved Dice, IoU, and HD95 on most views. Compared to EchoONE [ 1 ] , the only prior work targeting multi-view echo segmentation, P2Echo improves on most views (+5.3 Dice on 2CH, +1.1 on 3CH, +2.8 on 4CH; comparable on PSAX), using a single set of parameters rather than per-plane weights or learnable dense prompts. This is consistent with view- and structure-conditioned prompting providing an effective, explicitly specifiable alternative to per-view specialization. III-D 2 External Held-Out Generalization To assess the generalization and robustness to domain shifts from different scanners and acquisition protocols, we evaluate P2Echo on three external datasets in a held-out setting. As shown in Table IV , P2Echo maintains high performance and outperforms text-based baselines such as SAM3 [ 26 ] , MedCLIP-SAMv2 [ 9 ] , and Grounding DINO-US-SAM [ 10 ] . The substantially lower performance of generic foundation model baselines indicates limited transfer to the echocardiographic domain without task-specific adaptation. As illustrated in Figure 4 , baseline methods struggle with less consistently represented structures, such as myocardium and right-sided chambers, producing myocardial boundary errors in 2CH and incomplete right-ventricular or right-atrial masks in 4CH. In particular, EchoONE confuses the LV cavity with the myocardial wall in the shown 2CH case, with multiclass fusion further degrading myocardial delineation, and leaves a hole near the LV apex in 3CH. Residual cavity boundary errors are also visible in PSAX. In contrast, P2Echo delineates cavities more consistently across these examples, including challenging apical cases, although mild oversegmentation remains at the basal LV boundary in 3CH. This behavior aligns with the view-aware prompts, which provide view-specific shape and location cues that help guide segmentation in low-contrast or signal-dropout regions. Figure 5 (a) demonstrates P2Echo’s prompt-driven flexibility by generating anatomically plausible masks for targets that are not annotated in the available ground truth. Finally, to verify the effect of DyITA, we visualize feature maps before and after applying the DyITA block in Figure 5 (b). The maps before DyITA are diffuse and not well localized to the target region. After DyITA, activations are more concentrated on the prompted anatomy, which is consistent with sharper boundaries and improved contouring in the final predictions. III-D 4 Ablation Study We evaluate the contribution of the three DyITA sub-modules through component-wise ablations on HMC_QU ( Table V ). Replacing the dynamic projection with a static linear layer, removing the dynamic measure kernel, and removing the cross-modal differential operator cost 2.18, 1.70, and 2.19 mDice and add 0.39, 0.43, and 0.92 HD95. Removing any one of the three degrades both metrics, and the differential operator accounts for most of the boundary gain, matching its role of suppressing redundancy-driven cross-modal correlations before attention. This decline is backed up visually ( Figure 5 (c)): ablated variants attend more diffusely, which causes noisy activations that bleed into adjacent structures and blur target boundaries, whereas the full DyITA focuses sharply on the intended anatomy. Finally, increasing the text encoder from 0.6B to 4B yields only a 0.07-point mDice improvement while slightly increasing HD95, supporting the lightweight 0.6B encoder used in P2Echo. P2Echo matches or exceeds the strongest baseline across views, with the largest margin on 2CH and parity on PSAX. On the external datasets with natively annotated structures (EchoNet-Dynamic, CardiacNet), P2Echo shows consistent transfer across acquisition domains. The larger margin observed on HMC_QU has a specific explanation: MYO supervision is available only from CAMUS during training, limiting the diversity of domains in which this structure is represented. In heterogeneous datasets with incomplete annotation overlap, conventional segmentation models may implicitly associate the absence of a label with the absence of the corresponding anatomy, resulting in biased predictions toward frequently observed structures [ 29 , 30 ] . This effect is evident in HMC_QU, where baseline models show severe MYO degradation (e.g., nnUNet) while maintaining comparable LV segmentation. In contrast, P2Echo preserves more coherent myocardial segmentation, suggesting that explicit structure-aware conditioning helps mitigate annotation-space bias under domain shift. Crucially, P2Echo attains these results at 6.90 GFLOPs and 64.68 FPS, 4–160× more efficient than the promptable baselines, making a single controllable network a practical replacement for multiple view-specific specialists. The ablation results support the contribution of both textual conditioning and DyITA. The prompt intervention demonstrates sensitivity to prompt formulation, highlighting the importance of structured textual conditioning. Removing individual DyITA components consistently reduces performance, confirming the importance of the proposed dynamic alignment strategy. Several limitations remain. First, the current experiments focus on four standard echocardiographic views and assume that the view identity is available when constructing the prompt. Therefore, the present results do not establish generalization to previously unseen views. Second, the source datasets contain heterogeneous annotation sets and uneven view coverage. In particular, 3CH and PSAX supervision is available from only one development dataset each, reflecting the limited availability of publicly accessible segmentation datasets for these views. Despite this limited source diversity, P2Echo achieves strong performance on both 3CH and PSAX, suggesting robust learning across the available multi-view supervision. Finally, the current evaluation focuses on segmentation accuracy and not downstream clinical measurements. Future work will extend P2Echo to additional echocardiographic views and investigate whether improved segmentation translates into more accurate and reproducible cardiac measurements and functional assessment. V Conclusion We present P2Echo, a unified text-prompted framework for multi-view echocardiography segmentation. By conditioning a single network on structured view- and structure-aware prompts through our DyITA module, P2Echo suppresses cross-modal redundancy and sharpens boundaries while remaining explicitly controllable. Experiments demonstrate strong internal accuracy and competitive external results across diverse datasets at substantially lower computational cost than comparable promptable models, offering an efficient, controllable single-model solution for multi-plane cardiac assessment.
