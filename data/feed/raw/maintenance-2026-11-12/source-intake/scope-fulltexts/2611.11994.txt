We present Emo Harness , which runs such a model, the frozen student , inside a harness : the prompts, settings, and verification code around its calls. A human-written starting harness, the scaffold , numbers the student’s visual observations, transcript, and prosody descriptions as citable evidence and has the student weigh candidate emotions against it. In Meta-Harness Evolving (MHE), a stronger LLM, the Builder , reads the student’s errors and execution traces offline and edits the harness; static checks and validation gates decide which edits are kept, and the Builder never sees evaluation items. On 5,507 MME-Emotion items, Emo Harness raises task-macro Recognition by 7.6–13.4 points over a single video-only call for all four students and both Builders. Transcripts and prosody descriptions supply most of this boost ( + 5.38 +5.38 to + 10.71 +10.71 ); the scaffold changes the audio-augmented call by − 0.79 -0.79 to + 2.54 +2.54 , partly through extra multi-label predictions, and evolved edits add at most + 0.95 +0.95 , although no evolved increment survives Holm correction. The harness’s emotion context also transfers to IntentQA, with small gains for three of four students, none of which survives correction. (c) Task-macro Recognition contrasts (VL: Qwen3-VL; Q3.5: Qwen3.5-4B). 1 Introduction When someone says “That’s great,” a pause or a tense expression may reflect sarcasm rather than satisfaction. Understanding emotion requires interpreting words, voice, and visual behavior together, particularly when these cues conflict ( Bagher Zadeh et al., 2018 ; Shen et al., 2026 ) .
Misreading them can have consequences beyond an incorrect label: a system may misunderstand a person’s intent, respond inappropriately to a social interaction, or overlook a patient’s indirectly expressed fear or discomfort ( Li et al., 2023 ; Wilf et al., 2023 ; Han et al., 2026 ) .
For smaller multimodal models, transcripts and prosody descriptions supplement visual observations with evidence about what is said and how it is delivered ( Cao et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2026d ; Li et al., 2026b ) . But interpreting these sources together remains challenging, especially when they suggest different emotions. We therefore ask whether another AI can help a small multimodal student use this evidence more effectively without updating the student’s weights. Existing emotion models adapt weights through instruction tuning or reinforcement learning ( Cheng et al., 2024b ; Zhao et al., 2025 ) . An alternative is to adapt frozen systems through model-generated instructions and program optimization ( Zhou et al., 2023 ; Opsahl-Ong et al., 2024 ; Hu et al., 2025 ) .
We build on this approach but direct adaptation toward
a student’s recurring emotion-label failures and cross-modal conflicts. We introduce Emo Harness (Figure 1 ), an AI-for-AI (AI4AI) framework that adapts emotion inference through Meta-Harness Evolving (MHE). An offline Builder examines emotion-label confusions, cross-modal conflicts, and execution traces, and then proposes reusable edits to prompts, execution settings, and restricted verification code. The frozen student organizes visual observations, transcripts, and prosody descriptions into indexed textual evidence, proposes alternative emotional interpretations, and reviews their support and contradictions. Checks and validation select a harness that is frozen before evaluation. If no edit passes, the human-written scaffold is retained. The Builder does not answer evaluation questions. To assess the contribution of Builder-guided adaptation, we compare the evolved harness ( MHE ) with its initial human-written version ( Shared ) and with single-call prediction from video supplemented with transcripts and prosody descriptions ( Audio ). Comparing Audio with single-call video-only prediction ( Video ) measures the effect of adding this audio-derived evidence. We also examine transfer to task types excluded from harness construction and test whether emotion context generated without access to downstream questions helps intent question answering. Across four students and two Builders, Emo Harness improves task-macro Recognition on 5,507 MME-Emotion items by 7.6–13.4 points over Video. Most of this improvement comes from transcripts and prosody descriptions: Audio outperforms Video by 5.38–10.71 points, whereas Builder-guided adaptation produces much smaller changes relative to Shared ( − 0.07 -0.07 to + 0.95 +0.95 points). Adding the harness’s emotion context also yields small observed gains for three of four students on IntentQA. In summary, we introduce Emo Harness , a failure-driven harness construction framework, which boosts frozen multimodal students on emotion recognition and transfers its emotion context to intent question answering. In addition, we conduct a systematic analysis of where the boost comes from and what limits harness evolution. We believe our work paves the way for more adaptive and generalizable multimodal systems that can systematically learn from failures, improve without updating model parameters, and transfer acquired capabilities across related tasks. 2 Related Work Multimodal emotion understanding. Emotion-LLaMA and R1-Omni adapt model weights through instruction tuning and reinforcement learning, respectively ( Cheng et al., 2024b ; Zhao et al., 2025 ) ; Emo Harness instead adapts the inference program around frozen weights. We evaluate on MME-Emotion ( Zhang et al., 2026 ) and reuse emotion context on IntentQA ( Li et al., 2023 ) . Following the multi-dimensional evaluation of HEMM ( Liang et al., 2024 ) , we separate recognizing an emotion from producing useful downstream context. Optimizing prompts, programs, and harnesses. APE, OPRO, and ProTeGi search instructions with model-generated proposals or feedback ( Zhou et al., 2023 ; Yang et al., 2024 ; Pryzant et al., 2023 ) ; DSPy and MIPRO optimize multi-stage programs ( Khattab et al., 2024 ; Opsahl-Ong et al., 2024 ) ; TextGrad and Trace learn from textual feedback and execution traces ( Yuksekgonul et al., 2025 ; Cheng et al., 2024a ; Xu et al., 2026 ) ; and ADAS and AFlow search code-defined agents ( Hu et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2025 ) . Meta-Harness exposes prior code, scores, and traces to a proposer ( Lee et al., 2026 ) , automated harness adaptation can target the failures of smaller models ( Yang et al., 2026 ) , and multi-agent and self-evolving systems learn from feedback ( Li et al., 2025 ; Li et al., 2026c ; Li et al., 2026a ) . MHE applies this line of work to multimodal emotion recognition: a student’s emotion confusions and cross-modal conflicts drive reusable edits, a separate validation split gates every promotion, and the evolved harness is frozen and reused, unlike programs generated for each request ( Gupta and Kembhavi, 2023 ) . Feedback and procedural adaptation. Self-Refine revises single outputs and Reflexion carries feedback across trials ( Madaan et al., 2023 ; Shinn et al., 2023 ) ; Emo Harness separates the student’s per-instance review from the Builder’s offline edits. Self-correction can fail ( Huang et al., 2024 ) , agent errors can cascade across steps ( Zhu et al., 2025 ) , and explanations need not reflect decisions ( Turpin et al., 2023 ) . We therefore score harnesses only on items the Builder never saw, report validation and evaluation gains separately, and count student calls ( Kapoor et al., 2025 ) . 3 Emo Harness : Meta-Harness Evolving for Emotion Understanding A harness is a bundle of prompts, settings files, and verification code. The human-written scaffold H 0 H_{0} ( Shared ) uses four student calls. Observe extracts facial, bodily, scene, and temporal cues from the video, decoded at 2 fps; deterministic assembly indexes them ( v 1 , v 2 , … v_{1},v_{2},\ldots ) along with transcript sentences and prosody phrases ( a 1 , a 2 , … a_{1},a_{2},\ldots ), and later calls see only this textual record. Propose lists candidate labels with confidences and with identifiers of supporting and contradicting evidence; deterministic code checks the identifiers against the record and then ranks the candidates. Review re-examines the ranked candidates, and its output is verified the same way. A fixed controller then selects at most three labels from the vocabulary. It prefers candidates whose valid support exceeds their contradiction and fills any remaining slots from the ranking, so it always returns an answer, even one without valid support. Explain then writes an explanation that cannot change the labels. Each malformed call may be retried once, under a cap of six calls (Appendix B ). 3.3 Meta-Harness Evolving MHE evolves the harness over T = 4 T=4 generations. In each, the Builder proposes candidate edits, and gates either promote one candidate or keep the current harness. MHE inherits from Meta-Harness ( Lee et al., 2026 ) a proposer that reads prior harness code and execution traces. It differs in three respects. First, the Builder sees the failures of one frozen student, clustered by gold–prediction confusion and by cross-modal conflict; a deterministic check flags a conflict when the student’s visual and audio-derived citations favor different labels. Second, gates on a separate validation split decide promotion, and the Builder sees neither validation scores nor gate decisions, only the resulting harness. Third, each run keeps one current harness, the incumbent , giving a chain H 0 → ⋯ → H T H_{0}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow H_{T} . Variation. The incumbent runs on a construction split 𝒟 b \mathcal{D}_{b} , whose failures the Builder may read. Given the incumbent’s files, intermediate records, clustered failures, and earlier proposals with check and reviewer feedback, the Builder proposes up to four candidate harnesses, each an edit aimed at one failure cluster; evaluation data are never exposed. Selection. Candidates must pass file-scope, schema, loadability, and static-code checks, as well as a proposal reviewer that rejects, for example, edits that force a label regardless of evidence or add unsupported settings; these are filters, not guarantees. Algorithm B (Appendix B ) summarizes the loop. 4 Experimental Setup We ask what contributes to recognition (RQ1), whether evolved edits transfer to held-out task types (RQ2), and whether the harness’s emotion context helps intent question answering (RQ3). 4.1 Students, Builders, and Data Models. We serve Qwen3-VL-8B, Qwen3-VL-4B ( Bai et al., 2025 ) , Qwen3.5-4B ( Qwen Team, 2026 ) , and Gemma-4-E4B ( Gemma Team, 2026 ) with vLLM ( Kwon et al., 2023 ) at temperature zero, with thinking disabled for Qwen3.5 and Gemma. Audio reaches every student only as transcript and prosody text. Each student’s harness is evolved separately by GPT-5.6 Luna and by GPT-6.1 Sol; each Builder also acts as its own proposal reviewer, and GPT-5.6 Luna scores the reasoning proxy. Each student–Builder pair has one four-generation run. A third Builder configuration, which changes no conclusion, is described in Appendix A . Data. Construction and validation use 399 and 394 MME-Emotion items ( Zhang et al., 2026 ) from three task types (ER-Lab, ER-Wild: lab and in-the-wild emotion recognition; SA: sentiment analysis). Evaluation uses 5,507 further items, namely the benchmark minus every item whose media overlap construction, validation, or a reserved 129-item set. The evaluation set contains 2,963 items in the seen types and 2,544 in five held-out types (noisy, fine-grained, and multi-label emotion recognition: Noise-ER, FG-ER, ML-ER; fine-grained sentiment: FG-SA; intent recognition: IR). Held-out refers to task type, not to an untouched test set: 986 evaluation items, which we cannot identify, were inspected during development. Inspection could have influenced any human design choice, most plausibly the scaffold, but not the Builder’s edits directly: the Builder never saw evaluation items (Appendix A ). 4.2 Conditions and Metrics Video prompts the student once with the video, and Audio adds transcript and prosody text to that call. Shared runs the four-call scaffold H 0 H_{0} , and MHE runs the frozen evolved harness H ⋆ H^{\star} . Recognition, computed locally without an LLM judge, scores the first normalized prediction for single-label requests and the fraction of gold labels recovered for multi-label requests, a recall-like score that ignores extra labels. No experiment therefore compares a scaffold context with a substantively evolved one, so these changes cannot be attributed to evolution. 6 Discussion and Conclusion When does MHE help? The largest gain, GPT-5.6 Luna on Qwen3-VL-4B, occurs for the only student whose scaffold scores below Audio. Its edits add 0.95 points, mostly on held-out types ( + 1.24 +1.24 versus + 0.47 +0.47 on seen types). They change the label set on 1,189 items (21.6%): 281 improve, 233 worsen, and 675 score the same. Three findings suggest why the other gains are small. First, each promotion picks the best of up to four candidates on 394 items, so the winner’s validation gain tends to overstate its gain on new items (Section 5.1 ). The Recognition gate also requires no margin: three of the eleven promotions rest on a single validation item (0.25 points). Second, the regression gate blocked every large validation gain for both Builders: all twelve candidates that gained at least eight validation items (2.03 points) exceeded the 5% regression budget. Nine were for Qwen3.5-4B, whose prompt edits change the correctness of 56 construction items on average, against 23–27 for the other students. Third, the other evolved harnesses are conservative: each changes the label set on fewer than 4% of evaluation items. The regression gate protects solved cases at the cost of broad edits; whether a looser gate generalizes better needs a separate test set. Edits are instructions the student may not follow. In Figure 4 a the added rule moves the trace toward a neutral alternative. In Figure 4 b the rule written for the Neutral → \rightarrow Frustration confusion is ignored: Neutral is never named. These selected cases suggest that the student’s instruction following, not only the Builder’s proposals, can limit what edits achieve. Future gates could check that traces apply edits and that citations match the media. Emotion context transfers modestly to intent QA. The gains for three students are small, and none survives Holm correction (Table 2 ). Qwen3.5-4B, the one student that loses, also loses 2.06 points when only transcript and prosody are added. Its loss may therefore come from added text rather than from emotion content; this student also uses a different answer format (Section 4.3 ). Conditioning the context on the question could strengthen transfer but would reduce reuse across questions. Conclusion. Emo Harness boosts Recognition by 7.6–13.4 points over Video for every student. Most of the boost comes from audio-derived evidence (Audio − - Video: 5.38–10.71 points). Edits evolved by MHE add at most 0.95 points over Shared, and none survives Holm correction. The harness’s emotion context transfers to IntentQA with small, nonsignificant gains for three of four students. Limitations Evidence base. The media-overlap filter that defines the evaluation split was applied once and has not been independently re-verified. Part of the evaluation set was inspected during development, so held-out results describe unseen task types rather than an untouched test set. The 129 reserved examples remain unevaluated. We score Recognition against gold labels without the benchmark’s LLM judge, so our values may differ from published MME-Emotion results, and we do not report its Reasoning and Chain-of-Thought scores. Construction. Each student–Builder pair has one construction trajectory. The two Builders were run about three weeks apart, and the later runs used a slightly extended diagnostic packet. Four generations do not imply matched proposal budgets or compute (Appendix D ). The same model acts as Builder and proposal reviewer, and GPT-5.6 Luna also scores the reasoning proxy for both Builders’ candidates. The gate values are fixed design choices that we did not vary. Fixed-harness intervals do not capture construction variance, and nonsignificant corrected tests do not establish equivalence. We do not compare against existing optimizers (e.g., MIPRO, TextGrad, or Meta-Harness itself). Prompt-only, evidence-separated, and compute-matched controls are absent, as are Shared-generated contexts except for Qwen3.5-4B, whose GPT-5.6 Luna harness retained the scaffold. Measurement. Visual information can be lost when observations are converted to text. Acoustic descriptions depend on reference statistics, speakers, languages, and recording conditions, and the lack of diarization limits multi-speaker interpretation. MME-Emotion clips include English and Mandarin Chinese speech, and we do not analyze results by language. The three-label cap constrains recall, while recall-like Recognition does not penalize excess labels and favors the scaffold on multi-label tasks (Appendix C ). We do not report Recognition separately on items with cross-modal conflicts, so whether edits help where they are aimed is untested. Valid citations do not establish grounding or faithful explanations, fallback logic can complete unsupported answers, and static checks and model reviewers do not guarantee correct verification code. The 64-token answer cap leaves some downstream replies unparsed, and Qwen3.5-4B instead answers under a 1,024-token cap with a final-answer parser, so answer formatting differs across students. Ethical Considerations Benchmark emotion labels are interpretations of behavior, not direct measurements of private mental states or clinical conditions. This work does not validate use in diagnosis, employment screening, surveillance, or educational assessment. Cultural, linguistic, and demographic variation requires separate analysis with suitable annotations. Dataset access and redistribution must follow source terms. Videos, transcripts, and construction diagnostics may identify individuals; external model processing and released traces must respect applicable permissions, and the frames reproduced in our case-study figures come from public research datasets, with on-screen names removed. We use MME-Emotion (MIT license) and IntentQA (whose repository states no license) for non-commercial research evaluation only; source videos remain subject to their original terms. The student models are released under Apache 2.0, and Whisper and faster-whisper (MIT), openSMILE (audEERING research license), and vLLM (Apache 2.0) are used under their terms. Algorithm 1: Meta-Harness Evolving. The Builder proposes edits to the harness, and checks and validation gates decide which survive. The Builder never answers evaluation items. B.1 Perception and Evidence Assembly Whisper produces word-timestamped transcripts. Acoustic features are verbalized relative to reference means and standard deviations: an absolute standardized deviation of at least one yields a descriptor. Missing features and zero-variance reference features are omitted, and the descriptions neither identify speakers nor label emotion. Native-video decoding and auxiliary frame-cache sampling are separate paths (Appendix A ). Deterministic assembly assigns identifiers v1 , … \texttt{v1},\ldots to visual observations and a1 , … \texttt{a1},\ldots to transcript sentences and prosody phrases. The request target is stored as t1 but cannot be cited as observed decision evidence. Each hypothesis has a label, a model-generated confidence, supporting identifiers, and contradicting identifiers. Valid identifiers do not establish semantic support. B.2 Output and Failure Handling The controller enforces the requested vocabulary and cardinality, with a three-label cap for multi-label outputs. A candidate normally needs a valid supporting identifier and more support than contradiction. If too few pass, remaining slots are filled from available rankings and labels. This is completion logic, not an evidence guarantee. The Explain call receives the record and verified interpretations and writes context, facial and bodily cues, vocal and verbal cues, and a cross-modal synthesis with the strongest alternative. The controller sanitizes citations and appends a decision section. With upstream outputs fixed, the explanation cannot change the labels, but it can change the downstream context. One retry per malformed stage is permitted under the shared six-call cap; once the cap is reached, the remaining stages are skipped and the output is completed from available information. B.3 Edit Spaces and Selection History All runs search the full edit space (existing prompts, YAML settings, and restricted verification files) directly from Shared. Each proposal supplies complete replacements for the allowed files and names its target construction-failure cluster. The solved set 𝒮 t \mathcal{S}_{t} (Eq. 2 ) grows with each promotion. The proposal history contains proposals, construction effects, and check and reviewer feedback; validation scores and gate decisions are withheld, although validation still decides which incumbent supplies the next diagnostics. MHE obtains reasoning-proxy scores for the initial harness and for candidates that pass the cheaper recognition, regression, and call-budget gates before applying Eq. 3 . Static checks and the proposal reviewer constrain candidates but cannot guarantee their safety or semantic correctness. Reasoning proxy used for selection. We evaluate Q ^ v \widehat{Q}_{v} on a fixed 200-item sample from 𝒟 v \mathcal{D}_{v} . The judge receives each response, up to ten cached frames, and gold labels; the benchmark’s judge-side audio-clue field is left empty. Its rubric gives binary scores to non-final reasoning steps, marking clear contradictions with the supplied cues as incorrect. The per-item proxy is the mean of these non-final scores, or zero if none exist; Q ^ v \widehat{Q}_{v} averages the per-item values. The final decision-step score is excluded. Following the LLM-as-judge protocol of Zheng et al. (2023) , the judge is GPT-5.6 Luna. The proxy does not measure official reasoning performance or explanation faithfulness. Appendix G Controls for Future Work The controls below would sharpen the comparisons in this paper. Evidence and computation. Transcript-only and prosody-only conditions would separate the sources of the audio gain. A one-call structured prediction using the same tools and controller would test decomposition against a direct call that uses the same output contract. Repeated direct samples with a fixed aggregation rule would test the value of additional calls. Token and call counts must be measured rather than presumed matched. Giving the decision stages access to the media would measure how much information is lost when observations are converted to text. Construction and context. Comparing prompt- or configuration-only search with full-bundle search requires matched splits and budgets. Evaluating the twelve candidates that the regression gate rejected despite gains of at least eight validation items, or rerunning selection with a looser δ \delta , would test whether the gate rather than the proposals limits MHE. Independent construction repetitions are necessary to assess search variability. Shared-generated contexts for every student are needed to test the downstream increment from evolution. Length-matched visual captions and non-emotion summaries would separate emotion-specific utility from generic additional text.
