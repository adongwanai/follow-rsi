Every declared plan is scored by the proposal statistic at its leap policy, the plan mixed with ε = 0.001 \varepsilon=0.001 of the reference, as the mean over the 6,400 6{,}400 proposal trajectories minus two standard errors. On question answering and mathematics the 50 top-ranked plans each run on 1,000 1{,}000 fresh validation draws and the three with the largest mean net return become the leaps; on code the three top-ranked plans become the leaps directly. The certificate never reads the screening draws. The expansion serves here as a proposal heuristic for distant plans, so the remainder bound does not apply to the leap ratios, while their certificates stay valid because they use paired draws of their own. Matched budget. The frozen deployed policy is rerun on the test split with a fresh salt and 10 to 200 repeats per problem. On answer tasks a vote takes the most common non-empty answer key over the first K K episodes, with ties to the earliest; on code the episodes split into groups of K K , each reduced to one program by MaAS’s HumanEval-template selector with its tokens added. K K is the largest count whose tokens per vote fit the strongest baseline’s tokens per episode, for every listed seed. The budgets are 28.6k (GSM8K), 53.8k (MATH), 57.1k (DROP) and 26.5k tokens (HumanEval); on MBPP it is 35.4k, below DyLAN’s 121.9k. Statistics.
