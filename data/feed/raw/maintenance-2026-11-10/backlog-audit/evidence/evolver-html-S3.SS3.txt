3.3 Experience Acquisition and Consolidation Experience Sources. Evolver treats historical agent interactions as a source of reusable procedural knowledge, capturing not only task outcomes but also the exploration and feedback that precede them.
When session ingestion is enabled, runtime adapters collect records from supported agent harnesses, including task context, tool interactions, execution errors, and available outcomes.
These records are normalized into bounded experience materials with heuristic sensitive-information redaction.
Additional experience can be acquired through explicit asset submissions and external experience repositories, providing complementary sources beyond automatically collected sessions. Experience Extraction. Given collected experience materials, Evolver identifies lightweight signals associated with successful execution, failures, and task difficulty to construct candidate Gene objects.
The default automated drafting pathway extracts concise procedural hints from assistant utterances and marks them as unproven, rather than assuming that the resulting strategies have been validated.
When enabled, LLM-based distillation further supports structured experience extraction from execution records, including reusable strategies, associated cases, and negative procedural guidance.
These complementary pathways separate low-cost experience collection from more structured synthesis, while leaving the effectiveness of generated strategies subject to subsequent evaluation. Experience Consolidation. Extracted experience is consolidated into persistent, content-addressed assets, with supporting execution cases retained when available.
Automatically drafted and model-distilled candidates are placed under review, whereas certain explicit submission pathways can persist assets directly.
Review and provenance records maintain experience eligibility and source information separately from the underlying strategy content, although admission and trust enforcement depend on the acquisition and retrieval pathways.
By preserving candidate knowledge independently of its current lifecycle state, Evolver establishes a reusable experience base that supports subsequent retrieval and feedback-guided evolution, as detailed in Sections 3.4 and 3.5 .
