Appendix H Environments, Roles and Protocol Benchmarks. Each problem is stored as a target unit and a question in AFlow’s format ( Zhang et al., 2025b ) . Its long input shuffles the target unit among D D units of other problems of the same split, using a generator seeded by a hash of the problem text, so that the document is a fixed function of the problem. Units stay whole and numbered, which is what the evidence finder retrieves. The setting follows GSM-IC ( Shi et al., 2023 ) , carried to document length. The baselines read the whole document, and the team reaches it only through the evidence finder or the generalist; both receive the same question text, including the opening words of the target unit. MATH answers are scored by a matcher that takes the last balanced \boxed{...} from prediction and reference and applies MaAS’s MATH equality (identical strings, numbers within 10 − 3 10^{-3} , or a SymPy difference that simplifies to zero). It replaced a matcher that misread some answers, and every MATH arm was rerun with it. Roles. Question answering and mathematics use eight roles and code adds Test (Table 3 ). Teams run in a pipeline regime with typed interfaces: a worker’s product reaches only its designated consumer at the next decision, and candidates are combined only when the selector is chosen. The evidence finder ranks the numbered units with BM25 ( Robertson and Zaragoza, 2009 ) , and Test is MaAS’s public-test operator as released, with up to three repairs. Which roles are legal depends on the decision index and a few flags, and at the last decision only stopping is legal, which leaves 2,016 open-loop plans at five decisions and 432 at four. The orchestrator observes a MiniLM embedding of the task, the role signals, the previous action, the position and the share of the token budget spent, but never a score.
