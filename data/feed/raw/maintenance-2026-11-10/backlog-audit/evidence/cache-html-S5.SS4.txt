Table 4 compares drop targets and the use of repos \operatorname{repos} , showing that tool-response drop with repos \operatorname{repos} achieves the highest pass rate on both benchmarks.
Adding repos \operatorname{repos} raises BCP’s pass rate from 55% to 62%, allowing exploration beyond the position limit that would otherwise halt execution.
With repos \operatorname{repos} enabled, dropping reasoning cache, either alone or together with tool-response cache, yields lower pass rates on both benchmarks than dropping tool-response cache alone. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) .
Figure 4 shows that RSI finds lower-cost cache policies than our manually designed tool-response rolling-drop policy ( K = 12 K=12 ) while maintaining Standard’s accuracy.
We use MiniMax-M2.7 to refine policies on 20 BCP tasks and evaluate the resulting policies on 100 held-out tasks.
The policies from iterations 3 and 6 reduce per-task cost by 15.6% and 17.8% relative to Standard, respectively. Cache management as a tool .
Exposing cache management as a tool lets agents proactively decide when to reduce the cache and what to retain.
Table 4 shows that this approach achieves the highest pass rate on a 100-task BCP subset for both models: 61.0% for GPT-OSS-120B and 64.0% for MiniMax-M2.7.
Compared with Standard, GPT-OSS-120B’s per-task cost falls from 13.4 to 12.1 cents, while MiniMax-M2.7’s rises from 5.4 to 5.9 cents as it executes more turns.
