M. Leung Affiliation: \institution The University of British Columbia and Stan Z. Li Affiliation: \institution Westlake University Abstract. Weight-space programming (WSP) shows that language models can reason about and
rewrite neural network weights; recursive self-improvement (RSI) motivates
feedback-driven improvement. Models guiding experiments receive concrete
feedback: completed assays reveal prediction errors and inform what to test next.
We introduce WeightLoop, a framework that turns experimental feedback into
persistent updates to deployed predictors. GPT-6 Astra inspects a task
specification and the model implementation, then writes a bounded, reusable
parameter-editing program. As new measurements arrive, the program identifies
prediction discrepancies, validates candidate edits using observed outcomes,
and writes the selected changes back to the original model. The editor remains
fixed while the predictor evolves, enabling repeated updates without further
LLM calls or gradient retraining while preserving the original inference interface.
In offline replay, targeted edits improve mean ranking utility in seven of
eight endpoint–budget settings (all eight versus random edits) and mean final
discovery across three sequential tasks. WeightLoop connects WSP with an RSI-inspired
improvement loop, making experimental evidence a direct driver of model change
and subsequent decisions. Key words and phrases: Scientific recommendation, model editing, experimental feedback, weight-space programming 1. Introduction An experiment does more than test a prediction: it produces evidence that can
change the next decision. In experimental decision-making Settles (2009) ; King et al. (2009) ; Roohani et al. (2025) ,
a predictor ranks candidate interventions, but their effects become known only
after measurement. Historical data provide reusable knowledge, yet a new
objective may have only a few dozen functional measurements. These results can
expose task-specific errors in an otherwise useful model. The challenge is
therefore not only to evaluate predictions, but to incorporate evidence into
the deployed predictor so subsequent decisions reflect what has been learned.
Cellular perturbation research Cui et al. (2024) ; Roohani et al. (2023) and virtual
cells Bunne et al. (2024) provide a demanding example. From model editing to weight-space programming Model-editing research studies targeted corrections to neural
parameters Sinitsin et al. (2020) ; Mitchell et al. (2022) ; De Cao et al. (2021) ; Meng et al. (2022) ; Meng et al. (2023) . Takehi et al. introduced
weight-space programming (WSP), demonstrating language models interpreting and
rewriting weights according to natural-language objectives in specified
settings Takehi et al. (2026) . This opens a possibility: can an LLM program
how a deployed model should change when experimental evidence arrives?
Beyond producing one parameter correction, such a program could connect
successive measurements to the model’s future behavior. Where the improvement loop lives Recursive self-improvement (RSI) motivates systems such as STOP, whose
improvement program revises itself Zelikman et al. (2024) . In WeightLoop, the
sequence is concrete: the LLM writes an editor once; each returned assay batch
lets that editor revise the predictor; the revised predictor recommends the
next batch. The editor code and LLM remain fixed during this loop. New measured
outcomes determine the parameter changes, without another LLM call or gradient
retraining. The scientific predictor is the object of this improvement loop. The WeightLoop workflow Given a task objective and model, GPT-6 Astra OpenAI (2026) identifies task-relevant mutable parameters and writes a bounded editing program.
When feedback arrives, the program evaluates discrepancies between predictions
and measurements, constructs candidate changes, and selects a validated update.
Changes are written into the checkpoint, which reloads through its original
inference interface. The updated predictor guides the next decision, and the
process repeats as measurements arrive (Figure 1 ). Feedback
therefore changes the model itself, beyond adjusting one recommendation list. We study two complementary settings: single-round ranking and sequential
discovery. Cellular growth, migration,
autophagy, and LDL-uptake measurements supply concrete feedback across objectives.
Here, biology supplies measurable outcomes for testing a reusable editing workflow.
Neural, linear, and tree-based predictors offer different parameter structures
for the same workflow. Recorded measurements enable offline replay to test
whether updates improve subsequent decisions and remain usable after checkpoint
writeback and reload. Together, these experiments demonstrate how WSP can turn
returned measurements into an ongoing source of model improvement. Figure 1. WeightLoop separates one-time program construction from repeated

The model code separates a reusable molecular encoder from a linear functional
readout: changing one readout weight alters that feature’s contribution while
preserving both the representation and the RNA auxiliary branches. This structure
also makes a candidate change exactly computable from cached activations, so the
editor can test individual connections without backpropagation. Its diagnostic
asks whether weakening a connection improves the ordering of measured genes;
its validation combines a pairwise ranking loss with top-budget utility.
For coefficient and leaf-value updates, the implemented objective is instead
residual prediction error. Thus the program construction ties the mutable
interface and validation criterion to the model structure and task objective.

The update therefore persists in the predictor and serves subsequent queries
through the original inference interface. 4. Conclusion WeightLoop converts experimental feedback into persistent model updates.
A fixed, LLM-constructed editor selects parameter changes from observed
measurements, and the updated predictor guides the next decision. Ranking,
sequential acquisition, and multiple parameter interfaces demonstrate how
WSP connects accumulating scientific evidence to future experimental choices
through the original prediction interface. Ethical Considerations The study uses public functional-screen measurements, with no new human-subject
experiments or personal data collection. Recommendations guide research assays;
biological efficacy requires validation in the intended setting.
