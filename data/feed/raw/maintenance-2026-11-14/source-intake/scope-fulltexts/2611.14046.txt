Affiliation: Xi’an Jiaotong-Liverpool University Affiliation: China Email: Xiaobo.Jin@xjtlu.edu.cn Abstract Adversarial training is often plagued by “pseudo-attacks”—perturbations that significantly increase the loss value but fail to truly alter the model’s confidence structure. To address this, we propose two complementary approaches to directly attack and defend against confidence structure deformation. First, building upon the traditional loss maximization perspective, we introduce a hybrid objective that integrates cross-entropy and KL divergence, encouraging adversarial examples to simultaneously disrupt prediction accuracy and distribution consistency. Second, we geometrically reconstruct adversarial training by generating perturbations that maximize the distribution distance on the confidence manifold. Theoretical analysis shows that the optimal perturbation direction aligns with the principal eigenvectors of the Fisher information matrix. We also propose an efficient algorithm to avoid high-dimensional matrix computations. Experiments on multiple datasets and model architectures demonstrate that our method outperforms existing methods in adversarial robustness and robust transferability, particularly excelling in structural perturbations within high-confidence regions. Code repository: https://github.com/wnzgy/SCP . 1 Introduction Deep neural networks (DNNs) have achieved remarkable progress across numerous applications; however, despite their impressive performance, they remain highly susceptible to adversarial perturbations which conduct imperceptible modifications to inputs that cause incorrect predictions [ 1 ] . This vulnerability has raised significant concerns in safety–critical domains such as image recognition [ 2 ] , autonomous driving [ 3 ] , and anomaly detection [ 4 ] . Among various defense strategies, adversarial training has emerged as one of the most effective methods for improving model robustness [ 5 , 6 ] . By jointly optimizing over clean and adversarial samples, it encourages models to learn stable decision boundaries under input perturbations. Existing adversarial training methods are commonly grouped into gradient-based approaches, optimization-based formulations, and attack-driven adversarial sample generation. Gradient-based methods such as FGSM and PGD [ 7 , 8 ] construct perturbations by following the loss gradient, offering high computational efficiency but ensuring robustness only in a local linear neighborhood. Optimization-based defenses treat adversarial training as a min–max problem and explicitly minimize the worst-case loss under bounded perturbations, often using solvers like L-BFGS [ 9 ] or zero-order optimization [ 10 ] ; however, their computational overhead is typically prohibitive for large-scale models. Meanwhile, adversarial attack–based methods [ 11 ] generate perturbed samples directly by designing black-box or white-box attacks: black-box methods rely solely on model outputs, whereas white-box attacks exploit full model gradients and internal structures. Prevailing adversarial training methods largely share a common heuristic: they craft perturbations by maximizing the classification loss with respect to the true label. While effective in reducing the predicted probability of the true class, this loss-centric paradigm often fails to induce meaningful, global changes in the model’s output distribution. As illustrated in Fig. 1 , even when the loss is increased, the relative ordering of confidence across classes may remain intact—a phenomenon we term pseudo-attacks. We define a "pseudo-attack" as a perturbation δ \delta satisfying ℒ ⁡ ( x + δ , y ) > ℒ ⁡ ( x , y ) \mathcal{L}(x+\delta,y)>\mathcal{L}(x,y) but arg ⁡ max k ⁡ p ⁡ ( y = k | x + δ ) = arg ⁡ max k ⁡ p ⁡ ( y = k | x ) \arg\max_{k}p(y=k|x+\delta)=\arg\max_{k}p(y=k|x) . Even if the top-1 remains unchanged, the relative confidence ranking and distribution shape can still maintain local stability, meaning that the geometric vulnerability of the model near the decision boundary is not truly challenged. Such perturbations do not genuinely challenge the structural geometry of the model’s confidence manifold, leaving underlying vulnerabilities exposed. To move beyond local loss maximization, we argue that adversarial robustness should be grounded in the geometric stability of the model’s confidence distribution. In this paper, we introduce a unified adversarial training framework that operates directly on the statistical manifold of output probabilities.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows: ∙ \bullet We propose an enhanced Single Point Perturbation (SPP) strategy that combines cross-entropy (CE) and Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence into a hybrid objective. Unlike prior uses of KL as a regularizer [ 12 ] , we deploy it actively during attack generation to disrupt both the top-1 prediction and the overall confidence structure. ∙ \bullet We develop a SCP framework that reformulates adversarial search as maximizing the distance between probability distributions on the confidence manifold. We show theoretically that the optimal perturbation aligns with the principal eigenvector of the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM), which captures the local curvature of the manifold. To enable scalable computation, we derive an efficient eigen-decomposition that avoids explicit manipulation of high-dimensional FIM. ∙ \bullet Extensive experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet demonstrate that our methods consistently improve adversarial robustness and enhance attack transferability across architectures. By directly targeting the geometric roots of model vulnerability, SCP and SPP advance the state of the art in adversarial training, offering a principled and computationally feasible path toward truly robust deep learning systems. 2 Related work Adversarial Training and Geometry. Adversarial training (AT) has evolved from a local noise defense strategy into a holistic paradigm that reshapes the decision manifold of deep neural networks. Early mainstream gradient-based methods, such as FGSM [ 7 ] and PGD [ 8 ] , primarily constructed adversarial perturbations within a local ϵ \epsilon -sphere along the direction of steepest loss increase. While effective, their robustness was often limited to these direct input neighborhoods. To seek more globally stable solutions, optimization-based frameworks [ 8 , 12 ] modeled adversarial training as a minimax game. Although subsequent acceleration strategies, such as FreeAT [ 13 ] , YOPO [ 14 ] , improved training efficiency, they essentially still followed the same loss-centric heuristic. In recent years, some methods, including feature scattering [ 15 ] and MART [ 16 ] , have attempted to learn more transferable features through feature space alignment or hard example reweighting, but they often neglect the underlying geometry of the data manifold. Many existing approaches rely on maximizing the cross-entropy (CE) loss to measure adversarial vulnerability, while methods such as TRADES maximize distributional discrepancy during attack generation. This approach is essentially a “pointwise suppression” of the true logit, which typically fails to induce a global distortion of the model’s confidence structure. While TRADES [ 12 ] and ensemble-based approaches [ 17 ] introduce KL divergence or diversity constraints, they generally treat distributional disparity as a scalar penalty rather than a structural measure. Our work breaks with this convention by redefining adversarial perturbations as geometric transformations on the confidence manifold and asserting that true robustness stems from the manifold’s geometric stability under structural stress. Information-Theoretic Perspectives. The application of KL divergence in adversarial training has traditionally been limited to its use as a scalar measure of distributional disparity. A common practice is to use it as a consistency regularization term to balance the tradeoff between natural accuracy and adversarial robustness (e.g., TRADES [ 12 ] or gradient-smoothing approaches [ 18 ] ). Another perspective stems from knowledge distillation, where KL divergence guides distribution alignment [ 19 ] , thereby facilitating the learning of robust representations. However, most of these paradigms overlook the profound informational geometric meaning of KL divergence. Within the informational geometry framework, the KL divergence between two neighboring probability distributions is equivalent to a second-order approximation of the Mahalanobis distance defined by the Fisher information matrix (FIM). Existing defense methods utilize its size for constraint but fail to fully leverage its potential as a tool for discovering structured vulnerabilities. Our research bridges this gap by transforming KL divergence from a passive regularization term into an active target for attack generation. By maximizing the manifold distance along the Fisher principal eigenvectors, we can systematically disrupt the global structure of the confidence distribution, rather than merely suppressing local outputs, forcing the model to learn a more resilient geometric representation and effectively overcoming the evaluation blind spots inherent in traditional paradigms. 3 Methodology Traditional adversarial training generates perturbations by maximizing the loss function (e.g., cross-entropy CE), but such methods only locally suppress the logit of the true class, ignoring the global geometry of the confidence distribution (Fig. 1 ). We propose that true robustness should stem from the model’s stability on the confidence manifold, i.e., the input perturbation should maximize the structural deformation of the output probability distribution. To this end, we design two complementary strategies: ∙ \bullet Local Single-Point Perturbation (SPP): Jointly optimizes CE and KL divergence, simultaneously disrupting prediction accuracy and distribution consistency; ∙ \bullet Global Structured Confidence Perturbation (SCP): From an information geometry perspective, it generates perturbations using the principal eigendirections of the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM), directly manipulating the curvature of the confidence manifold. 3.1 Geometric motivation: from single points to manifold deformation Let the model be f θ : 𝒳 → R K f_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow R^{K} , where θ \theta denotes the parameters and K K is the number of classes. The softmax output p ⁡ ( y | x ; θ ) p(y|x;\theta) defines a statistical manifold. Each input x x corresponds to a point on ℙ \mathbb{P} , and a perturbation δ \delta moves x x to x ′ = x + δ x^{\prime}=x+\delta , inducing a trajectory on the manifold. CIFAR-10 contains 60,000 color images of size 32 × \times 32 in 10 categories. CIFAR-100 contains 60,000 color images of size 32 × \times 32 in 100 categories. Tiny-ImageNet is a subset of ImageNet [ 22 ] with 200 categories, similar to the ImageNet dataset, where each category contains 500 color images of size 64 × \times 64. To comprehensively evaluate the relationship between model geometry and attack resistance, we conduct experiments on representative architectures with distinct structural characteristics: ∙ \bullet ResNet [ 23 ] : It introduces residual connections, allowing gradients to bypass nonlinear layers directly. Skip connections stabilize the gradient flow, reduce gradient vanishing/explosion, and reduce variance, so its maximum eigenvalue is relatively low. ∙ \bullet DenseNet [ 24 ] : Each layer of DenseNet receives the output of all previous layers as input. With more gradient paths and gradients dispersed to each layer, its variance is potentially the smallest among the considered models. ∙ \bullet ViT [ 25 ] : Vision Transformer (ViT) is based on the self-attention mechanism, which causes the gradient to involve global interactions, potentially introducing large variance; however, LayerNorm and residual connections help stabilize the gradient. This global interaction induces highly non-local and high-variance gradients. Since almost all current adversarial training methods (especially our SCP, which relies on the Fisher Information Matrix for local geometric approximation) depend on stable local gradient information, the noisy gradient landscape of ViT causes the estimated worst-case perturbation directions to become highly inaccurate.
