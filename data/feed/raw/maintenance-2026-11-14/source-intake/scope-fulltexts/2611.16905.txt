However, they still remain costly in practice, and even SOAP still pays substantial optimizer-state memory and eigendecomposition overhead. We ask whether the benefits of SOAP can be retained at nearly the efficiency of AdamW. In this paper, we answer yes with a lightweight SOAP variant, Factored One-sided Adam-Moment (FOAM). The path to practical impact is twofold: first, FOAM employs input-only preconditioning, dropping the output-side Kronecker factor to reduce memory and remove one eigendecomposition. The Kronecker structure of the Hessian shows that this is exact for a diagonal output-side curvature and nearly lossless for a near-diagonal one, as we observe empirically. Second, FOAM replaces SOAP’s dense rotated second moment with a factored Adam-moment estimator based on row-by-column statistics. Under the K-FAC Fisher assumption with a diagonal output curvature, this estimator exactly recovers SOAP’s dense second moment, and we measure only a 15–20% K-FAC violation, with negligible effect on the update. Our empirical results support the claim that FOAM matches strong SOAP and Muon baselines in validation loss across scales of large language model (LLM) pre-training, uses half of SOAP’s optimizer state, runs at Muon-class wall-clock time, and retains robustness across a three-fold learning-rate range. The results hold from 124M to 760M parameters, demonstrating that SOAP’s gains need not require SOAP’s cost. Their successors, including memory-efficient adaptive optimizers [ 50 , 51 , 53 , 15 ] , show that comparable optimization quality can be achieved with substantially less optimizer memory. In another direction, Shampoo [ 16 ] , a foundational method in the family of Kronecker-factored second-order optimizers, has shown that the optimization quality of adaptive first-order methods such as Adam can be surpassed. Its most practical variant, SOAP [ 44 ] , the Adam-in-eigenbasis version of Shampoo, reports 35–40% improvements in wall-clock time and step count over AdamW at large batch sizes, building upon the Distributed Shampoo baselines [ 3 , 40 ] that won the external-tuning track of the first AlgoPerf competition [ 6 , 21 ] . Still, practitioners often default to AdamW or Muon [ 20 ] , because SOAP achieves its gains at the expense of computational efficiency, requiring about 4.5 × 4.5{\times} the optimizer state of AdamW and a significantly slower per-step wall-clock time. More specifically, SOAP’s memory is dominated by the first moment, the second moment in the eigenbasis-rotated space, and the two Kronecker factor matrices L = G ​ G ⊤ L=GG^{\top} ( m 2 m^{2} ) and R = G ⊤ ​ G R=G^{\top}G ( n 2 n^{2} ) of a gradient G ∈ ℝ m × n G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n} . Its runtime is further occupied by the periodic eigendecomposition of these factor matrices, which makes SOAP substantially more expensive than AdamW despite its strong optimization performance. This paper aims to retain SOAP-level quality while approaching AdamW-level cost. Our motivation is simple. If SOAP’s computational overhead can be sufficiently reduced, its optimization advantages could make it a practical successor to AdamW. To this end, we revisit SOAP’s major cost components and show that several can be relaxed or replaced without sacrificing performance. The original SOAP paper [ 44 ] already evaluated one-sided and factored variants, but as cost-reducing ablations without a criterion for when they preserve quality. Frans et al. [12] then found that input-only rotation and a factored variance each retain most of SOAP’s quality, and other works [ 30 , 11 ] improved SOAP along other axes. Our contribution is thus not one-sided preconditioning or factorization itself, but explaining which side to retain , when factorization is justified , and why their combination is effective . Concretely, our approach is built on two structural simplifications: input-only preconditioning and a factored Adam-Moment update. The first simplification drops the output-side factor L L and retains only the input-side factor R R , eliminating its m 2 m^{2} memory and one of the two eigendecompositions. Its justification follows from the Kronecker structure of the Hessian, in which all output rows of an affine layer share the same input-side outer product x ​ x ⊤ xx^{\top} [ 25 ] , while the Hessian of transformer layers is empirically near-block-diagonal along output neurons or heads [ 50 ] , making the input-only form the natural one-sided approximation. The second simplification replaces the dense rotated second moment with a row-and-column factored estimator, r ∈ ℝ m r\in\mathbb{R}^{m} and c ∈ ℝ n c\in\mathbb{R}^{n} , with trace normalization, reducing the buffer size from m ​ n mn to m + n m+n . Under the K-FAC Fisher assumption [ 35 ] with a diagonal output curvature, this estimator exactly recovers SOAP’s dense second moment, and the K-FAC part holds to a 15 15 – 20 % 20\% relative error in practice. Together, these simplifications reduce SOAP’s optimizer state from 2 ​ m ​ n + 2 ​ m 2 + 2 ​ n 2 2mn+2m^{2}+2n^{2} to m ​ n + 2 ​ n 2 + m + n mn+2n^{2}+m+n and the amortized eigendecomposition count from two to one. We call the resulting method FOAM ( F actored O ne-sided A dam- M oment). Figure 1 highlights FOAM’s main advantages. On pre-training, FOAM matches the strong Muon and SOAP baselines on validation loss with half of SOAP’s optimizer state and reaches AdamW’s final loss within 7 % 7\% of AdamW’s own wall-clock time , while SOAP needs more than twice as long at 760M, and FOAM matches AdamW’s hyperparameter robustness over a three-fold learning-rate range. The advantage holds from 124M to 760M parameters with learning rates re-tuned at every size from a μ \mu P [ 46 ] anchor. In summary, our contributions are: (i) a structural justification of input-only preconditioning, with the Kronecker structure of the Hessian and empirical verifications with LLMs with recent architectural elements; (ii) a factored Adam-Moment update with trace normalization, exact under K-FAC with a diagonal output curvature and empirically validated through K-FAC-residual measurements during training; and (iii) the resulting algorithm FOAM, a scalable and practical SOAP variant that delivers SOAP-class quality with half of SOAP’s optimizer state ( + 7.4 % +7.4\% measured peak training memory over AdamW) and Muon-class wall-clock time. 2 Related Work Adaptive optimizers can be categorized by how aggressively they approximate the empirical Fisher F = 𝔼 ⁡ [ vec ⁡ ( G ) ​ vec ​ ( G ) ⊤ ] F=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{vec}(G)\,\mathrm{vec}(G)^{\top}] , which all of Adam [ 23 ] /AdamW [ 32 ] , Shampoo [ 16 ] , and SOAP [ 44 ] implicitly use as a curvature surrogate in place of the true Fisher [ 24 ] . 2.1 Empirical Fisher and Kronecker-factored Optimizers AdaGrad [ 8 ] introduced per-coordinate scaling by an empirical second-moment statistic, an early diagonal approximation of the inverse empirical Fisher. K-FAC [ 35 ] replaces the diagonal with a Kronecker product, F ≈ 𝔼 ⁡ [ a ​ a ⊤ ] ⊗ 𝔼 ⁡ [ s ​ s ⊤ ] F\approx\mathbb{E}[aa^{\top}]\otimes\mathbb{E}[ss^{\top}] where a a is the layer input and s s the gradient with respect to the layer’s pre-activation output, the assumption under which our factored Adam-Moment update is exact. Eschenhagen et al. [10] adapt K-FAC to transformer architectures via the expand and reduce flavors. Shampoo [ 16 ] carries this idea to the matrix parameters. It maintains EMAs of L = G ​ G ⊤ L=GG^{\top} and R = G ⊤ ​ G R=G^{\top}G and steps in L − 1 / 4 G R − 1 / 4 L^{-1/4}GR^{-1/4} , i.e., it preconditions by ( R ⊗ L ) − 1 / 4 (R\otimes L)^{-1/4} ; Morwani et al. [36] show that R ⊗ L R\otimes L equals one power-iteration step toward the optimal Kronecker-product approximation of the empirical Fisher and is empirically close to it. SOAP [ 44 ] eigendecomposes L L and R R and runs Adam in the resulting rotated coordinates, an EKFAC-style construction [ 14 ] that Lu et al. [33] interpret as gradient whitening. Variants modify specific pieces: CASPR [ 9 ] combines the per-axis factors through a Kronecker sum instead of a Kronecker product. KL-Shampoo and KL-SOAP [ 30 ] recast the family via KL-divergence minimization, and Purifying Shampoo [ 11 ] shows that trace scaling of the preconditioner can replace learning-rate grafting, which corroborates our trace-norm choice in § 4.2 . The one-sided fallback in distributed Shampoo [ 3 ] drops a factor on memory grounds, whereas we give a structural argument for keeping the input side (§ 4.1 ). Purifying Shampoo’s adaptive tolerance criterion for skipping the periodic eigendecomposition is adopted unchanged as FOAM’s amortized eigenbasis-refresh policy (§ 4.2 ). 2.2 Optimizers with Coarser Approximations A second line of work adopts coarser approximations to the empirical Fisher in exchange for lower computational cost. Adam [ 23 , 32 ] keeps only the diagonal of F F , sacrificing all matrix structure. Signum [ 4 ] keeps only the sign of the momentum. Muon [ 20 ] replaces the second-moment buffer with Newton-Schulz orthogonalization M ( M ⊤ M ) − 1 / 2 M(M^{\top}M)^{-1/2} , which can be read as a square-root natural-gradient step under a structured Fisher R ~ ⊗ I m \tilde{R}\otimes I_{m} with an input-side factor R ~ ∈ ℝ n × n \tilde{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n} shared across output rows [ 34 , 15 ] . AdaMuon [ 41 ] adds an element-wise Adam-style second moment on top of the orthogonalized update. SPlus [ 13 ] , a related SOAP variant, replaces the rotated second moment with sign normalization in the Shampoo eigenbasis and adds iterate averaging. Recent Muon variants (NorMuon [ 28 ] , Muon 2 [ 31 ] , NAMO [ 48 ] , Polar Express [ 2 ] ) refine specific components of Muon. A parallel branch targets memory-efficient AdamW rather than matching SOAP-class quality: Adam-mini [ 50 ] , GaLore [ 51 ] , APOLLO [ 53 ] , Alice [ 15 ] , and SWAN [ 34 ] . We exclude these methods from the main comparison since they mainly target a smaller memory footprint than AdamW, whereas we target SOAP-level performance at near-Adam cost. Positioning our method. FOAM lies at the intersection of these two branches. It stays within the Kronecker-factored Fisher framework but borrows the factored-second-moment idea from Adafactor [ 39 ] ’s row-by-column estimator, applied here in the rotated coordinates of SOAP. Both ingredients appear in the SOAP paper as cost-reducing ablations, a one-sided variant and an Adafactor-style factored second moment in the eigenbasis, and Frans et al. [12] found empirically that input-only rotation and a factored variance each preserve most of SOAP’s quality; the distinction lies in the criteria. SOAP’s one-sided variant keeps the side with the smaller dimension, whereas we keep the input side irrespective of size (§ 4.1 ), so the two rules choose opposite sides for the MLP down-projection; likewise, we factorize only after the input-side rotation, which makes the rotated second moment separable into row and column factors (Lemma 1 , § 4.2 ). 3 Preliminary: Shampoo and SOAP We consider a transformer with matrix parameters W ∈ ℝ m × n W\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n} , where n n and m m denote the input and output dimensions, respectively. Our experiments stop at GPT-2 scale, up to 1.56B parameters, so whether FOAM’s advantages hold for frontier-scale pre-training remains the main open question. Our implementation does not yet shard R R and Q R Q_{R} across devices, which matters at the ≥ 7 \geq 7 B scale, although the layer-wise distributed optimizer of Khona et al. [22] suggests that this is an engineering rather than a fundamental obstacle. The factored update is least accurate at the key projection, whose gradients pass through RoPE and softmax (Appendix C ). Finally, the embeddings and the lm-head stay on AdamW because their vocabulary dimension rules out a dense factor on that side, although a FOAM-style factor on their small hidden dimension would be affordable. Extending FOAM to these layers, and attention-aware factorizations for the key projection, are natural next steps. When to use FOAM. FOAM fits when SOAP-class quality is needed but SOAP’s memory or wall-clock is binding, since it stays within 0.004 0.004 – 0.008 0.008 nats of SOAP at half its optimizer state and, at 760M, about half its wall-clock. Against Muon and AdaMuon, quality slightly favors FOAM at every scale and speed is near parity, while AdaMuon keeps less state. Muon and AdaMuon thus remain preferable when optimizer state is the hardest constraint. Against AdamW, FOAM, like other matrix-preconditioned methods, is most valuable at large effective batch, where AdamW degrades (Appendix G ) and, at fixed 𝚙𝚏 \mathtt{pf} , the optimizer’s compute share shrinks. FOAM is least attractive when memory is dominated by modules with a large input dimension, such as the MLP down-projection, whose n 2 n^{2} factor carries most of FOAM’s residual state over AdamW. Conclusion. We presented FOAM , a scalable and practical SOAP variant built on two structural simplifications: input-only preconditioning, justified by the Kronecker structure of the Hessian and a near-diagonal output curvature, and a factored Adam-Moment update, exact under K-FAC, with Purifying Shampoo’s skip rule for the amortized eigenbasis update. Where memory-efficient variants mainly shrink AdamW’s memory, FOAM addresses the complementary problem and brings SOAP-level quality close to Adam-level cost. FOAM matches strong Muon and SOAP baselines on LLM pre-training with modern architectures while using half of SOAP’s optimizer state and Muon-level wall-clock time, from 124M to 760M , with supporting evidence at 1.56B and on ViT.
