Abstract— We present an innovative framework called Pixie, 
designed to enhance digital marketing operations through a 
context-aware, multi-agent large language model (LLM) 
architecture. The framework is versatile, supporting both real-
time and offline marketing activities, and leverages multimodal 
LLM capabilities to increase reliability and performance. 
Pixie offers a generalized multiagent architecture capable of 
performing a broad spectrum of digital marketing tasks. This is 
achieved through cooperation between a primary orchestration 
agent and task-specific specialized agents. These agents combine 
different learning techniques, such as zero-shot and few-shot 
prompt-based learning, with insights derived from marketing 
data, and proprietary expert marketing strategy guidelines and 
documentation on our proprietary statistical models. The agents 
broadly belong to the following categories - Primary 
Orchestration 
Agent 
which 
coordinates 
the 
workflow, 
understands user queries, and consolidates outputs from all 
agents; Task-Specific Agents which perform specific tasks such 
as performance analysis and Reconciliation/Reviewer Agents 
which evaluate and reconcile outputs from other agents using 
chain of thought reasoning. 
We experimented with different agentic workflows, ranging 
from single agent to multi-agent systems. The framework was 
evaluated through a combination of human expert assessments 
(trained marketers) and measuring LLM output variance. The 
evaluation criteria focused on accuracy, consistency, and the 
ability to solve domain-specific challenges. The results 
demonstrated that Pixie’s multi-agent framework significantly 
improves decision quality and operational efficiency in digital 
marketing workflows.  
We evaluated the efficacy of the Pixie framework on some of 
the most frequent digital marketing processes and it can 
potentially reduce the time spent on tasks such as root cause 
analysis and reporting, campaign research and planning. The 
framework is flexible, allowing for the integration of additional 
specialized agents tailored to specific marketing tasks. This 
expansion will further enhance the system's capabilities and its 
ability to adapt to the evolving demands of digital marketing. 
Keywords—Agentic workflow, Large Language Models, 
Digital Marketing 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Modern digital marketing faces unprecedented challenges 
due to the explosion of multimodal data and rapidly changing 
consumer behaviors. Traditional marketing tools are often 
task-specific, optimized for narrow use cases, such as 
campaign optimization or performance monitoring. While 
effective within their domains, they lack the ability to 
integrate insights, communicate with other systems, or adapt 
to changing requirements. 
Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated 
significant potential in digital marketing [1], enabling 
personalized content creation, strategy analysis, and 
explainable insights generation. However, these models are 
constrained by challenges such as complex reasoning 
limitations, numerical errors and a tendency to generate 
impractical 
suggestions. 
LLMs 
frequently 
produce 
hallucinations due to training data restrictions that lack 
domain-specific or latest information. The rapidly evolving 
marketing landscape necessitates designing systems which are 
capable of providing contextually relevant responses, 
integrating seamlessly across tools, and identifying optimal 
solutions that align with critical user constraints such as 
budget limits, brand guidelines, and audience preferences. 
Autonomous agents [2], [3], [4] have gained significant 
traction for their ability to enhance large language models 
(LLMs) with advanced functionalities, such as context-aware 
memory [5], multistep planning [6], and capability to learn 
using strategic tools [7]. These developments aim to create a 
generalist agentic system that employs a team of specialized 
agents capable of performing various tasks, such as web 
browsing, file management, and code execution [8]. This 
approach is not limited to technology; similar advancements 
are being observed in sectors like logistics where multi-agent 
systems can dynamically manage tasks and commitments [9]. 
In marketing analytics [10] and data science [11], these agents 
are being utilized to automate the generation of synthetic data 
and automating machine learning workflows, while in 
healthcare, they enhance decision-making and improve 
patient care outcomes [12].  
II. SCOPE OF WORK 
Motivated by the expert-like potential of autonomous 
agents, we introduce Pixie, a novel agentic workflow 

application to the digital marketing domain. Pixie works 
through co-operation between a primary orchestration agent 
and task-specific specialized agents. These agents combine 
different learning techniques, such as zero-shot and few-shot 
prompt-based learning, with insights derived from marketing 
data, historical actions, and proprietary expert reasoning 
documents and documentation on our proprietary statistical 
models.  
Our current work is focused on the most frequent and 
essential use-cases in performance marketing related to 
performance analysis and optimization, creative and targeting 
strategy analysis and insights generation. Our generalized 
multi-agentic architecture allows developing more granular, 
hyper-specialized agents for other tasks in future. We have 
created a platform-independent reasoning framework, aimed 
at efficiently working in multiple advertising platforms such 
as Meta and Google.  
The different agentic workflows were evaluated for the 
aforementioned tasks through a combination of human expert 
assessments (trained performance marketing experts) and 
measuring LLM-agent output variance. The evaluation 
criteria focused on accuracy, consistency, and the ability to 
solve domain-specific challenges.  
The next sections are divided into 1. Solution Architecture 
which explains different agentic workflows we evaluated and 
in-depth overview of multi-agentic workflow 2. Evaluation 
Framework and Results which explains our evaluation 
framework and results  for different agentic workflows and 
individual specialized agents and 3. Conclusions and Future 
Work. 
III. SOLUTION ARCHITECTURE 
We have experimented with three different types of 
agentic workflows implemented in increasing order of 
complexity. We performed this experiment to evaluate and 
identify optimal architecture for generalized digital 
marketing agent workflow. All three workflows are 
implemented in LangGraph and have three essential 
components: 
 
• 
Agents - Agents are Large Language Models which 
are tasked with different roles. We used OpenAI’s 
GPT-4o model because of its larger context 
window. 
• 
Specialized Task-specific Models Integrated as 
Tools - We provide specialized LLM agents with 
access to proprietary statistical recommendation 
models for various digital marketing tasks such as 
Performance Analysis, Bid-Budget Optimization, 
Audience Targeting Refinement and Creative 
Recommendations. Each task is addressed by an 
independent, task-specific model integrated as a 
tool for the corresponding LLM agent. 
• 
Knowledge Infrastructure - Pixis knowledge 
infrastructure integrates knowledge graph and data 
warehouse to support contextual data-driven 
decision-making. The knowledge graph serves as a 
semantic network, mapping industry vertical 
relationships, brand hierarchies, and marketing 
keyword taxonomies. The data warehouse 
aggregates structured data from major marketing 
platforms like Meta Ads and Google Ads, capturing 
campaign performance metrics, historical spending 
information, and third-party conversion metrics.  
 
The three workflows essentially differ from each other in 
whether specialized task-specific models are implemented 
as tools or agents. Furthermore, the workflows differ in how 
different agents accessed different models as tools. 
Additionally, we experimented with collaborative and 
hierarchical agentic structure. Our primary objective behind 
these experiments was to address whether increased agentic 
complexity leads to improved performance in terms of 
accuracy.  
A. Workflow 1 – Single Agent Architecture 
In single-agent architecture, we deployed a comprehensive 
digital marketing agent equipped with access to task-specific 
models. The agent's primary objective is to understand and 
address user queries related to digital marketing campaign 
performance analysis and identify measures for improvement. 
The workflow involves a direct interaction between the 
user and the agent, where the user presents specific marketing 
challenges or seeks optimization recommendations. Upon 
receiving a query, the agent strategically leverages its tool 
suite to analyse the problem, extract relevant insights, and 
formulate data-driven recommendations. This approach 
enables a holistic and tool-integrated response mechanism, 
allowing the agent to provide nuanced, context-aware 
suggestions for enhancing marketing campaign effectiveness.

C. Workflow 3 – Multi-agent with hierarchical architecture 
 
We expanded our multi-agent architecture by introducing 
a hierarchical graph structure with a primary orchestrator 
agent and seven specialized sub-agents. This approach 
allowed for increased problem-solving scope and task 
specialization without compromising output quality and 
mimicking real world marketing agency workflow which we 
have studied in our daily work. By introducing a sub-agent 
with vision capabilities, we transformed the workflow into a 
multi-modal system. The hierarchical structure enables 
flexible integration of additional agents, positioning this 
workflow termed Pixie as a generalized framework for 
addressing diverse digital marketing tasks and performing 
deep-dive on each task through follow-up user-interaction.

architecture 
The individual agents are described in detail as below: 
• 
Primary Agent - The primary agent serves as the 
main interface for user interaction, responsible for 
comprehending user requirements and strategically 
delegating tasks to appropriate agents to generate 
comprehensive 
recommendations. 
This 
agent 
orchestrates an iterative process of reconciliation and 
review, 
coordinating 
between 
recommender, 
reconciler, and reviewer agents before finalizing the 
report using the writer tool. To enhance task 
complexity management, we applied zero-shot and 
few-shot chain-of-thought prompting techniques, 
enabling the primary agent to decompose complex 
tasks into smaller, more manageable subtasks, 
thereby improving overall system performance and 
output quality. 
• 
Analyser Agent - The Analyser Agent, delegated 
tasks by the Primary Agent, specializes in 
performance analysis and identifying necessary 
actions for campaign improvement across tactical 
(bid/budget and targeting), strategic, creative, and 
audience exploration dimensions. Utilizing the 
performance analyser tool and media plan extractor 
tool, this agent examines current campaign 
performance and extracts performance goals from 
strategic documents. Access to arithmetic tools 
enables precise numerical operations such as 
calculations and averages. Upon completing its 
assigned tasks, the Analyser Agent returns control to 
the Primary Agent, facilitating a streamlined and 
focused workflow for performance optimization. 
• 
Recommender Agents - The Tactical and Strategic 
Recommender 
Agents 
provide 
targeted 
recommendations within the multi-agent framework. 
The Tactical Recommender Agent focuses on 
generating short-term recommendations to improve 
campaign performance efficiency, utilizing bid-
budget, targeting and creative tools to propose 
specific bid, budget, and targeting updates. In 
contrast, the Strategic Recommender Agent develops 
long-term strategic recommendations by analyzing 
campaign structure and performance goals using the 
campaign structure and campaign objective tracker 
tools. The agents leverage proprietary statistical and 
machine learning models as tools to perform 
complex numerical and analytical tasks, ensuring 
precise and data-driven recommendations across 
performance analysis, bid-budget and targeting 
optimization, and creative strategy. Both agents are 
augmented with insights from marketing data, 
proprietary expert reasoning documents, and 
documentation on statistical models which are 
integrated as tools, enhancing the framework's 
context-awareness.  
• 
Audience Explorer Agent: The Audience Explorer 
Agent is designed to provide audience segment 
recommendations for marketing campaigns by 
intelligently analyzing product preferences and 
generating customer insights. Leveraging the 
principles of SELF-DISCOVER [13] and Subgraph 
RAG [14], the agent systematically breaks down 
complex use cases into actionable solution steps 
using a comprehensive suite of tools and advanced 
knowledge infrastructure. The agent comprises six 
specialized tools that work synergistically to deliver 
precise targeting recommendations: 
1. Query Broker Tool: Employs few-shot prompting 
to determine optimal execution steps based on 
knowledge graph and database schemas. 
2. Entity Resolution Tool: Validates and matches 
query entities within the Pixis Knowledge Graph, 
enhancing query accuracy and relevance. 
3. Scoring Tool: Calculates semantic relevance for 
entities, enabling prioritized decision-making. 
4. Ideal Customer Profile Tool: Generates detailed 
customer profiles 
using one-shot prompting, 
characterized by interests, demographics, and 
behavioral attributes. 
5. Neo4j Query Tool: A dynamic graph query 
generator built on GraphCypherQAChain, (Chain for 
question-answering against a graph by generating 

Cypher statements) customized for marketing 
requirements. 
6. PGVector Query Tool: A flexible query generation 
mechanism using PGVector (An implementation of 
LangChain vectorstore abstraction using postgres 
database structure) for retrieving contextually 
aligned entities across database tables. 
The agent's intelligence is powered by two databases: 
• 
Pixis Knowledge Graph:  
 
A multi-layered semantic graph featuring: 
1. Brand Layer: Comprehensive brand metadata 
2. Latent 
Representation 
Layer: 
Industries, 
products, and customer profiles 
3. Platform Targeting Layer: Cross-platform 
targeting options 
• 
PGVector Database:  
A multi-table database optimized for efficient entity 
searching, particularly handling disconnected data 
components. 
This architecture enables the Audience Explorer Agent to 
generate precise, actionable marketing insights by seamlessly 
integrating advanced querying techniques with rich, 
interconnected data resources. 
• Creative Agent (Vision) - The Creative Agent is an 
advanced vision-enabled analytical system designed to 
provide 
comprehensive 
creative 
assessment 
for 
advertising campaigns. Utilizing a Vision Large 
Language Model (GPT-4 Vision), the agent conducts 
multi-dimensional analysis of ad creatives through 
intricate evaluation frameworks. The system performs 
granular assessment of both textual and visual elements, 
examining 
text 
attributes 
such 
as 
occlusion 
characteristics, clutter density, color and size variations, 
and cross-relevance between multiple text elements. 
Simultaneously, it analyzes product visual components 
across critical dimensions including color composition, 
thematic coherence, product size and spatial prominence, 
attention generation metrics, and background relevance. 
An integrated creative performance tool enables the agent 
to generate actionable recommendations, specifically 
determining whether to activate or deactivate specific 
creative assets based on comprehensive visual analysis. 
Upon completing its analytical task, the Creative Agent 
autonomously returns control to the Primary agent, 
ensuring seamless workflow integration and intelligent 
creative optimization. 
• Reconciler and Reviewer Agents - The recommendation 
generation process incorporates two critical quality 
assurance mechanisms: the Reconciler and Reviewer 
Agents. These agents serve as rigorous checkers that 
critique and refine recommendation reports before final 
user presentation. The Reconciler Agent focuses on 
identifying and resolving potential inconsistencies or 
gaps in recommendations generated by different 
specialized agents. When conflicting or missing 
recommendations are detected, this agent instructs the 
Primary agent to regenerate recommendations with 
updated guidance, ensuring comprehensive and coherent 
outputs. Similarly, the Reviewer Agent evaluates reports 
for critical attributes such as clarity, specificity, and 
explainability. If the reports fall short of these standards, 
the agent triggers a refinement process wherein the 
Primary agent re-delegates tasks to the appropriate 
recommender agents for improvement. To prevent 
infinite iteration, the review process is strategically 
limited to three feedback rounds. This systematic 
approach of iterative refinement ensures that the final 
recommendations meet high-quality standards, providing 
users with precise, well-structured, and actionable 
insights derived from a collaborative and self-critical 
multi-agent system. 
IV. EXPERIMENTS AND EVALUATION FRAMEWORK 
A. Workflow Evaluation Methodology 
The evaluation of three agentic workflows and the 
individual specialized agents for the multi-agent workflow 
were made with the help of expert digital performance 
marketers. LLM response variability was evaluated for 
consistency and repeatability through multiple sampling of the 
same query. Expert human marketers interacted with the 
agentic workflows through a series of questions addressing 
tasks related to performance analysis and efficiency across 
different dimensions. The evaluation was repeated for a total 
of four diverse marketing campaigns across geography, 
platform and spends. The experts then evaluated the quality of 
the responses on the basis of various dimensions listed below 
on a scale of (0,1,2) - 0 indicating the worst acceptance, 1 
being partial and 2 being absolute agreement with the response 
[15]. The different metrics of evaluation are as follows:  
• Conciseness: This metric evaluates how effectively the 
response conveys its message without unnecessary 
repetition or extraneous details. 
• Explainability: This metric evaluates how clearly the 
response articulates its reasoning, ensuring that the 
rationale behind the answer is transparent and 
understandable.  
• Coherence: This metric assesses the logical and 
grammatical consistency of the response, ensuring that all 
parts of the text flow smoothly and relate meaningfully to 
one another.  
• Reasoning accuracy: This metric evaluates the logical 
correctness of the thought process behind the response.  
• Recall: This metric measures the response’s ability to 
retrieve and include all relevant information required to 
address the query comprehensively.  
• Numerical accuracy: This metric focuses on the 
correctness of numerical information in the response, 
including 
values, 
calculations, 
or 
quantitative 
comparisons. 
• Factual accuracy: This metric assesses the factual 
correctness of the response, focusing on whether the 
information provided aligns with verified facts from the 
ground truth and the available knowledge base.

framework to assess the performance of agents within the 
hierarchical multi-agent workflow. The framework consisted 

of systematically measuring the quality of individual agent’s 
response using a similar framework as used for evaluating 
different agentic workflows.

C. Agent variance evaluation methodology 
Further, we evaluated consistency and reliability of each 
agent response through repeated executions. We executed the 
LLM workflow 10 times for each of four diverse marketing 
campaigns, generating 10 responses per query per agent. To 
analyze variance, we computed text distance metrics including 
factual accuracy, response structure, and entity identification 
across these responses. We calculated the mean distance 
between responses for each unique query-campaign pair for 
every agent. Then, we created variance distributions using 
box plots. The evaluation process aimed to quantify system 
stability by examining how closely variance values 
approached zero, with consistently low variance indicating 
high reliability. By rigorously testing agents across multiple 
dimensions and repeated trials, we sought to validate the 
robustness of the multi-agent workflow and its ability to 
generate consistent, high-quality recommendations across 
different marketing contexts. We performed this analysis for 
both individual agent responses as well as every workflow’s 
response for different queries. 
V. RESULTS 
Table 1 summarizes cost and latency metrics for different 
agentic workflows. The latency metric includes response time 
of the agent as well as computation time for the tools 
associated with each agent. As the complexity of the workflow 
architecture increases, the cost and latency metrics increase. 
Table 2 summarizes the human expert evaluation results 
across different metrics for different agentic workflows. The 
increase in cost and latency metrics are offset by an 
improvement in the response quality of the workflow. 
Thus, our experiments demonstrate the reliability of the 
hierarchical multi-agentic workflow as opposed to the agentic 
workflows in single-agent and collaborative multi-agent 
frameworks. This further concretises the hypothesis that 
adding more specialised agents will help improve the 
accuracy of metrics across the board. Better scores for 
explainability and reasoning for Pixie highlight that as we 
convert individual statistical tools into agents, they become 
better at explaining the ‘black box’ outcomes of statistical 
models. Although based on limited but comprehensive 
testing, these preliminary results will need to be further 
corroborated with more data collection.

VI. CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORK 
Pixie framework presents a novel approach to digital 
marketing operations, and our research opens up several 
promising avenues for future investigation and development. 
This section outlines potential research directions that can 
build upon our current work and further advance the field of 
multi-agent language model systems in digital marketing. 
Our current framework demonstrated significant potential 
in handling diverse marketing tasks, but there remains 
substantial room for expanding agent specialization. Future 
research could focus on developing more granular, hyper-
specialized agents targeting specific marketing sub-domains 
such as media-planning, competitor intelligence, marketing-
mix optimization etc. By creating increasingly sophisticated 
domain-specific agents, we can enhance the framework's 
ability to provide nuanced, context-aware marketing insights. 
The reconciliation agent represents a critical component of our 

multi-agent architecture. Future work could investigate more 
sophisticated reasoning and validation techniques, such as 
implementing more complex chain-of-thought reasoning 
algorithms. Our preliminary results suggest significant time 
savings in marketing operations. Our next steps include 
developing a more robust scalable architecture and reducing 
computation overhead in multi-agent interactions. Further, we 
propose several experimental directions to validate and extend 
our framework through comparative studies with alternative 
multi-agent architectures and stress testing the framework 
under more diverse marketing scenarios. 
The Pixie framework represents a significant step towards 
more intelligent, adaptive digital marketing systems. By 
continuing to refine our multi-agent approach, we can develop 
increasingly sophisticated tools that not only automate 
marketing processes but also provide strategic, context-aware 
insights.
