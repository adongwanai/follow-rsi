This division of labor raises a fundamental question: if code builds the world, can video models just render it? A reliable generative renderer hinges on two complementary criteria: ( i ) world preservation , retaining prescribed 4D kinematics and camera paths; and ( ii ) visual realization , persistently binding text-specified target attributes over time.
To evaluate this capability, we introduce WorldDraft , a benchmark of 432 432 strictly paired test cases across 48 48 executable 4D world families spanning six dynamic challenge categories.
Under a 3 × 3 3\times 3 factorial design, WorldDraft orthogonally crosses draft granularity (abstract proxy to previs) and appearance alignment (neutral to conflicting) under identical dynamics.
Extensive evaluations across 12 12 representative models reveal that:
(I) world preservation and visual realization remain in stark operational tension;
(II) richer draft scaffolding anchors structure but paradoxically impairs appearance fidelity; and
(III) conflicting source cues trigger severe source leakage (up to 64 % 64\% ), exposing fragile cross-modal disentanglement.
To mitigate these bottlenecks, we propose WorldDraft-Primer , a plug-and-play visual prompting strategy that consistently improves both objectives across frozen video renderers.
Together, WorldDraft and WorldDraft-Primer lay a foundation for bridging programmatic 4D simulation with video generation, advancing physically grounded generative rendering. Figure 1 : Performance overview of 12 12 video models on WorldDraft across 3 3 dimensions. 1 Introduction Video generation has achieved remarkable progress in visual realism and temporal coherence, yet generating complex, controllable dynamic scenes remains challenging ( Wan et al., 2025 ; Agarwal et al., 2025 ; Li et al., 2026a ; HaCohen et al., 2024 ) . Since end-to-end models must implicitly infer 4D world evolution from brief text while simultaneously generating pixels, high visual quality often comes at the expense of physical and spatiotemporal reliability. Recently, advances in multimodal coding agents have enabled an alternative paradigm that decouples world construction from visual generation ( Li et al., 2026b ; Wang et al., 2026a ; Liang et al., 2026 ; Zhang et al., 2026 ) . By programmatically controlling engines such as Blender ( Blender Online Community, 2026 ) or MuJoCo ( Todorov et al., 2012 ) , coding agents can explicitly specify scene layouts, object trajectories, and physical interactions as executable simulation drafts, leaving video generation models to transform these low-fidelity drafts into photorealistic videos. This emerging division of labor explores repurposing video models from world generators toward generative renderers , raising a fundamental question: if code builds the world, can video models just render it? We thus argue that a reliable generative renderer hinges on fulfilling two complementary objectives: ❶ world preservation , which demands faithfully retaining the scene geometry, object dynamics, event timing, and camera motion prescribed by the draft without rewriting the underlying world; and ❷ visual realization , which requires accurately generating text-specified target attributes ( e.g. , color, material) while persistently binding them to the corresponding 4D entities across motion, occlusion, and viewpoint changes. Formally, generative rendering operates as a conditional video-to-video (V2V) transformation guided by a structural draft and a target text prompt, yet existing benchmarks only partially address its requirements. Early V2V benchmarks ( Li et al., 2025 ; Liu et al., 2026 ) focus on editing real-world videos that already possess rich source textures, whereas recent programmable-world evaluation ( Huang et al., 2026 ) primarily examines whether generated videos follow program-logged states and interaction events. Crucially, achieving world preservation does not guarantee faithful visual realization : models frequently retain coarse draft dynamics while failing to generate or temporally bind target appearance, or conversely fulfill appearance prompts at the cost of altering the underlying 4D world. This gap motivates the need for a new benchmark that jointly evaluates both objectives to systematically characterize the capability boundaries of generative renderers. In this work, we propose WorldDraft , a systematic benchmark designed to evaluate video models as draft-to-video generative renderers through three progressive questions ( Figure 2 ). First, ➀ can video models render target appearances without corrupting complex 4D worlds? To test this foundational capability, we curate 48 48 executable 4D world families spanning ♣ six challenge categories : multi-entity orchestration, multi-stage dynamics, occlusion and reappearance, camera motion, non-rigid deformation, and hybrid interactions. Grounded in explicit simulator references, WorldDraft jointly measures how faithfully a model preserves the prescribed 4D structure and how accurately it realizes and persistently binds target visual attributes over time. Building upon these base worlds, we further probe the capability boundaries of generative renderers along two complementary axes: ➁ how much information must a draft provide to support reliable rendering? We then instantiate each world across ♠ three draft granularity levels (ranging from abstract proxies to detailed previews) to quantify minimal input requirements and the gains from richer structural and visual cues; and ➂ can models disentangle the world structure to preserve from the source appearance to overwrite? We further pair each draft with ♠ three appearance alignment levels (neutral, aligned, and conflicting) under a shared target prompt to evaluate selective adherence to structural versus textual conditions. Crossing both axes across all 48 48 world families yields a unified 3 × 3 3\times 3 factorial design of 432 432 strictly paired test cases. Through extensive evaluation on WorldDraft , we observe that existing video models struggle to jointly preserve underlying 4D worlds and realize persistent target appearances, especially under coarse draft granularity or conflicting source-target cues (see Figure 1 ). Motivated by these findings, we further propose WorldDraft-Primer , a plug-and-play visual prompting strategy designed to enhance frozen video renderers. Unlike existing adaptation pipelines that rely on costly retraining or privileged simulator access ( Li et al., 2026b ; Gomez-Nogales et al., 2026 ) , WorldDraft-Primer operates solely on the input draft and target prompt. Specifically, it injects subtle visual cues into the first draft frame via two complementary strategies: ( i ) object emphasis , which highlights key or easily overlooked entities to reinforce world preservation ; and ( ii ) attribute cueing , which encodes target colors, textures, and shading cues around entities and supporting surfaces to guide visual realization . Experiments show that WorldDraft-Primer consistently boosts performance across diverse models, serving as both an analytical companion to WorldDraft and a practical solution for improving draft-to-video generative rendering. To summarize, this work contributes threefold: • We propose WorldDraft , a systematic benchmark for draft-to-video generative rendering comprising 432 432 strictly paired test cases across 48 48 executable 4D world families, organized across six challenge categories and a 3 × 3 3\times 3 factorial grid of draft granularity and appearance alignment levels. • Through extensive experiments across 12 12 representative video models, our findings highlight that: ( i ) world preservation and visual realization remain in stark operational tension; ( ii ) finer draft granularity anchors structure but paradoxically impairs appearance fidelity; and ( iii ) conflicting source cues cause severe source leakage, exposing fragile source-target disentanglement. • We further introduce WorldDraft-Primer , a simple yet effective plug-and-play visual prompting strategy that injects object emphasis and attribute cues into the first draft frame, consistently boosting both world preservation and visual realization across frozen video renderers. 2 Related Work Code-for-Vision. Emerging works explore executable code as an intermediate representation for 3D reconstruction and scene synthesis ( Yin et al., 2026 ; Wang et al., 2026b ; Huang et al., 2024a ) , driving graphics engines to explicitly control geometry, camera parameters, and lighting. Extending this paradigm to dynamic environments, frameworks such as GS-Agent ( Zhang et al., 2026 ) and Code as Worlds ( Wang et al., 2026a ) describe 4D physical evolution in code, relying on simulators to govern deterministic trajectories and interaction rules. This approach establishes a modular division of labor: systems like VideoCoCo ( Li et al., 2026b ) and Code Video Model ( Liang et al., 2026 ) also employ coding agents to construct simulation drafts, leaving video generation models to synthesize photorealistic outputs. While prior research primarily targets upstream world construction, WorldDraft focuses on the downstream rendering stage, evaluating whether video models can faithfully realize target appearances without corrupting the underlying executable world. Evaluation of Video-to-Video. V2V evaluation has traditionally focused on text-guided editing, measuring instruction adherence, temporal consistency, and background preservation on natural videos ( Chen et al., 2025 ; Li et al., 2025 ; Liu et al., 2026 ; Gao et al., 2026 ) . With the rise of programmatic generation, recent benchmarks such as PROWBench ( Huang et al., 2026 ) check behavioral fidelity and event timelines against engine-logged states while evaluating visual quality through holistic metrics such as CLIP. However, such holistic evaluations overlook whether models can faithfully realize fine-grained target attributes and persistently bind them across dynamic 4D processes. In this work, WorldDraft goes a step further by investigating how varying input conditions govern the interplay between world preservation and appearance realization. 3 WorldDraft 3.1 Task Formulation Generative Rendering. Programmatic video generation decouples physical reasoning from visual generation: upstream engines explicitly govern scene kinematics, leaving downstream video models to act as renderers. Formally, an upstream simulator executes an underlying 4D world 𝒲 \mathcal{W} to yield a draft video 𝒟 = { 𝐈 t src } t = 1 T ∈ ℝ T × H × W × 3 \mathcal{D}=\{\mathbf{I}_{t}^{\text{src}}\}_{t=1}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times H\times W\times 3} . Given 𝒟 \mathcal{D} and a target prompt 𝐩 \mathbf{p} , a generative renderer 𝒢 \mathcal{G} synthesizes the target video: 𝒱 ^ = 𝒢 ⁡ ( 𝒟 , 𝐩 ) , \hat{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{D},\mathbf{p}), (1) where 𝒟 \mathcal{D} provides the 4D spatiotemporal scaffold ( e.g. , geometry, kinematics, camera), and 𝐩 \mathbf{p} dictates target visual attributes ( e.g. , color, material, lighting). At inference, 𝒢 \mathcal{G} strictly accesses ( 𝒟 , 𝐩 ) (\mathcal{D},\mathbf{p}) , with 𝒲 \mathcal{W} reserved solely for draft generation and ground-truth evaluation references. Dual Criteria. To determine whether 𝒢 \mathcal{G} faithfully executes this conditional transformation, the correctness of generative rendering hinges on two criteria: ❏ World Preservation , requiring 𝒱 ^ \hat{\mathcal{V}} to faithfully preserve the prescribed 4D kinematics, entity identities, event timelines, and camera motion in 𝒟 \mathcal{D} without prompt-countermanded structural hallucinations. ❏ Visual Realization , requiring 𝒱 ^ \hat{\mathcal{V}} to accurately instantiate target attributes from 𝐩 \mathbf{p} and persistently bind them to corresponding entities across dynamic motion, occlusions, and viewpoint changes. Generative rendering thus demands that 𝒢 \mathcal{G} disentangle the world structure to preserve from the source appearance to overwrite : a capability fundamentally governed by the draft’s input condition. 3.2 Executable World Families Challenge Taxonomy. To evaluate renderers beyond trivial rigid motions, candidate worlds should enforce deterministic physical constraints for world preservation while imposing dynamic stressors for visual realization . Guided by these principles, we first establish 48 48 executable world families across six primary categories ( Figure 2 ): World Coverage I. Multi-Entity Orchestration: Preserving multi-entity spatial arrangements and coordination, challenging the renderer to bind distinct target attributes without identity cross-contamination. II. Multi-Stage Dynamics: Retaining causal event ordering and phase transitions, challenging attribute persistence across state-changing boundaries. III. Occlusion & Reappearance: Maintaining physical occlusion orders and reappearance trajectories, challenging the renderer to re-identify and restore the exact target appearance post-emergence. IV. Camera Motion: Retaining 6-DoF camera trajectories and parallax geometry, demanding strict multi-view appearance consistency under perspective shifts. V. Non-Rigid Deformation: Retaining continuous mesh topology and soft-body kinematics, requiring surface textures and materials to deform realistically without visual slipping or artifacts. VI. Hybrid Interactions: Retaining dynamic physical coupling across disparate mechanisms ( e.g. , collisions and force transfer), challenging identity and material retention under violent multi-body contact. These categories serve as primary stressors rather than disjoint splits; each family is classified by its dominant challenge while incorporating secondary dynamics. In particular, multi-entity orchestration focuses on cooperative spatial topology, whereas hybrid interactions stresses dynamic physical coupling between contact processes. Construction and Validation. For each challenge, we first collect diverse real-world videos as dynamic blueprints, and employ multimodal coding agents ( i.e. , GPT-6-Astra ( Singh et al., 2025 ) ) to translate their underlying physical interactions and camera paths into executable MuJoCo ( Todorov et al., 2012 ) or Blender ( Blender Online Community, 2026 ) scripts.
Crucially, the footage serves as world reference rather than a target for pixel-level reconstruction.
Execution produces deterministic 4D rollouts while programmatically logging the ground-truth state tuple: 𝒲 i = ( 𝒮 i , 𝒯 i , ℰ i , 𝒞 i ) , \mathcal{W}_{i}=(\mathcal{S}_{i},\mathcal{T}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{C}_{i}), (2) with 3D geometry 𝒮 i \mathcal{S}_{i} , dense trajectories 𝒯 i \mathcal{T}_{i} , event timestamps ℰ i \mathcal{E}_{i} , and camera poses 𝒞 i \mathcal{C}_{i} .
Each world passes a three-tiered admission audit: ➀ numerical stability without divergence, ➁ fidelity to the reference interaction, and ➂ evaluative viability with unambiguous 4D binding stressors, freezing 48 48 standardized families, as shown in Figure 3 . Prompt Suite. A unified prompt 𝐩 i \mathbf{p}_{i} per family specifies 2 ∼ 4 2\sim 4 verifiable attributes ( e.g. , color, material, lighting). To guarantee unbiased evaluation, 𝐩 i \mathbf{p}_{i} enforces: ➀ observable verification via unambiguous visual criteria; ➁ alignment-invariant references that index entities via spatial/semantic roles ( e.g. , “leading cube”) rather than volatile appearance tags; and ➂ structural neutrality , strictly omitting trajectories or camera paths so text cannot compensate for draft sparsity. More examples are in Appendix A . Draft Granularity ( P P ). To investigate minimal input requirements, we instantiate each world across three monotonic information levels P ∈ { P 0 , P 1 , P 2 } P\in\{P_{0},P_{1},P_{2}\} : ❶ Abstract ( P 0 P_{0} ): Primitive geometries and flat shading; retains basic semantics and kinematics to test low-scaffold completion. ❷ Simulation ( P 1 P_{1} ): Standard physical meshes and basic lighting; serves as the canonical draft-to-video rendering operating point. ❸ Previs ( P 2 P_{2} ): Dense meshes, full context, and realistic shadows; quantifies headroom while retaining a synthetic-to-real gap. This axis establishes an information ladder of structural completeness rather than cosmetic image filtering. Appearance Alignment ( A A ). Rich visual cues can either guide rendering or trigger severe source leakage. Therefore, to evaluate adherence to text over deceptive source cues, each granularity P P is paired with three appearance alignment states A ∈ { A N , A A , A C } A\in\{A_{N},A_{A},A_{C}\} : ❶ Neutral ( A N A_{N} ): Uninformative placeholder shaders ( e.g. , gray clay), relying solely on text for appearance. ❷ Aligned ( A A A_{A} ): Source appearances match 𝐩 i \mathbf{p}_{i} within the precision of the respective granularity level. ❸ Conflicting ( A C A_{C} ): Contradictory source cues ( e.g. , blue metal vs. target red plastic) to probe source leakage. Variations affect strictly surface appearance without altering geometry 𝒮 i \mathcal{S}_{i} , kinematics 𝒯 i \mathcal{T}_{i} , or camera 𝒞 i \mathcal{C}_{i} . Crossing both axes yields a unified 3 × 3 3\times 3 grid of 9 9 paired inputs 𝒟 i ( j , k ) \mathcal{D}_{i}^{(j,k)} for each world, totaling 48 × 3 × 3 = 432 48\times 3\times 3=432 strictly paired test cases. Fixing alignment isolates the marginal gain of draft information ( Δ 01 , Δ 12 \Delta_{01},\Delta_{12} ), while fixing granularity isolates conflict sensitivity and source leakage. 3.4 Evaluation Suite Standard video metrics ( e.g. , FVD ( Unterthiner et al., 2018 ) ) fail to accurately evaluate both world preservation and visual realization directly. We thus establish an analytical suite across three disentangled dimensions: ➀ world preservation ( WP \mathrm{WP} ), ➁ rendering fidelity ( RF \mathrm{RF} ), and ➂ appearance persistence ( AP \mathrm{AP} ).
We report these dimensions separately to preserve diagnostic interpretability, with an additional composite score for overall comparison. World Preservation ( WP \mathrm{WP} ). Grounded in 𝒲 i = ( 𝒮 i , 𝒯 i , ℰ i , 𝒞 i ) \mathcal{W}_{i}=(\mathcal{S}_{i},\mathcal{T}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{C}_{i}) , WP \mathrm{WP} independently benchmarks: ➀ Entity & Layout ( 𝒮 i \mathcal{S}_{i} ) , using Count Accuracy and trajectory-matched BBox IoU; ➁ Kinematics & Events ( 𝒯 i , ℰ i \mathcal{T}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i} ) , via Matched Track Coverage, Interaction Success Rate (ISR), and Event Timing Coverage; and ➂ Camera ( 𝒞 i \mathcal{C}_{i} ) , via relative rotation and scale-normalized translation errors against the canonical draft. Metrics evaluate intended topology, ensuring P 0 P_{0} is not penalized for lacking fine details only introduced in P 2 P_{2} . Details in Section B.1 . Rendering Fidelity ( RF \mathrm{RF} ). Adherence to 𝐩 i \mathbf{p}_{i} is evaluated via attribute-level binary QA (Yes/No) across target colors, materials, and lighting, incorporating balanced affirmative/negated probes. Critically, QA judges receive only the target text and generated video, remaining strictly isolated from source drafts to prevent bias . Standard perceptual scores ( e.g. , Image Quality, Aesthetic) are reported separately as non-gated references VQ \mathrm{VQ} . Appearance Persistence ( AP \mathrm{AP} ). Since maintaining an incorrect appearance over time is not always consistency, we therefore establish a correctness-gated persistence metric: AP = 100 | 𝒬 AP | ​ ∑ q ∈ 𝒬 AP c q ⋅ s q , \mathrm{AP}=\frac{100}{|\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{AP}}|}\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{AP}}}c_{q}\cdot s_{q}, (3) where c q ∈ { 0 , 1 } c_{q}\in\{0,1\} verifies target attribute realization at an anchor frame, and s q ∈ { 0 , 1 } s_{q}\in\{0,1\} checks whether the identical entity retains that attribute across subsequent timestamps. Credit is awarded if and only if both pass ( c q ⋅ s q = 1 c_{q}\cdot s_{q}=1 ). Grid Diagnostics and Aggregation. On the full 3 × 3 3\times 3 grid, we designate ( P 1 , A N ) (P_{1},A_{N}) as the canonical operating point and derive two diagnostic quantities: ( i ) Conflict Penalty , CP = RF ⁡ ( A A ) − RF ⁡ ( A C ) \mathrm{CP}=\mathrm{RF}(A_{A})-\mathrm{RF}(A_{C}) , measuring performance drop under conflicting cues, and ( ii ) Source Leakage , SL = N source_leaked / N conflict \mathrm{SL}=N_{\text{source\_leaked}}/N_{\text{conflict}} , quantifying the erroneous persistence of conflicting source attributes. 4 WorldDraft-Primer The dual correctness criteria of WorldDraft expose an inherent operational dilemma in generative rendering: how to reliably anchor target attributes without corrupting prescribed kinematics. While prompt engineering might seem intuitive, text prompts are inherently spatially agnostic, frequently inducing attribute cross-contamination across multiple entities. Conversely, modifying all draft frames risks destroying the deterministic kinematics and inducing temporal flickering. Leveraging the fact that video diffusion models naturally propagate initial appearances via spatiotemporal attention ( Fang et al., 2025 ; Qi et al., 2026 ) , we propose WorldDraft-Primer , a plug-and-play visual prompting strategy. WorldDraft-Primer intervenes strictly on the initial frame 𝐈 1 src \mathbf{I}_{1}^{\text{src}} , producing a cued frame 𝐈 ~ 1 \tilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1} : 𝒟 ~ = ( 𝐈 ~ 1 , 𝐈 2 src , … , 𝐈 T src ) , 𝒱 ^ = 𝒢 ⁡ ( 𝒟 ~ , 𝐩 ) , \tilde{\mathcal{D}}=(\tilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1},\mathbf{I}_{2}^{\text{src}},\dots,\mathbf{I}_{T}^{\text{src}}),\qquad\hat{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{G}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}},\mathbf{p}), (4) leaving the remaining T − 1 T-1 draft frames untouched to preserve the exact physical evolution. Object Emphasis for World Preservation. To prevent renderers from dropping small or low-contrast entities, object emphasis introduces sparse, non-intrusive bounding cues. Guided by the prompt 𝐩 \mathbf{p} , an open-source VLM ( i.e. , Qwen3.5 ( Yang et al., 2025 ) ) identifies K K critical entities, while an open-vocabulary grounding model predicts their 2D bounding boxes ℬ = { 𝐛 k } k = 1 K \mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{b}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K} . For each box 𝐛 k \mathbf{b}_{k} , we generate a binary mask 𝐌 k corner ∈ { 0 , 1 } H × W \mathbf{M}_{k}^{\text{corner}}\in\{0,1\}^{H\times W} outlining minimalist corner brackets strictly around the entity periphery. The structural emphasis signal is formulated as: Δ ​ 𝐈 OE = ∑ k = 1 K 𝐌 k corner ⊙ 𝐂 mono , \Delta\mathbf{I}_{\text{OE}}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{M}_{k}^{\text{corner}}\odot\mathbf{C}_{\text{mono}}, (5) where 𝐂 mono \mathbf{C}_{\text{mono}} is a neutral monochrome tone. Since 𝐌 k corner \mathbf{M}_{k}^{\text{corner}} occupies only narrow peripheral margins without covering entity interiors or physical contact surfaces, it acts purely as a spatial anchor without altering underlying geometry. Attribute Cueing for Visual Realization. To steer appearance binding, attribute cueing injects non-textual hints across two levels: ( i ) entity cues ( Δ ​ 𝐈 ent \Delta\mathbf{I}_{\text{ent}} ), which modulate peripheral brackets with target colors, simplified texture patterns, or coarse specular shading while leaving source interiors visible; and ( ii ) environment cues ( Δ ​ 𝐈 env \Delta\mathbf{I}_{\text{env}} ), which introduce soft contact shadows, coarse ground textures, or subtle lighting gradients onto supporting surfaces. The composite initial frame 𝐈 ~ 1 \tilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1} is formulated as: 𝐈 ~ 1 = 𝐈 1 src ⊙ ( 1 − 𝐌 all ) + ( Δ ​ 𝐈 OE + Δ ​ 𝐈 ent + Δ ​ 𝐈 env ) ⊙ 𝐌 all , \tilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1}=\mathbf{I}_{1}^{\text{src}}\odot(1-\mathbf{M}_{\text{all}})+\big(\Delta\mathbf{I}_{\text{OE}}+\Delta\mathbf{I}_{\text{ent}}+\Delta\mathbf{I}_{\text{env}}\big)\odot\mathbf{M}_{\text{all}}, (6) where 𝐌 all = ∑ k 𝐌 k corner + 𝐌 env \mathbf{M}_{\text{all}}=\sum_{k}\mathbf{M}_{k}^{\text{corner}}+\mathbf{M}_{\text{env}} is the aggregate cue mask. Cues remain strictly localized to keep source-target conflicts ( A C A_{C} ) uncompromised. Appendix A More Details of WorldDraft A.1 Motivation of Each Challenge Grounded in the dual objectives of world preservation and visual realization , WorldDraft curates dynamic scenarios that jointly impose deterministic physical constraints and multi-modal binding stressors.
Evaluating generative renderers on these scenarios probes spatiotemporal reliability across diverse physical regimes: capabilities that conventional static image metrics fundamentally fail to measure.
Below, we detail the design motivations behind our six dynamic challenge categories: I. Multi-Entity Orchestration When scenes contain multiple concurrent entities, particularly those with identical geometries, dense clusters, or dynamic spatial swaps, models face severe risks of identity confusion and attribute cross-contamination.
This challenge explicitly assesses whether a renderer preserves exact instance counts, relative spatial layouts, and coordinated multi-agent trajectories, while accurately binding distinct target attributes ( e.g. , individualized colors and materials) to their designated semantic roles without spatial bleeding or entity dropping. II. Multi-Stage Dynamics Locally smooth motion does not imply global causal correctness: models often generate visually plausible micro-movements while skipping discrete phase transitions or reversing event sequences.
This category evaluates whether key temporal boundaries, causal action-reaction orders, and phase-dependent kinematics are faithfully preserved, while simultaneously requiring target appearances to remain invariant across state-changing boundaries ( e.g. , a mechanical latch unlocking, opening, and locking). III. Occlusion & Reappearance Physical occlusion temporarily removes direct visual evidence of an entity, breaking adjacent-frame continuity and demanding long-range spatiotemporal memory.
This challenge measures whether the renderer maintains physical occlusion orders, follows emergence trajectories, and accurately re-identifies the target entity upon reappearance: restoring its precise target color, material, and surface texture rather than resetting or re-synthesizing a generic object. IV. Camera Motion Aggressive 6-DoF camera trajectories continuously reshape perspective projections, surface visibility, and parallax depth.
This category evaluates whether the model faithfully preserves the canonical camera path and epipolar geometry. It demands strict multi-view appearance consistency, verifying that intrinsic material identities remain stable while view-dependent visual cues ( e.g. , specular highlights and Fresnel reflections) deform realistically under dynamic perspective shifts. V. Non-Rigid Deformation Unlike rigid bodies with static topologies, soft-body kinematics continuously deform mesh geometries and local surface coordinates.
This challenge tests whether continuous topological deformations and structural integrity are preserved, and whether target textures, patterns, and elastic materials conform naturally to the deforming substrate without visual slipping, surface tearing, or texture resetting. VI. Hybrid Interactions Violent physical interactions, such as contact collisions, momentum transfer, and friction, couple the motions of disparate objects into codependent physical systems. Generating plausible individual motions in isolation easily violates joint Newtonian laws.
This category focuses on preserving contact boundaries, interaction outcomes, and kinetic energy exchange, while testing whether entity identities and materials survive intense physical contact. It directly complements Multi-Entity Orchestration by emphasizing dynamic force coupling over static spatial coordination. Each executable world family in WorldDraft is categorized by its primary physical stressor, while naturally incorporating secondary dynamics. Appendix B More Details of Evaluation Suite This section details the formal definitions, score normalization, and aggregation protocols underlying the three evaluation perspectives and the composite score in Table 1 .
Formally, let c = ( P , A ) ∈ Ω c=(P,A)\in\Omega denote an input condition, where Ω = { P 0 , P 1 , P 2 } × { A N , A A , A C } \Omega=\{P_{0},P_{1},P_{2}\}\times\{A_{N},A_{A},A_{C}\} represents the full 3 × 3 3\times 3 grid of 9 paired configurations across 48 48 executable world families { 𝒲 i } i = 1 48 \{\mathcal{W}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{48} . All rate- and accuracy-based metrics are reported on a standard [ 0,100 ] [0,100] scale. Shared Temporal & Spatial Protocol. To ensure fair cross-model comparisons regardless of native frame rates, all evaluations operate on the first 5 5 seconds of generated videos using absolute timestamp alignment rather than frame indices: • Continuous Tracking & Geometry: Dense geometric evaluation ( e.g. , bounding boxes, tracks, and 6-DoF camera trajectories) is conducted at 8 8 Hz across 40 40 uniformly spaced timestamps ( t ∈ [ 0 , 4.875 ] t\in[0,4.875] s). • Sparse Attribute & Event QA: Appearance and interaction probes evaluate 8 8 key timestamps distributed across the 5 5 -second duration. C.3 Text Prompt Engineering Baseline (Text PE) Our Text PE baseline adapts Video Prompt Optimization (VPO) ( Cheng et al., 2025 ) for generative rendering under four strict controls:
( i ) the target prompt is optimized once per world family ( p i → p i ′ p_{i}\to p^{\prime}_{i} ) and shared across all nine factorial conditions to preserve paired design;
( ii ) the meta-prompt enriches descriptive appearance attributes while explicitly delegating kinematics, spatial layout, and camera motion to the draft;
( iii ) generations use identical random seeds, drafts, and model inference templates; and
( iv ) all evaluations strictly assess adherence to the original prompt p i p_{i} , preventing spurious metric shifts. C.4 System Prompt for Visual Prompting To automate first-frame cue generation of WorldDraft-Primer , we deploy Qwen3.5-27B ( Yang et al., 2025 ) as an agentic visual grounding reasoner.
