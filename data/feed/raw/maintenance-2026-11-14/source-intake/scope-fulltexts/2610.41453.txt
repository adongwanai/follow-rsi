Tool timeouts can leave AI agents facing ambiguous tool outcomes and partial execution: an action may have failed, committed or remained in flight while other workflow steps have already completed. We formulate evidence-guided task recovery over approved obligations, provider capabilities and states compatible with public evidence. A shared admissibility check handles key expiry, authorization timing, dependencies and residual effects; a terminal certificate separates actual goal satisfaction from publicly supported safe completion. The known-model implementation combines standard observation-contingent planning with a separately implemented effect-history adjudicator. In a finite study of 37 assignments, planning and three active controls each certify 28 completions; four saved synthetic fee units incur additional computation. A preregistered assignment-held-out comparison then evaluates planning advice against evidence prompting with two local model bundles. Across 58 paired units in 19 previously developed task families, advice certifies 30 completions versus 9, a 36.2-percentage-point difference (exact family sign-flip p = 0.0078 p=0.0078 ), using 272 proposals versus 399. All 116 trajectories pass independent replay. Both models exhibit a positive completion difference. The comparison measures the evaluated advice interface under a common shield; search-versus-rule advice requires a separate matched comparison. Unknown and handoff outcomes remain explicit, with guarantees tied to the declared provider capabilities rather than arbitrary third-party exactly-once execution. Keywords: AI agents, tool use, failure recovery, ambiguous tool outcomes, partial execution, idempotency, compensation, planning under partial observation 1. Introduction An agent that creates an invoice, updates a business record, and notifies a customer interacts with several independently operated services. A timeout at the first service does not reveal whether the invoice was created. A batch update may have changed some records before returning an error. A notification can be delivered while its acknowledgment is lost. Repeating the entire workflow can duplicate irreversible effects; stopping can leave legitimate obligations unfulfilled. The operational problem is therefore not only to avoid another write, but to determine which remaining work can safely be completed. The distinction between a failed invocation and an unknown remote outcome is fundamental to remote procedure calls ( Birrell and Nelson, 1984 ) . Durable identifiers, receiver-side deduplication, and application-level evidence can improve recovery, but their guarantees depend on the endpoints that participate. A local transaction cannot make an uncooperative remote service atomic. End-to-end arguments explain why correctness conditions that depend on an application’s effects require support at the relevant endpoints ( Saltzer et al., 1984 ) . Compensation provides a different form of recovery: a permitted later action can repair some state, without erasing the fact that the original action occurred ( Garcia-Molina and Salem, 1987 ) . Agent research now addresses these problems directly. SagaLLM combines persistent context, validation, and compensating recovery ( Chang and Geng, 2025 ) . Atomix distinguishes transaction settlement from execution progress and coordinates different effect classes ( Mohammadi et al., 2026 ) . LIMBO studies how models, harnesses, and tool contracts affect duplicate side effects, including late commits and limited read paths ( Li, 2026 ) . UndoBench separates nominal task competence from fault recovery using paired executions and both effect-history and state evaluation ( Sah et al., 2026 ) . Building on these recovery and evaluation foundations, we focus on selecting evidence acquisition and continued execution for the remaining task under a fixed provider contract. We study the decision problem that remains when the provider’s capabilities are fixed. A recovery controller must choose whether to spend resources obtaining evidence, wait for an observation to become decisive, retry under a valid deduplication contract, continue independent obligations, compensate a reversible step, or hand the task to an authorized operator. These choices interact. Waiting for one uncertain step can consume the time needed for another obligation. A cheap query can be inconclusive until a visibility delay has passed. Compensation can create its own ambiguous outcome. Retrying before a key expires can be safe under one contract and unsafe under another. An operator may not respond before the business deadline. The controller receives the approved task, public capability contracts, public observations, and a supplied finite transition model. That model includes the candidate initial commit times, complete future write/compensation attempt sequences, and their weights. It does not receive the realized assignment or private effect ledger. Thus, this is known-model partial-observation planning, not recovery from a timeout using capability flags alone. We represent uncertainty by all states compatible with these inputs. A common admissibility check excludes actions that add a checked violation or abstract pending hazard in any compatible state. Within that common action set, a finite-horizon planner compares evidence acquisition and continued execution. Probabilities guide selection; they cannot turn a low-probability prohibited transition into an admissible action. The contribution is a recovery decision interface and its controlled evaluation, built on established observation-contingent planning: (1) An explicit contract connects remaining obligations, public provider evidence and residual in-flight effects. It distinguishes a correct current snapshot from certified completion stable at every accepted finite-tail prefix. (2) An executable known-model recovery profile compares common action checks, capability rules, one-step selection and standard observation-contingent search under matched observations, authority and resources. Separately implemented adjudication and exhaustive development diagnostics check effects and completed search layers. (3) A source-bound empirical decomposition separates deterministic policy behavior from model-facing advice. A registered assignment-held-out study records 116 model episodes: planning advice certifies 30/58 paired units versus 9/58 with evidence prompting, while strong deterministic controls already tie at 28/37 in their separate frame. Table 1 maps these claims to their evidence. The model-facing comparison measures the evaluated advice interface under its common shield; isolating search depth would require matched alternative advice. Weights describe the supplied finite model, and the resource analysis reports synthetic fees separately from selection computation and deployment costs. The intended application is a cross-system coordinator that preserves useful progress and gives an actionable explanation when completion cannot be certified. The coordinator does not grant additional authority, manufacture provider capabilities, or promise exactly-once completion for arbitrary services. A task with no trustworthy way to distinguish a pending effect from a completed one can legitimately remain unresolved. Remote effects and durable execution Birrell and Nelson’s RPC design makes the failure semantics of remote execution explicit ( Birrell and Nelson, 1984 ) . The end-to-end principle is relevant when delivery, duplication, and application correctness depend on facts outside a transport layer ( Saltzer et al., 1984 ) . These foundations explain why an HTTP error alone is not a negative effect certificate. RIFL provides linearizability with durable request identity and completion records in cooperating storage systems ( Lee et al., 2015 ) . Beldi supports fault-tolerant, transactional stateful serverless workflows through controlled logging and execution machinery ( Zhang et al., 2020 ) . These are important positive examples of stronger execution guarantees when the required infrastructure is available. Our setting treats a third-party tool contract as an input: the controller cannot add a missing receiver-side atomic deduplication record merely by persisting a local request. 2.2. Compensation and agent transactions Sagas decompose a long-lived transaction into subtransactions with compensating actions ( Garcia-Molina and Salem, 1987 ) . The compensation is a separately executed operation, subject to permissions, dependencies, and its own failures. It need not reverse every external consequence. For example, removing a draft can restore a local object condition, whereas deleting a record cannot retract a message already delivered. SagaLLM applies state management, independent validation, and recovery to multi-agent planning ( Chang and Geng, 2025 ) ; we refer to its formally published PVLDB version. Atomix supports progress-aware transactional settlement, reversible external effects, and gating of irreversible effects ( Mohammadi et al., 2026 ) . Its reported scope already includes ambiguous acknowledgments and partial irreversible settlement. Our decision object is the selection of evidence and continuation actions under a fixed, potentially incomplete provider contract. An original upstream implementation and an author-written reconstruction are distinct evaluation tracks. 2.3. Recovery evaluation LIMBO varies models, harnesses, and tool contracts, and evaluates recovery conditions including verification, waiting, keys, and guards ( Li, 2026 ) . It identifies the role of in-flight requests: a negative read does not justify a non-idempotent retry if an earlier request may still commit. Our common action checks incorporate this boundary. A strong combined verify–wait–key control is therefore necessary; blind retry and immediate stop serve only as diagnostic lower controls. UndoBench uses paired normal and fault executions to distinguish ordinary task competence from recovery capability ( Sah et al., 2026 ) . Effect-history checks are needed alongside final-state checks because an apparently correct final state can hide an extra irreversible action. We retain this distinction and compare all recorded assignments, rather than conditioning the main denominator on each method’s own normal successes. Benchmark tasks and capabilities that an author extends are explicitly distinguished from the original workload. The empirical question is whether joint evidence and continuation selection improves the recorded completion–cost relationship beyond these combinations. The claim requires equal observations, actions, authority, and resources. It cannot be supported by granting only the proposed controller an authoritative query or a compensating operation. AgentSpec specifies runtime constraints and interventions such as stopping, obtaining user input, and replanning ( Wang et al., 2026 ) . We build on runtime enforcement as an established component and compare selection policies inside a common admissibility interface. DART studies semantic recoverability and rollback certificates for structured tool agents, including observed failures and timeouts, with dependency- and effect-aware rollback ( Yang et al., 2026 ) . Here, an opaque request may already have produced an external effect while returning no usable acknowledgment. The provider’s evidence and termination capabilities determine whether continued execution or compensation is admissible; a local rollback certificate alone cannot establish that an unknown remote request has terminated. 2.4. Planning under partial observation Partially observable planning provides the established framework for maintaining possible states and choosing actions contingent on observations ( Kaelbling et al., 1998 ) . Constrained POMDP methods address resource-limited decisions ( Isom et al., 2008 ) . An expectation constraint is not a pathwise authorization guarantee. We separate the hard support-based action check from the weighted planning objective, and disclose the finite latent distribution used by the controlled model. ReAct alternates reasoning and action to obtain useful observations ( Yao et al., 2023 ) . Reflexion uses verbal feedback to improve subsequent decisions ( Shinn et al., 2023 ) . They motivate model-based recovery controls, but an agent’s reflection is not a provider receipt. A same-model comparison must expose the same public evidence and charge the same tool, token, retry, and waiting costs. Scripted policies in the present controlled track are not counted as executions of either language-agent method. Comparative scope. SagaLLM integrates persistent state and compensating recovery, while Atomix regulates settlement and irreversible release ( Chang and Geng, 2025 ; Mohammadi et al., 2026 ) . Their execution guarantees depend on their participating machinery; the present profile takes a fixed provider contract and a supplied transition model as inputs and evaluates remaining recovery choices within them. LIMBO and UndoBench inform capability and effect-history controls, but their qualifications here do not constitute information-matched model rankings. The measured added value is a recovery formulation, auditable conditional execution and a model-facing advice effect. The standard search, compensation and shielding components retain their existing research provenance. 3. Tasks, Contracts, and Evidence 3.1. Approved obligations A task consists of a finite directed acyclic dependency graph, approved object-level goals, a deadline, and resource limits. An operation i i binds a target, a request intent, and an idempotency key when the provider supports one. The reference model uses distinct targets and distinct logical keys across operations. Repeated attempts of the same operation retain that binding. Tasks with shared mutable targets or cross-operation key aliasing require a richer provider model and are rejected by this profile. Each operation has a goal value g i ∈ { 0 , 1 } g_{i}\in\{0,1\} . Value one requires the intended object or effect to be present once; value zero requires an approved reversible staging effect to be absent. A retirement workflow can therefore require removal of an old staging configuration followed by activation of its replacement. This is different from changing the original goal after a failure. Removing the old configuration alone does not complete a task that also requires the new configuration. The goal predicate is G ⁡ ( s ) = ⋀ i ( x i ​ ( s ) = g i ) , G(s)=\bigwedge_{i}\left(x_{i}(s)=g_{i}\right), where x i x_{i} is the operation’s modeled object/effect count. Dependencies require the predecessor’s approved goal value at the relevant effect boundary. The model distinguishes append effects from replacement effects. An additional append can violate an effect constraint even if a later repair yields an acceptable current object state. The full history records both writes and compensations. Reversibility is an explicit capability, not an inference from a successful delete call. An irreversible notification-like append cannot be compensated by deleting a local record. Compensation of a predecessor is also restricted while a dependent effect exists or is still pending. These restrictions protect dependency consistency rather than treating a reverse traversal of the graph as sufficient authority. 3.2. Common action admissibility The action interface contains query, write, wait, compensate, and handoff. Each action consumes declared fee, time, and call resources. Waiting is charged under the task’s public resource model, and handoff consumes resources without resolving the goal by definition. All primary controls use the same action interface and admissibility procedure. For each candidate action, the procedure enumerates the full current support, evaluates the declared transition, and checks the remaining resource limits. It settles finite pending effects for both the no-new-action reference and the action’s resulting state. An action is rejected if it introduces an additional modeled violation. For unknown-delay sentinels, a separate abstract check rejects increases in the covered duplicate, dependency, or compensation hazards; it does not concretely replay arbitrary late commits. The safety check does not sample the support or omit a low-weight state. This procedure gives different answers under different contracts. With a valid atomic same-key receiver contract, a repeat attempt can be allowed when it cannot append another effect. Without that contract, an uncertain append remains blocked unless the public model and evidence rule out both a prior commit and a harmful future commit. A replacement can be admissible under the implemented same-target, same-intent model, but this is not permission to overwrite a different business intent. Compensation is checked as an ordinary externally effectful action. Its target must be reversible and approved, its dependencies must allow removal, and its possible delayed result must be included in the safety assessment. A timeout during compensation therefore creates another evidence problem, not a successful rollback marker. Repeated compensation depends on its own deduplication contract. 4.2. Observation-contingent search The planner builds branches for complete public observations. For action a a at belief b b , all model states predicting the same observation form a branch, and their positive masses give its probability. The planner evaluates subsequent admissible actions within each resulting belief, building on standard partially observable planning ( Kaelbling et al., 1998 ) . The implemented value is lexicographic. It prioritizes the projected probability of certified completion, then expected satisfaction of approved obligations, then negative recovery cost. The final cost component uses the task’s declared fee plus a fixed small duration tie-breaker. This tie-breaker is a controlled implementation choice, not a conversion of money into a universal value of human time. Actual fees, calls, duration, and compensation counts are reported separately. An iteratively deepened expectimax search evaluates the observation branches. It caches complete belief/depth subproblems and orders actions by public one-step values. Only a layer in which all root actions and their observation branches have been backed up at the same depth can replace the previous selection. Diagnostics retain each completed root action’s full vector ( completion probability , goal progress , negative cost ) (\text{completion probability},\text{goal progress},\text{negative cost}) . A partially explored deeper layer is recorded but cannot compare its deeply searched action against another action’s immediate leaf value. A node budget limits optimization effort; it never limits the support checked by admissibility. With no completed informative layer, selection uses the capability-rule fallback. Exactness is restricted to the completed horizon and supplied transition model and prior. Receding-horizon execution does not imply globally optimal recovery or guaranteed completion under a finite budget. Resource-constrained POMDP shielding already combines hard action exclusion with planning ( Ajdarów et al., 2023 ) . Our use of this separation is explicit. The application-specific content is the provider evidence semantics, task obligations, in-flight effect tail, and experimental comparison of recovery decisions under those semantics. 4.3. Matched controls The capability-rule control can continue independent legal obligations, attempt valid same-key recovery, obtain informative queries, wait for possible evidence, traverse a finite safe retry sequence, compensate in dependency-safe order, and hand off. It receives the same public latent support as the planner. It is not deprived of a query or inverse operation to produce a favorable comparison. The verify–wait control uses the same admissibility checks and actions, but prioritizes informative queries before continued execution. It is an author implementation motivated by the recovery combinations in LIMBO ( Li, 2026 ) , not an execution of an unavailable original artifact. The greedy control ranks safe actions using one-step completion, obligation progress, information change, and cost. It has the same prediction model but does not build a multi-step observation tree. Immediate stop is a diagnostic control and does not report success on an unresolved task. The controls’ fixed priorities and tie-breaking rules are recorded in source before each collection. Both greedy and joint selection use the complete capability rules when their evaluated values provide no completion or obligation progress. That fallback is part of the implementation, not evidence of a separate planning gain. Safe retry opportunities are not filtered out merely because the immediately predicted attempt fails: a finite sequence can contain a known failed attempt followed by an allowed successful retry. 4.4. Execution and handoff After selection, the controller executes one public action, records its observation, updates the full support, and replans. A completion check can stop execution only when all supported states satisfy the terminal certificate. Otherwise, the controller returns an unresolved condition, an explicit handoff, a model contradiction, or a recorded resource/decision-limit outcome. A practical handoff should contain the bound logical requests, known effects, unresolved alternatives, remaining obligations, permitted next actions, and resources already consumed. The controlled interface records the handoff decision and its cost. It does not simulate an operator as if the operator had completed the task, nor does it assign an operator access to private scientific state. Implementation and Conditional Properties 5.1. Finite reference profile The reference implementation uses JavaScript modules for the provider model, public-belief update, and recovery policies. A separate Python implementation reconstructs observations, effects, costs, terminal state, and the public certificate. Both implementations operate on synthetic requests and logical time. No customer schema, real financial transfer, or sent email is represented as a measured deployment. Schema validation rejects cycles, cross-operation target/key aliasing, unsupported fields, invalid assignments, and unsafe integer arithmetic. A task has at most twelve operations and at most 4,096 initial support combinations. These are declared implementation limits. The study’s templates remain within them; the limits are not evidence of scalability for large business workflows. The environment and controller are separate interfaces. Collection can obtain the private evidence export after an episode. The recovery module imports only the declared finite transition model, not a live environment instance or its evidence export. Supplying the public model is part of the controlled assumption: it does not mean that arbitrary provider behavior has been inferred from a timeout. 5.2. Support retention Proposition 0 (Retention under a correct finite contract). Suppose the actual initial state belongs to the declared support, the provider follows the declared transition/observation model, and the controller retains every state matching a complete observation. The actual state remains in the support after every successful update. Proof. Initially the conclusion holds by the support assumption. If the actual state belongs to the current support, its declared transition produces the actual next state and public observation. Exact observation filtering therefore retains that transition. Applying the argument to each action proves the claim. A contradictory observation triggers an error rather than a completed update.
∎ The proposition characterizes support retention by the implemented observation filter under the stated contract. Initial-support coverage and provider adherence are its explicit premises. 5.3. Action and completion conditions Proposition 0 (Conditional preservation and completion). Under the retention assumptions for the explicit finite transition profile, an action checked against every support member does not introduce any violation covered by the implemented action check. If the initial state is safe including its accepted finite effect tail, repeated admissible actions preserve those checked conditions. If a completion certificate holds for every support member and every accepted finite-tail prefix, it holds for the actual supported execution. Separately, the unknown-sentinel abstraction rejects actions that increase its covered hazard counts and refuses completion while a sentinel remains. Proof. Retention includes the actual execution in each checked support. The action check therefore includes the actual transition and its modeled pending tail. An action that adds a covered violation in that transition would be rejected. Starting from a safe settled initial state, induction preserves that condition. A prefix without a violation is insufficient if an accepted request is already scheduled to violate a condition later. The same membership argument applies to the universal completion check.
∎ The finite-state membership argument is conditional on transition-model coverage. The unknown-sentinel check is an abstract containment rule, not a proof over all concrete commit times and interleavings. Neither grants receiver-side cancellation, distributed consensus, or an omitted provider capability. On an initially unsafe history, the relevant implementation condition is containment of new checked violations; the prior violation remains an absolute task failure. For finite delays, the model settles all already accepted events when assessing the future tail. A residual unknown-delay sentinel prevents terminal certification and remains distinct from a concrete scheduled commit. A non-idempotent retry is inadmissible when the same public history permits both a prior effect and an in-flight future effect that could duplicate it. This action boundary applies established remote-outcome reasoning and the in-flight analysis studied by LIMBO ( Birrell and Nelson, 1984 ; Li, 2026 ) . 5.4. Native persistence qualification An existing single-host durable executor and SQLite store are reused for a separate native qualification. Its public source repository is https://github.com/VancouverCanada/openport ; the qualification binds the locally tested build by file hashes rather than assuming its package version identifies every tested byte. The qualification exercises successful persistence, replay without invoking the handler again, reopened receipt reconstruction, wrong-request and altered-result rejection, missing evidence, and ambiguous delivery containment. An acknowledgment-loss handler creates a synthetic effect before throwing; the default executor retains manual review and does not automatically invoke that handler again. This is an actual call path through the existing public modules, rather than a renamed reimplementation of their state machine. It is also a qualification track, not a claim that the joint planner has been deployed through a third-party service or that every manual-review state has an automated native reconciliation path. The controlled decision study and native persistence checks retain separate evidence and denominators. 6. Study Design 6.1. Recorded workload and denominator The controlled workload is a fixed set of authored mechanism scenarios. It contains 20 public templates and enumerates all 37 combinations of their declared initial latent support. Each combination is executed by the five policies, yielding 185 records. A template is a business/contract scenario, not a new independent task for every change of an entity name. The workload is development and mechanism evidence, not a random sample of enterprise incidents. The workload covers dependent billing-like records, a stock dependency diamond, a file fork, independent obligations, and item-level partial batches. Additional templates isolate valid and expired keys, delayed reads, pending requests, absent queries, late effects beyond a deadline, ambiguous compensation, an approved retirement-and-activation goal, a safe retry sequence, initial duplication hazards, two authorization timings, and a restricted budget. The batch profile exposes individual operations. It does not represent an opaque all-in-one call with arbitrary unknown subsets. All methods receive the same public task, initial observation, finite support, action set, and limits. Private assignments choose the realized state without changing the public inputs. The collection runner passes only the public history and belief into selection. It retains an episode even when an existing violation makes safe completion impossible or when no safe continuation remains. Immediate stop is a diagnostic control; comparisons of planning choices use the shared-check policies. 6.2. Freezing and collection The collection protocol is written before the first episode. It binds the exact case list, policies, resource model, maximum number of decisions, search configuration, runtime, and hashes of the scientific source closure. Evidence is append-only within a new numbered collection directory. A record contains actions, complete observations, effects, resource totals, stop reason, errors, and public search diagnostics. The private fields are for adjudication, not policy input. The bounded planner uses a maximum horizon of six decisions and a budget of 128 non-leaf belief expansions across iterative-deepening layers. This is not a cap on transition evaluations, full-support checks, or wall computation. It does not remove states from an admissibility check. Every policy has at most 96 decisions and must respect the task’s tighter fee, logical-action, and deadline constraints. Search truncations and the completed horizon are retained. Logical waits model decision consequences and are not measured wall-clock service latency. This development process includes an initial recorded planner qualification and, when necessary, a separately recorded method revision. The initial record and its source archive are preserved. Results from a revision on the same templates remain development evidence; they are not relabeled as a blinded or held-out confirmatory test. 6.3. Independent adjudication The checker first validates input/source bindings, the complete case–policy denominator, record order, hash chain, and recorded errors. It then re-executes each deterministic public policy and environment trajectory. Wall-clock collector duration is excluded from deterministic equality; all scientific actions and observations are included. A separate Python program replays the assigned provider state and actual actions. It reconstructs due effects, complete observations, resource totals, dependencies, authorization, and final counts. It also maintains the complete public support to check a completion claim. The program imports no JavaScript policy or goal predicate. Validation tests reject forged completion, changed costs, lost late effects, malformed state bindings, and inconsistent observations. Independent implementation reduces shared-label errors; it is not a claim of independence from the common written specification. The primary descriptive count is certified safe completion within the task deadline, including all explicit finite-tail prefixes. We also report private goal satisfaction at the task deadline, existing absolute violations, newly introduced violations, handoff, unresolved outcomes, and all costs. A deadline goal can be true while its public certificate is false. Unresolved counts include handoff and therefore overlap with that category. Unknown-delay sentinels remain unresolved; their eventual concrete effects are not claimed to have been adjudicated. 6.4. Operational meaning Recovery should expose what is still owed, what is known to have occurred, and which evidence would permit another action. A blanket timeout retry conceals these distinctions. A blanket stop can also leave independent approved obligations unfinished. The reference interface makes continued execution, safe waiting, compensation, and unresolved handoff comparable under one declared contract. The goal representation matters. Retirement of an old staging object followed by activation of a replacement is a two-obligation task. A compensation response does not satisfy the activation obligation. A final receipt for an earlier write likewise does not establish the current goal after an approved compensation. Scope and intent bindings connect evidence to the business condition being certified. Provider capability differences change the appropriate action, not the intended goal. A valid atomic key can support a repeated attempt; an expired key cannot. A current negative query may help after a trusted settlement bound but can be insufficient while an older request is pending. Under commit-time authorization, a provider must enforce authority at that boundary. A local journal cannot retroactively fence an opaque remote operation. 8.2. Evidence scope and validity The study measures recovery choices in an explicit, finite, known provider model, including candidate future attempt sequences and repeated final outcomes. This makes invisible outcome differences auditable and supports complete enumeration, but gives substantially more predictive information than a normal capability declaration. Default cross-operation product weights impose prior independence; the separate supplied-correlated-support diagnostic checks implementation handling, not estimation of real correlations. Real services may change contracts, introduce unmodeled failures, share mutable targets, or use batch semantics beyond the item-level profile. Observable model contradiction fails closed, while an unobservable omitted alternative can still invalidate retention and safety assumptions. Those conditions require independently qualified model coverage, not a repaired posterior chosen for convenience. The implementation bounds operation counts and support size. Its explicit finite-tail proof covers scheduled effect prefixes, while the unknown-sentinel mechanism conservatively contains unresolved work. A null commit time records missing settlement information and remains distinct from every concrete future arrival time; persistence of that marker leaves completion uncertified. Additional invariants for harmless residual work or a stronger concretization abstraction define a separate qualification target beyond this profile. The common safety check is an evaluation control. Zero newly introduced violations in the recorded finite set establishes coverage of those checks for those executions, not zero operational risk. The three assignments with prior or externally scheduled hazards remain absolute task failures. Separating attribution helps explain a recovery decision without erasing the unsafe history. The four-unit synthetic fee saving and additional computation form a measurable resource trade-off. Complete per-task cost vectors retain zero and negative savings, and the compute-inclusive Pareto comparison preserves all reported axes. The symbolic break-even makes the required price ratio explicit. Translating these resource quantities into deployment economics requires isolated-host timing, provider prices, end-to-end latency and integration-cost measurements. The exhaustive reference validates the implemented finite-horizon objective within the declared provider model; the local-model study separately evaluates how its advice changes recovery behavior. 8.3. Deployment and model evaluation The native persistence qualification establishes a real call path through existing public modules for request binding, durable replay, receipt reconstruction and ambiguous-delivery containment. The decision policies are evaluated in a separate finite runner. These tracks characterize persistence behavior and recovery selection, respectively. Evaluating their integrated deployment through independent agent runtimes and third-party providers would extend that evidence with matched executor and provider outcomes. The local-model comparison holds model configuration, action schema, observations and per-episode limits constant, while adding planner advice in one condition. All proposals, rejections and observable tokens are retained. Its 29 held-out units are latent assignments within task families used during development, and both models share LM Studio. Every executed action passes the common shield, so the completion comparison evaluates advice within that shared safety layer. The confirmation establishes an advice-interface benefit in this frame; unseen task families, independent runtimes and real provider sandboxes define separate transfer tests. ReAct and Reflexion provide established agent-control context ( Yao et al., 2023 ; Shinn et al., 2023 ) , while the reported scripted controls are the explicitly defined finite-runner policies. An external service experiment should use synthetic sandbox entities, documented provider capabilities, and response-loss injection without sending real communications or creating real charges. Original capability and author-imposed restrictions must be separated. Evaluation of task completion, delayed effects, and unknown outcomes should be independently bound to the resulting public evidence. Native demos remain qualification evidence until that matched interface and outcome coverage are supplied. 8.4. Reproduction and material availability The retained materials include source-bound protocols, all controlled trajectories, a separately implemented adjudicator, generated tables, per-task costs, complete-root objective checks, budget-layer diagnostics, native qualification reports, local-model qualification records and the complete assignment-held-out confirmation. The recovery source/data candidate contains the synthetic controlled study and is retained locally for release review. No public identifier has yet been assigned to that candidate. The existing executor repository is separate from the new recovery study artifact. 9. Conclusion Recovery from ambiguous tool effects is a task-level decision over evidence, remaining obligations, and provider capabilities. The known-model reference separates realized private alternatives from publicly supplied future attempt sequences, checks actions against the full compatible support, and requires prefix-stable finite-tail evidence rather than a plausible success message. Unknown-delay sentinels remain a separate conservative containment abstraction. The model includes intent-bound keys, authorization timing, dependencies, approved compensation, and resource limits. The recorded finite study compares five policies on 37 assignments from 20 synthetic templates, with independent effect-history adjudication. The planner and the three active strong controls each complete 28 assignments safely and certifiably. Its resource trade-off consists of four saved synthetic fee units, two saved actions and increased selection computation, with monetary deployment costs requiring separate measurement. Persistent task costs and a full-vector/budget-layer audit make that trade-off and the implemented finite-horizon objective auditable. The retained first planner version demonstrates the importance of comparing completed search layers under a common evaluation objective. The preregistered local-model comparison supplies end-to-end recovery evidence within the same known model. Planning assistance certifies 30/58 paired episodes versus 9/58 with evidence prompting, a 36.2-percentage-point difference ( p = 0.0078 p=0.0078 under the registered family test), while using fewer proposals and observed model tokens. All 116 trajectories pass independent replay. This result retains the common shield, authored scenario frame and one-runtime scope. The operational conclusion is capability-specific: continue approved work when the public evidence and contract support it, and retain explicit unknown or handoff outcomes when they do not. The completed model study supports the evaluated advice interface. Generalization to new families, independent runtimes and third-party provider effects requires corresponding evidence. Public task schema An operation binds an ASCII identifier, target, request digest, dependencies, effect class, approved goal count, fees, durations, and the provider capability fields in Table 2 . Goal counts are zero or one. The finite support records positive weights, initial copies, historical commit and visibility times, key records, existing pending effects, and declared write/compensation outcome sequences. A compensated initial history explicitly records both its historical write and its removal. A valid historical key can therefore remain while the current count is zero. Outcome sequences specify successful or failed attempts, response type, finite nonnegative integer delay, and effect multiplicity. Their last entry repeats for every later attempt; they are complete known-model sequences, not capability-only hints. The initializer uses a cross-operation product prior; a supplied public joint support is required for correlations. Subsequent attempts use finite declared outcome delays. An initially pending null commit time is an unknown sentinel that does not advance or certify completion, not concrete simulation of arbitrary unbounded delay. Initial finite effects and pending events are replayed by event time, dependency order for equal times, and stable event identifier. A topological initial replay alone would conceal an earlier child effect that violated its predecessor condition, so temporal order is preserved. A.2. Evidence closure The controlled protocol binds the provider environment, scenario construction, recovery policies, Python adjudicator, collector, and checker. The row chain includes sequence, previous-row hash, and the full episode. The final manifest binds the exact protocol and raw stream. Verification checks source identity, frozen configuration, denominator, and order before replaying policies and independently adjudicating their outputs. These hashes support consistency and provenance under a fixed source set. They are not an append-only remote storage service or a defense against an authorized person rewriting both source and evidence. Native qualification has its own source bindings and raw logs. Upstream gitlinks are recorded by their pinned commit and marked uninitialized when not needed by the executed demo; no unexecuted submodule is represented as tested code. A.3. A.4. Evaluation distinctions Actual goal satisfaction, a public terminal certificate, and absolute safe completion answer different questions. The actual goal uses the private replayed state; the certificate uses all states consistent with public observations; safe completion additionally requires the full history and deadline constraints. A handoff is counted as handoff, not task completion. Compensation is counted as a public action and never silently changes the approved goal. The records retain logical durations, collector wall times, and one measured selection time per decision separately. These timings include implementation and host-load effects and do not substitute for provider latency or CPU billing. Replay compares semantic outcomes while excluding nondeterministic timing fields. Total fees, calls, waits, compensation, full-depth decisions, planner/fallback selections, and truncations are derived from complete rows. The expanded small-domain audit independently enumerates all root-action vectors, compares each complete layer, allows tied optima, and separately checks partial-layer exclusion and rule fallback. Complete task-cost rows retain all five policies; Pareto axes maximize certified completion and minimize introduced violations, fee, actions, logical time, and selection wall time. This is finite-horizon implementation validation and symbolic cost sensitivity, not full-task or production optimality. A.5.
