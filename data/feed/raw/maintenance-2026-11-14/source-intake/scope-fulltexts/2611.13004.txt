However, they face a fundamental trade-off: long token sequences preserve representation quality but slow generation, while aggressive spatial compression reduces representation capacity. Based on product quantization, we show that, under a fixed nominal information budget, group size should scale quadratically with the per-axis spatial compression factor. Building on this principle, we propose GSTAR, a spatial–group co-scaling framework that enables 32 × 32\times and even effective 64 × 64\times compression while preserving strong reconstruction, understanding, and generation performance. Since larger groups introduce intra-group dependencies that must be modeled efficiently, we analyze their impact on inference latency and generation quality and introduce a semi-autoregressive decoder that combines parallel feature computation with lightweight causal modeling. With these designs, our highly compressed models perform competitively with leading models across five representative text-to-image benchmarks (GenEval, DPG-Bench, PRISM, LongText-Bench, and Qwen-Image-Bench). At 1024 × 1024 1024\times 1024 resolution, our model delivers a 20 × 20\times speedup over prior discrete autoregressive models, generating each image in 2.5 seconds on a single NVIDIA H200. generation and autoregressive sequence modeling. By representing images as
sequences of discrete symbols, visual generation can be formulated using the
same next-token prediction paradigm that has driven the success of large
language models [ 16 , 46 ] . This formulation has
also shown promise for unifying visual generation and multimodal understanding
within a single autoregressive architecture [ 36 , 60 ] . Despite its conceptual simplicity and scalability, discrete visual
autoregressive modeling faces two fundamental limitations. First, mapping
continuous visual features to a finite codebook introduces quantization error,
which can discard fine-grained details and degrade reconstruction fidelity [ 49 , 16 ] . The same discretization can also remove
semantically relevant cues that are important for visual understanding and
language alignment, reducing the informativeness of the resulting discrete
representation [ 36 , 15 , 60 ] . Second, discrete autoregressive models generate visual tokens sequentially,
making inference latency grow with sequence length [ 46 , 51 , 1 , 60 ] .
This bottleneck is amplified at high resolutions, where the number of tokens
grows quadratically with image side length, widening the latency gap with
diffusion models that update all spatial positions in parallel at each
denoising step [ 22 , 43 ] . Increasing the spatial compression rate offers a direct way to reduce the
number of global autoregressive steps. However, aggressive spatial compression
forces each spatial token to encode more information [ 8 , 9 ] , exacerbating the information loss introduced
by discrete quantization [ 49 , 16 ] . To mitigate this
loss, we adopt multi-codebook quantization (MCQ) [ 28 , 54 , 36 ] , which represents each
spatial token with multiple discrete codes and thereby expands its
representation capacity. More importantly, MCQ admits a useful scaling
property: as detailed in Section 4.2 , jointly scaling the
spatial compression rate and the number of codes per token can preserve the
total nominal information budget while reducing the number of global
autoregressive steps. We systematically study how code scaling affects
reconstruction, generation, and semantic representation, and find that it
enables high-compression tokenizers at 32 × 32\times and even 64 × 64\times —well
beyond the common 16 × 16\times setting—while retaining high fidelity and
providing an efficient interface for downstream generation. Multi-codebook quantization requires each global autoregressive step to predict a group of codes. RQ-Transformer-style causal depth decoders model their dependencies, but their latency grows linearly with group size and can dominate the global backbone under aggressive compression [ 28 ] . Parallel and masked-token predictors remove this serial cost [ 4 , 51 , 40 , 31 , 5 ] , yet sample the codes independently and may combine incompatible modes—a failure known as multi-modal collision [ 12 ] . We therefore introduce a lightweight semi-autoregressive depth decoder: most layers run in parallel, while only the final layer retains a causal path for intra-group conditioning. This keeps group-scaling overhead nearly flat without sacrificing generation quality. We evaluate our approach on large-scale text-to-image generation using five
representative benchmarks: GenEval, DPG-Bench, PRISM, LongText-Bench, and
Qwen-Image-Bench. Our models perform competitively with leading systems across
these benchmarks while supporting spatial compression ratios from 32 × 32\times to 64 × 64\times . Controlled studies further examine how group scaling affects
reconstruction fidelity, generation quality, and semantic representation, and
compare the quality–latency trade-off among causal, parallel, and
semi-autoregressive intra-group decoders. At 1024 × 1024 1024\times 1024 resolution, our
model delivers a 20 × 20\times speedup over prior discrete autoregressive models
and generates each image in 2.5 seconds on a single NVIDIA H200. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We systematically study how scaling the group size affects
reconstruction, generation, and semantic understanding under 32 × 32\times and 64 × 64\times spatial compression, showing that scaling the per-step
capacity preserves strong representation quality with a short global
sequence. • We propose a lightweight semi-autoregressive depth decoder for
efficiently modeling intra-group dependencies, together with an optimized
inference implementation. Our system generates a 1024 × 1024 1024\times 1024 image in 2.5 seconds on a single NVIDIA H200. • We further validate the effectiveness of our approach at 32 × 32\times and even effective 64 × 64\times spatial compression by training two models, GSTAR-Pro-8B and GSTAR-Flash-1.7B , respectively, in a large-scale text-to-image setting. Our models achieve performance competitive with leading models across Qwen-Image-Bench, PRISM, DPG-Bench, GenEval, and LongText-Bench, while our approach delivers a 20 × 20\times inference speedup over prior discrete autoregressive models. 2 Related Work 2.1 Discrete Image Generation Discrete generators learn distributions over quantized visual tokens [ 49 , 16 , 46 ] .
Recent advances include next-scale prediction in VAR, Infinity, and NextFlow [ 47 , 21 , 60 ] , randomized generation
orders in RAR and RandAR [ 57 , 41 ] , and parallel
decoding in PAR and BitDance [ 51 , 1 ] .
Janus-Pro and Emu3.5 scale discrete generation within unified multimodal
systems [ 11 , 13 ] ; UniAR further combines a shared
visual tokenizer with parallel bitwise prediction [ 42 ] .
GLM-Image pairs autoregressive visual-token prediction with a diffusion
image decoder [ 59 ] . Representation capacity is another key axis. RQ-Transformer, MAGVIT-v2,
GSQ, UniTok, and WeTok explore residual, binary, or grouped quantization [ 28 , 56 , 50 , 36 , 63 ] ,
while BAR and BitDance model large binary token spaces with masked-bit and
diffusion heads, respectively [ 58 , 1 ] .
Building on these advances, we study spatial–group co-scaling at 32 × 32\times and effective 64 × 64\times compression, jointly examining reconstruction,
semantic representation, and the trade-off of group decoding. 2.2 Discrete Diffusion for Accelerated Generation MaskGIT and Muse accelerate image generation through iterative parallel
unmasking [ 4 , 3 ] . Show-o combines discrete
diffusion with autoregressive language modeling [ 53 ] , while
Emu3.5 adapts pretrained autoregressive models to parallel image-token
prediction via DiDA [ 13 ] .
NVIDIA’s Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion-Image advances this direction with token
editing and grouped cross-entropy for large-vocabulary image synthesis [ 31 ] . For speculative language decoding, DFlash uses block-diffusion drafting [ 5 ] , whereas DSpark combines a parallel backbone with a
lightweight sequential module to model intra-block dependencies [ 12 ] .
Inspired by these approaches, we accelerate intra-group code generation by
computing representations for all group slots in parallel, while using a
lightweight single-layer causal Transformer to model dependencies among codes. 3 Preliminaries Multi-codebook quantization. Multi-codebook quantization (MCQ) represents each spatial token with multiple

correlated discrete codes. A common approach, following RQ-Transformer [ 28 ] , is to use a causal depth head to model intra-group
dependencies, but its sequential decoding incurs increasing inference latency
as the group size grows. Fully parallel prediction removes this serial
overhead, yet sacrifices generation quality by ignoring these dependencies.
To reconcile these competing objectives, we propose a semi-autoregressive head
that preserves causal dependency modeling while approaching the decoding speed
of parallel prediction. Below, we first examine the causal and parallel
alternatives, then present our design. At global decoding step t t , the autoregressive backbone produces a context
representation 𝐡 t \mathbf{h}_{t} . The intra-group head uses this context to generate G G codes, 𝐲 t = ( y t , 1 , … , y t , G ) \mathbf{y}_{t}=(y_{t,1},\ldots,y_{t,G}) , by modeling the conditional
joint distribution p ⁡ ( 𝐲 t ∣ 𝐡 t ) p(\mathbf{y}_{t}\mid\mathbf{h}_{t}) . Causal Autoregressive Head. Following the spatial–depth decomposition of RQ-Transformer and its use in
grouped-code generation [ 28 , 36 ] , a causal
autoregressive head predicts the codes one by one, conditioning each prediction
on the global context and previously generated codes: p AR ​ ( 𝐲 t ∣ 𝐡 t ) = ∏ g = 1 G p ⁡ ( y t , g ∣ 𝐡 t , 𝐲 t , < g ) . p_{\mathrm{AR}}(\mathbf{y}_{t}\mid\mathbf{h}_{t})=\prod_{g=1}^{G}p(y_{t,g}\mid\mathbf{h}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{t,<g}). (4) This factorization preserves intra-group dependencies, but requires running
the full head for each of the G G sequential predictions. As G G grows, this
serial computation becomes a substantial decoding bottleneck and can dominate
the global backbone under aggressive compression. Parallel Prediction Head. To remove this sequential overhead, a fully parallel head predicts all G G codes in a single forward pass: p PAR ​ ( 𝐲 t ∣ 𝐡 t ) = ∏ g = 1 G p ⁡ ( y t , g ∣ 𝐡 t ) . p_{\mathrm{PAR}}(\mathbf{y}_{t}\mid\mathbf{h}_{t})=\prod_{g=1}^{G}p(y_{t,g}\mid\mathbf{h}_{t}). (5) This removes the intra-group serial path [ 4 , 51 , 40 , 31 ] , but makes the
codes conditionally independent during sampling. Since codes within a group
jointly describe the same visual content, independently sampled marginals may
select incompatible modes, leading to multimodal collision [ 12 ] . The quality degradation motivates a head
that retains causal conditioning while keeping most computation parallel. Semi-Autoregressive Head. We propose a semi-autoregressive head that combines a parallel trunk with a
lightweight causal module, retaining intra-group dependencies without repeating
expensive representation modeling G G times. Drawing architectural inspiration
from the parallel–sequential drafters in DFlash and DSpark [ 5 , 12 ] , the parallel trunk first computes
representations for all group slots in one pass: 𝐛 t , 1 : G = P ψ ( [ Mask ] 1 : G ; 𝐡 t ) . \mathbf{b}_{t,1:G}=P_{\psi}([\textsc{Mask}]_{1:G};\mathbf{h}_{t}). (6) A lightweight, single-layer causal Transformer then generates each code conditioned on
both its parallel representation and the previously sampled intra-group codes: p SAR ​ ( 𝐲 t ∣ 𝐡 t ) = ∏ g = 1 G p ⁡ ( y t , g ∣ 𝐡 t , 𝐛 t , g , 𝐲 t , < g ) . p_{\mathrm{SAR}}(\mathbf{y}_{t}\mid\mathbf{h}_{t})=\prod_{g=1}^{G}p(y_{t,g}\mid\mathbf{h}_{t},\mathbf{b}_{t,g},\mathbf{y}_{t,<g}). (7) The parallel trunk is evaluated only once per global position. Only the
single-layer, KV-cached causal Transformer remains sequential, allowing each
code to depend on previously sampled codes without repeatedly executing the
full head. Experiments in Sec. 5.2.2 show that our head
achieves generation quality comparable to the fully causal head with latency
close to parallel prediction, while consistently outperforming the parallel

that shorten the global autoregressive sequence, while the
Semi-AR Head reduces the latency of intra-group modeling.
Together, these designs reduce decoding costs at both the
global and local levels.
Beyond the architectural savings, we further optimize the system-level inference pipeline. We measure per-image latency for GSTAR-Flash-1.7B generating at 1024 × 1024 1024\times 1024 and apply four cumulative optimizations (Figure 5 ).
Starting from a Causal AR head baseline of 22.23 s, replacing it with our Semi-AR Head reduces latency to 14.93 s ( 1.49 × 1.49\times ) by parallelizing intra-group token prediction.
The remaining optimizations target the two main bottlenecks separately: the intra-group head and the LLM backbone.
For the head, we first capture each global step as a single CUDA graph, eliminating kernel-dispatch overhead ( 2.55 × 2.55\times , 5.85 s).
We then fuse chains of small elementwise and reduction kernels in the sampling and normalization paths into composite Triton kernels, reducing the per-block kernel count and eliminating redundant global-memory round trips ( 1.74 × 1.74\times , 3.37 s).

Appendix D Lossless Packing at 64 × 64\times Spatial Compression We evaluate whether our lossless packing strategy can increase the spatial
compression factor while preserving both tokenizer reconstruction and
text-to-image generation performance. Starting from f32g8 \mathrm{f32g8} (i.e., 32 × 32\times spatial compression with G = 8 G=8 ;
other configurations follow the same notation), we pack each 2 × 2 2\times 2 neighborhood of spatial latent positions along the group dimension to obtain f64g32 \mathrm{f64g32} . Specifically, four spatial positions with eight groups
