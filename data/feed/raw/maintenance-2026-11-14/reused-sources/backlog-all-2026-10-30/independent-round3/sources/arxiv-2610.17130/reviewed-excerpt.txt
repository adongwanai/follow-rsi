Affiliation: The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology Microsoft Tsinghua University Affiliation: University of Macau Abstract Automated alpha factor discovery is critical for modern quantitative research. While large language models (LLMs) have increasingly been integrated into quantitative workflows, existing agent-based approaches remain highly susceptible to severe backtest overfitting. To address this challenge, we propose EvoQuantLab , a training-free, dual-loop self-evolving framework for robust factor discovery. Instead of relying entirely on LLM generation, EvoQuantLab deploys an inner genetic-programming (GP) loop that dramatically improves token efficiency while evolving typed factor expressions under structural and diversity constraints. An outer diagnosis loop then systematically audits evidence across cycles to drive protocol-level improvements. Furthermore, our evaluation pipeline applies multi-metric validation to rigorously filter out spurious factors before deployment. Extensive out-of-sample experiments across both daily and hourly frequencies—covering equities and cryptocurrency—demonstrate the robustness of EvoQuantLab . Specifically, on daily CSI 500 and S&P 500 tasks, EvoQuantLab achieves leading annualized returns of 18.34% and 21.30%, consistently outperforming existing agent baselines. On high-turnover hourly cryptocurrency data, EvoQuantLab delivers a 37.91% annualized return while effectively avoiding execution collapse. 1 Introduction Automated alpha discovery has become an increasingly important direction in quantitative research ( Gong et al., 2026 ; Xiao et al., 2024 ; Lopez-Lira and Tang, 2026 ) . Recent large language model (LLM) agents can formulate economic hypotheses, generate factor implementations, interpret backtest feedback, and iteratively refine candidate signals, enabling substantially larger research spaces than traditional manual workflows ( Li et al., 2025 ; Tang et al., 2025 ; Han et al., 2026 ; Wang et al., 2026 ) . Systems such as R&D-Agent-Quant, AlphaAgent, QuantaAlpha, and FactorMiner demonstrate that LLMs can automate significant portions of factor and model discovery across heterogeneous financial data. Figure 1: A conventional quantitative-research workflow. Iteration over development backtests is necessary, but repeated inspection makes the complete search history part of the statistical experiment. The held-out test must remain outside this feedback path. However, scaling automated research also amplifies a fundamental problem: backtest overfitting . An agent may repeatedly generate and evaluate large numbers of factors, transformations, windows, and model variants, making it increasingly likely that apparently strong signals arise from noise or adaptive selection rather than persistent predictive structure ( Harvey et al., 2016 ; Bailey and López de Prado, 2014 ) . This issue is particularly severe for iterative LLM agents, since development results are repeatedly fed back into subsequent generations. Existing systems mainly focus on improving candidate generation, memory, reflection, or S&Psearch ( Yang et al., 2024 ) , but stronger search alone can further increase the effective number of trials ( Kou et al., 2025 ) . Meanwhile, relying on LLM calls for every candidate is unnecessarily expensive when many factor variations are symbolic transformations that can be explored more efficiently ( Shi et al., 2025 ; Romera-Paredes et al., 2023 ) . To address these challenges, we introduce EvoQuantLab , a training-free dual-loop self-evolving framework for robust alpha discovery. At the inner level, EvoQuantLab combines LLM-generated economic hypotheses with genetic programming (GP), which efficiently evolves typed factor expressions through selection, crossover, and mutation while maintaining structural and population diversity. At the outer level, the framework audits completed research cycles for evidence of overfitting, instability, excessive complexity, and selection bias, and uses these diagnoses to improve the research protocol for subsequent cycles. Importantly, protocol changes only affect future cycles rather than retroactively modifying completed evaluations. Together with a multi-metric validation pipeline that filters unstable, redundant, and economically inconsistent candidates, this design allows the search process itself to evolve while preserving disciplined out-of-sample evaluation. We evaluate EvoQuantLab across daily and hourly quantitative prediction tasks covering equities and cryptocurrency. On CSI 500 and S&P 500, EvoQuantLab achieves annualized returns of 18.34% and 21.30%, respectively, compared with 14.02% and 15.23% from the strongest baselines. On hourly cryptocurrency data, it achieves a 37.91% annualized return despite the shorter horizon and substantially higher turnover. Beyond final performance, our experiments show that GP provides an efficient mechanism for large-scale factor exploration, while outer-cycle diagnosis improves robustness across successive research cycles. Our contributions are: • We introduce EvoQuantLab , a training-free dual-loop framework that jointly evolves factor expressions and the quantitative research process to address adaptive backtest overfitting. • We combine LLM-guided hypothesis generation with diversity-preserving genetic programming, enabling efficient exploration of large symbolic factor spaces with substantially reduced reliance on repeated LLM inference. • We develop a diagnosis-driven outer evolution mechanism and rigorous multi-metric validation pipeline, and demonstrate strong out-of-sample performance across daily and hourly equity and cryptocurrency tasks. 2 Related Work Automated alpha factor discovery. Formulaic alpha discovery seeks interpretable expressions that predict future asset returns. Classical approaches rely on human-designed signals or search over symbolic expressions, with genetic programming (GP) providing a natural mechanism for evolving executable factor trees through crossover and mutation ( Koza, 1992 ; Kronberger et al., 2026 ) . Evolutionary search has been applied to trading-rule discovery ( Neely et al., 1997 ; Allen and Karjalainen, 1999 ) , while AutoAlpha extends this paradigm with hierarchical evolutionary search for formulaic alphas ( Zhang et al., 2020 ) . Learning-based approaches instead optimize the generation process directly; for example, deep symbolic regression frameworks ( Petersen et al., 2019 ) and AlphaGen ( Yu et al., 2023 ) formulate formulaic alpha discovery as reinforcement learning. Despite their effectiveness, these methods typically operate within a predefined search space whose operators and search procedure remain fixed. EvoQuantLab retains an explicit GP search engine but allows validated research knowledge and the search vocabulary to evolve across cycles. LLM agents for quantitative research. Recent work uses large language models to automate broader parts of quantitative research, connecting hypothesis generation, factor implementation, empirical evaluation, and iterative refinement ( Wang et al., 2024 ; Yang et al., 2026 ) . R&D-Agent(Q) coordinates research and development agents for joint factor–model optimization ( Li et al., 2025 ) ; AlphaAgent combines LLM-driven factor generation with regularization against redundancy, complexity, and alpha decay ( Tang et al., 2025 ) ; and systems such as QuantaAlpha and FactorMiner further explore iterative factor discovery with evolutionary search and accumulated research experience ( Han et al., 2026 ; Wang et al., 2026 ; Li et al., 2023 ) . These systems demonstrate the potential of LLMs as autonomous quantitative researchers, but also expose the model repeatedly to noisy backtest feedback in open-ended trial-and-error loops, where context noise readily misguides planning decisions ( Lu et al., 2024 ; Liang et al., 2026 ) . In contrast, EvoQuantLab separates semantic research evolution from candidate-level optimization: the LLM updates reusable research knowledge, while a deterministic, budget-accounted GP engine performs factor search. Self-evolving agents. Beyond finance, self-evolving agents aim to improve their own reasoning procedures, workflows, architectures, or procedural skills through accumulated execution feedback and experiential memory ( Shinn et al., 2023 ; He et al., 2026a ; Zhao et al., 2024 ; Zheng et al., 2026 ) . Pioneering efforts search over executable programs and algorithmic heuristics using LLMs in an evolutionary loop ( Romera-Paredes et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2023 ; He et al., 2026b ) . Other methods automate agent designs and workflows via iterative feedback ( Hu et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2025a ; Zhang et al., 2025b ) , or recombine modular components like planning and memory ( Shang et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2026 ) . This line of work shows that adaptation can occur at the system level rather than solely through model parameters, extending from agent workflows to broader research automation that co-evolves skills, memory, and policies ( Xu et al., 2026 ) . EvoQuantLab brings this perspective to quantitative research, but evolves research knowledge and symbolic search across cycles under a frozen evidence protocol, preventing held-out outcomes from influencing subsequent evolution. 3 EvoQuantLab Figure 2: Overview of EvoQuantLab . Automated quantitative discovery faces two intertwined challenges: efficiently exploring vast symbolic spaces, and preventing adaptive backtest overfitting caused by repeated experimentation. The pipeline begins at L0 (Data) , which assembles canonical panels, tradable universes, and purged fold masks under strict prefix invariance. Next, L1 (Factory) instantiates typed, economically grounded terminals spanning order flow, funding, microstructure, and momentum, while L2 (Tree GP) searches typed symbolic expression trees guided by the worst-fold fitness objective in Equation 1 . Surviving expressions undergo economic falsification at L3 (Falsify) through only-arm/drop-arm ablations, sign-flip and participant-swap tests, and cross-sectional residualization against pre-registered factor carriers. A candidate survives only if its hypothesized core mechanism remains directionally consistent and the full expression provides reproducible incremental value. At each timestamp, we cross-sectionally residualize candidate scores against a pre-registered carrier matrix and re-evaluate the residual signal across development folds. Candidates whose predictive power vanishes after residualization are rejected as redundant with known factor families. Subsequently, L4 (Stress Battery) stress-tests signal stability across regime splits (market trend × \times volatility), execution delay curves, asset clusters, and matched-budget block permutations. Candidates that clear all development hurdles reach L5 (Admit) , where their implementation, terminal manifest, data version, and metric configuration are frozen and recorded by content hashes before OOS access. Finally, L6 (Portfolio) maps admitted signals into trading strategies under fixed volatility targeting, risk constraints, and transaction costs. 3.3 Loop A: Inner GP Search & Knowledge Ingestion L1 Knowledge Ingestion. The trajectory exhibits a clear transition from exploration to exploitation. Initialized with four factors at Cycle 0 (IR 1.57), early evolution targets momentum- and spread-based structures, elevating the cumulative IR to 2.93 by Cycle 2. While Cycle 3 finds extra candidates, their highly overlapping risk profile ( ρ = 0.881 \rho=0.881 ) yields negligible gain, showing that factor quantity alone cannot bolster the portfolio. A larger improvement occurs in the middle of the run when evolution expands into a previously underexplored factor family. After the rejected Cycle 4 proposals, Cycle 5 introduces coverage-tail signals combined with CCI and contributes three new L5-approved factors, increasing the cumulative IR from 2.67 to 3.21. Continued refinement of this family reaches the peak portfolio IR of 3.62 at Cycle 6, together with an arithmetic return of 8.52%. These transitions suggest that the main benefit of iterative evolution comes from discovering new predictive axes rather than merely recombining candidates within an already saturated region of the search space. Later cycles show diminishing returns and increasing redundancy. By Cycle 7, exploitation begins to dominate novelty: four portfolio seats are proposed but only two correspond to unique additions, while the cumulative IR slightly decreases from 3.62 to 3.59. The final recombination stage exhibits a similar pattern. Although Cycle 10 continues to construct candidates from coverage, RSI, and CLV signals, the audit identifies substantial overlap with earlier discoveries; deduplication reduces the corresponding IR from 3.39 to 3.08. Overall, the trajectory shows that the evolution process is most effective while it continues to open new factor families, whereas later cycles increasingly revisit correlated structures and approach a performance plateau. C.2 Trading Strategy We implement execution-aware portfolio simulations tailored to the characteristics of equities and digital assets. Daily Equities (CSI 500 and S&P 500). For equity markets, all methods utilize a daily-bar LightGBM paradigm based on pre-screened feature pools. The trading pipeline is simulated as follows: • Pre-OOS Feature Selection : Candidate factors undergo strict pre-screening using only development data (train and validation sets). We remove exact duplicates, discard factors with negative validation IC or IR, and greedily prune redundant factors based on the Pearson correlation of their daily portfolio weights (candidates with an absolute correlation ≥ 0.70 \geq 0.70 against previously accepted factors are excluded). This retains 9 factors for CSI 500 and 20 factors for S&P 500. • Model Training and Target : Features are rank-normalized and mean-centered cross-sectionally each day. The LightGBM model is trained to predict the next-day cross-sectional return ranking, with hyperparameters and early stopping (patience of 50) determined strictly on the validation set without OOS lookahead (CSI 500: max depth 4, 15 leaves, learning rate 0.02; S&P 500: max depth 5, 31 leaves, learning rate 0.03). • Portfolio Construction : At the close of trading day t t , the model generates score s i , t s_{i,t} for stock i i . The dollar-neutral portfolio weight w i , t w_{i,t} is constructed as: w i , t = s i , t − s ¯ t ∑ j | s j , t − s ¯ t | w_{i,t}=\frac{s_{i,t}-\bar{s}_{t}}{\sum_{j}|s_{j,t}-\bar{s}_{t}|} (11) where s ¯ t \bar{s}_{t} is the cross-sectional mean score. This guarantees zero net market exposure ( ∑ i w i , t = 0 \sum_{i}w_{i,t}=0 ) and a total absolute gross leverage of ∑ i | w i , t | = 1 \sum_{i}|w_{i,t}|=1 . • Execution : Daily portfolio returns are calculated from close-to-close price changes over the subsequent trading day. The reported performance for CSI 500 and S&P 500 reflects gross returns prior to execution fees and slippage. Hourly Cryptocurrency (Crypto). Due to higher turnover and volatility, Crypto adopts an equal-weight factor portfolio strategy over hourly bars: • Factor-Level Normalization : For each retained candidate factor k ∈ { 1 , … , K } k\in\{1,\dots,K\} , hourly raw scores across tokens are transformed into mean-zero rank signals and inverse-volatility scaled. The resulting single-factor positions are smoothed over a 72-hour rolling window and re-normalized so that the sum of absolute holdings equals 1 ( ∑ i | w k , i , t | = 1 \sum_{i}|w_{k,i,t}|=1 ). • Portfolio Aggregation : The final multi-factor portfolio weight is aggregated at the portfolio allocation level: w t = 1 K ​ ∑ k = 1 K w k , t w_{t}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_{k,t} (12) Aggregating positions rather than raw factor values ensures proper re-estimation of aggregate portfolio turnover and transaction friction. • Execution and Costs : Signals formed at hourly timestamp t t are executed with a one-hour implementation latency, evaluating against subsequent hourly returns. To suppress heavy-tailed price anomalies, returns are causally winsorized based on historically available volatility up to signal formation. Portfolios rebalance hourly with an applied transaction cost of 5 bps per one-way turnover (net returns, excluding perpetual funding costs).
