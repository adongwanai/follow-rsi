3.1 Infrastructure and phase-adaptive training configuration Phase-adaptive training safeguards inherited value baselines against policy-drift shock by decoupling actor learning scales from critic recalibration. Backbone and asynchronous infrastructure. We instantiate Terminal-RSI on Qwen3.8-27B with a 262,144-token model context capacity, adopting T1’s slime coordination, Megatron optimization, SGLang sampling, and Harbor-based execution [ 46 , 56 , 38 , 55 , 12 ] . Rollouts overlap optimization in a one-rollout-batch-ahead pipeline, corresponding to the step-wise off-policy setting of one. Training data. Our 3,000-task pool combines 2,400 semantic-verifier tasks and 600 supplementary terminal tasks. Scientific Workflow Reconstruction (SWR) supplies execution-grounded supervision through hidden semantic checks on reconstructed scientific programs [ 20 ] . SWR100 has no task overlap with this training pool. Semantic grading resolves partial progress at verification, not necessarily after every action. Stage-specific hyperparameter curriculum. We adapt refinement to each stage’s starting policy (Table 1 ). RPR-1 uses actor rate 5 × 10 − 7 5\times 10^{-7} and critic rate 1.5 × 10 − 5 1.5\times 10^{-5} , updating values on a faster optimization scale. After RSR-2, RPR-2 reduces the actor rate fivefold to 1 × 10 − 7 1\times 10^{-7} while retaining the critic rate; both actor schedules are constant within phase. The smaller actor rate is intended to limit disruptive changes while the inherited critic recalibrates. This schedule accompanies FDR in RPR-1 and SPR in the retained Long-Reward RPR-2 branch; ordinary RPR-2 retains FDR. Critic preparation and phase-local calibration follow Section 2.1 , building on value-initialization precedents [ 46 , 49 , 50 ] . Both optimization phases configure global batches of 256 with oversampling to 288 requests to minimize long-tail sample latency, generating one response per task. To accommodate the extreme 256k sequence capacity across distributed workers, we apply Megatron 3D parallelism coupled with context parallelism of degree 4, setting the per-GPU token budget to 8,192 and the log-probability chunk size to 1,024. Across both stages, PPO retains conservative policy clipping with bounds [ 0.30 , 5.00 ] [0.30,5.00] of ϵ l ​ o ​ w \epsilon_{low} and ϵ h ​ i ​ g ​ h \epsilon_{high} , an actor gradient clipping threshold of 1.00, and zero KL penalties. Additionally, RPR-2 incorporates dynamic sample balancing, a unit microbatch size, and sequence padding aligned to multiples of 2,048 tokens to address heterogeneous trajectory lengths. Finally, the retained local optimization steps of 20 and 18 are distinguished from the nominal rollout horizon. Benchmarks. We evaluate Terminal-Bench 2.1, 3.0, 4.0, and Hard, together with LHTB and SWR100 [ 28 , 41 , 27 , 19 , 22 , 20 ] . LHTB filters 120 candidate projects to 46 tasks with reference solutions and hidden tests; broken projects require multi-turn repair across long dependency chains, with weighted subtask grading and hidden stress cases. We report mean process reward and strict completion at score 1.00, not the 0.95 near-completion threshold. The source SWR benchmark derives six variants from each of 500 executable workflows spanning 46 families and six domains; this construction is distinct from our 2,400-plus-600 training mixture. SWR100 tests 100 reconstructions from public input–output examples against hidden semantic configurations, structural checks, and anti-shortcut checks. Evaluation protocol. Each retained stage checkpoint is evaluated on each benchmark in three independent runs, reporting their arithmetic mean (Mean@3), not pass@3 or a union of solved tasks. LHTB counts use the compact notation mean strictly completed tasks/46. Sampling uses temperature 0.60 and top- p = 0.95 p=0.95 , at most 8,192 output tokens per turn, and 96,000 input tokens per call. Up to 100,000 turns are permitted, subject to benchmark termination and time limits. Proactive summarization triggers at 32,000 accumulated tokens, compressing prior context before execution continues; it is not a terminal event. Following standard evaluation practices for large-scale RL, multiple independent evaluation runs rigorously capture stochastic inference variance and ensure result reliability.
