3.3 Training dynamics and checkpoint comparison
Shared-checkpoint retention balances nonmonotonic, benchmark-dependent changes.
Figure 6 includes all 30 observations per Terminal-Bench suite: Direct PPO, the discarded RPR-1
continuation, ordinary RPR-2, and Long-Reward RPR-2 through step 20. The phase-ordinal axis aligns
local steps without merging branches or equating total compute. All final results use the same checkpoint.
Continuations expose cross-suite tradeoffs. After retained RPR-1 step 20, Terminal-Bench 4.0 falls
from 16.67% to 11.11% at step 35, while Terminal-Bench 2.1 rises to 78.95%. Ordinary FDR RPR-2,
initialized from RSR-2, falls from 17.46% at step 6 to 11.11% at step 10 on Terminal-Bench 4.0. The
retained SPR Long-Reward checkpoint instead reaches 19.84%, up 5.55 points from its step 6, and 86.52%
on Terminal-Bench 2.1. At step 18 it exceeds ordinary RPR-2 on three suites but scores 0.19 points lower on
Terminal-Bench 3.0. Long-Reward also regresses: continuing to step 20 preserves Terminal-Bench 2.1 but
loses 2.66, 2.38, and 3.00 points on Terminal-Bench 3.0, 4.0, and Hard. These tradeoffs favor cross-suite
strict-success monitoring over reward alone.
