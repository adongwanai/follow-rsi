The behavioral analysis contains 15,211 distinct trajectories: 14,143 training and 1,068 evaluation trajectories, with 2,999 observed training tasks in the nominal 3,000-task pool. The principal comparison uses 9,023 baseline and 4,352 later trajectories and excludes earlier continuation segments. Across 2,802 shared tasks, the mean-length difference is − 23.63 -23.63 turns, with a task-bootstrap interval of [ − 25.09 , − 22.18 ] [-25.09,-22.18] . Task resampling does not control policy initialization, execution budgets, or temporal dependence, so this interval describes a distributional difference rather than a treatment effect. Mean generated length decreases from 49,391 to 33,110 tokens, mean interaction length decreases from 69.04 to 44.28 turns, and success rises from 21.35% to 28.68%. In the later population, 3,104 failed trajectories still receive 50.09% of total reward mass. The 748 early-declaration failures are distinct from the 468 failed trajectories that terminate within ten turns. Across recorded steps 0–16, neither mean length nor mean reward exhibits a sustained collapse. The separately reported per-attempt evaluation success reaches 82.02%, compared with approximately 67.00–68.00% under earlier training conditions with different initialization. This comparison is neither a causal estimate of SPR’s effect nor a pass@3 result. The eight later steps were already available when the reward was designed. The offline comparisons therefore assess sensitivity to success separation, saturation, and family calibration rather than blind held-out performance or online policy effects.
