5  Limitations and Conclusion Causal attribution. Compute-matched alternation, isolated TB-GAE, matched online reward, and inherited-versus-reset critic controls remain necessary. Stage-specific learning rates, rewards, and initialization change together; estimator identities and offline rescoring do not isolate their training effects. Evaluation and selection. Three evaluations do not replace independent training seeds or establish statistical superiority. Checkpoint selection requires protected validation, not adaptation to final benchmarks. Verification coverage. Tests certify enforced requirements only: the Rust–C case exposes a native-architecture gap. Reward calibration may retain shortcuts, while replay filtering may discard useful behavior. Scope. SWR100 is training-disjoint; broader repository and pretraining overlap remain incompletely audited. Five stages of one 27B backbone establish neither scaling laws nor universal monotonicity. Terminal-RSI coordinates verified rewriting, critic-inheriting refinement, turn-aware credit, and evolving rewards. Terminal-RSI-27B reaches 50.58% macro-average success, with 19.84% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 , 86.52% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 , 16.95% on Terminal-Bench 3.0 , and 79.00% on Terminal-Bench Hard . LHTB process reward rises from 0.21 to 0.37 with 5/46 strict completions; SWR100 reaches 6.00% , twice Base but unchanged after RPR-1. Controlled studies should establish how these components interact under matched training budgets. Matched task exposure, inference budgets, and checkpoint selection would distinguish the separate contributions of critic transfer and reward evolution.
