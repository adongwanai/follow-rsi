Consistency conditions. Unit clocks with matched values, rewards, and episode endpoints recover standard
GAE at retained generated positions. Adding zero-reward observation nodes leaves TB-GAE unchanged
when retained prefix inputs and value predictions are fixed. Computational partitioning preserves the result
when both successor value and successor trace are transported without changing retained inputs. Context
summarization changes retained conditioning and is therefore outside this exact fixed-input invariance.
Reward sums alone are insufficient: with unit clocks, turn rewards 0.10 and 1.00 imply returns 1.10 and 1.00,
whereas moving their sum to termination incorrectly gives 1.10 to the second turn. Computational boundaries
must preserve episode identity, turn structure, and causal reward placement rather than automatically
terminate the episode. These invariances establish estimator properties rather than evidence of deployment
in a particular evaluated checkpoint.

Prospective checkpoint selection requires predefined validation objectives, patience, tolerances, evaluation
frequency, and retention criteria. The reported sequence records retained checkpoints rather than validating
a prospective selection rule. Repeated selection using final benchmark feedback would confound learning
with test-set adaptation. Conservative policy-improvement arguments likewise distinguish surrogate gain
from distribution-shift error [18]. Five stages of one 27B backbone establish neither a model-size scaling law
nor an equal-compute benefit of recursion.
